## Numbers 11:7
<!-- source-locus: 11.7 -->

**Verse 7.** “The manna was like coriander seed.” But for “like coriander seed,” the other translators have “bdellium”; another says “from honey”; Aquila and Symmachus [have] “oil of the breast,” “from a breast into fat.”

[Source note: This is a damaged list of translation variants. The final two expressions are transmitted as `μαστοῦ ἔλαιον, μαστοῦ εἰς λίπας` and are not syntactically secure; no botanical or doctrinal explanation has been supplied.]

## Numbers 12:1
<!-- source-locus: 12.1 -->

**Verses 1, 2, 10, 15.** After Miriam reviled Moses, she became leprous; being outside the camp, she sat for seven days. This happened for the fear of the people. For if he did not spare his sister, neither would he spare another.

## Numbers 14:7
<!-- source-locus: 14.7 -->

**Source heading: verse 36.** The men who disparaged the land showed God to be a liar—“piously,” according to them—although he praised Palestine.

[Source note: The JSONL locus is labelled `14.7`, but its heading reads `Στίχ. λς΄` (verse 36). The phrase `κατʼ αὐτοὺς εὐσεβῶς` is syntactically and semantically uncertain. The secure point is that slandering the land imputes falsehood to the God who praised it.]

## Numbers 22:2
<!-- source-locus: 22.2 -->

**Verse 2.** “And Balak saw.” “The nations heard and were angered.” Among these is also: “The rulers of Moab—trembling seized them.”

## Numbers 22:4
<!-- source-locus: 22.4 -->

**Verse 4.** “[They did this] because, as [for] a seer, they were bringing the wage of this [unclear word] in order to bring him.”

[Source note: The entire surviving comment is corrupt/elliptical: `Ὅτι ὥσπερ μάντιν τὸν μισθὸν ταύτης ἔφερον προσαγαγεῖν αὐτόν.` The antecedent of `ταύτης` and the construction of the sentence cannot be recovered from this JSONL alone. This translation makes no claim about Balaam’s motive, office, or doctrine beyond the surviving references to a seer, a fee, and bringing him.]

## Numbers 23:7
<!-- source-locus: 23.7 -->

**Verse 7.** “And, taking up the parable.” In certain places the divine Scripture gives precedence and first place, within its subject matter, to the symbolic element that is in the historical narrative.

[Source note: `προτίθηοι` is evidently a defective form, but the contrast between symbolic meaning and historical narration is clear.]

## Numbers 24:7
<!-- source-locus: 24.7 -->

**Verse 7.** “And he shall rule over many nations.” There are many lords and many gods. But blessed are those who have our Lord Jesus Christ as Lord; for they will rule over many “ladies” [or “mistresses”].

[Source note: The last noun is the feminine plural `κυρίας`, not the expected “nations” or a masculine noun. It is retained with a bracketed literal rendering rather than silently normalized.]

## Numbers 24:17
<!-- source-locus: 24.17 -->

**Verse 17.** “And a man shall arise from Israel.” They say that Balaam had pupils in his craft, who had become his disciples in magic; and [they say] that, concerning him, they wrote down and left behind his prophecies, among which is also, “A star shall arise.” Or perhaps the Magi, having received this through ancestral tradition and succession, came to Bethlehem.

[Source note: The phrase after “magic” is elliptical (`καὶ δόξαν περὶ αὐτοῦ`); the translation preserves the report as an uncertain tradition rather than asserting a historical chain of transmission.]
