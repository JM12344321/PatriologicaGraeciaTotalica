## Deuteronomy 1:31

**Verse 31.** “As if a man should carry and nurture.” Aquila translated: “As a nurse might take up a son, he carried him.” Origen says in the homilies on Jeremiah: “He bore with you and came down together with you, making our weakness his own: like a teacher sounding out syllables with children, like a father tending his own children and putting on their ways, little by little leading them toward the more perfect and higher things.”

<!-- source-locus: 3.18 -->
## Deuteronomy 3:18

**Verse 18.** “And I commanded you.” He says these things to the tribe of Reuben, the tribe of Gad, and the half-tribe of Manasseh.

<!-- source-locus: 3.27 -->
## Deuteronomy 3:27–28

**Verses 27–28.** He gives Moses more than Aaron: to behold the land by direct sight. But he does not grant him entrance, imposing this punishment for his deficiency of faith. In accordance with it, he removes both men from life outside the promised land, though they were able to reach it, since attainment of the goal is given through faith. This is indeed also a symbol concerning the law, which is unable to perfect or to bring about the promise, because “the righteous person will live by faith.” Yet this is an honor: to know God’s judgment and to know one’s own death beforehand.

Of the same author. He brings Joshua forward. Joshua had already been sanctified by following and attending upon Moses, but he was about to receive the ruling Spirit through Moses, so as to establish the people as his own charge in Moses’ place. So also Elisha was present in the spirit of Elijah to do Elijah’s work, for the same things must be accomplished in the same Spirit. “Take to yourself,” as he says in Numbers, “Joshua son of Nun, a man who has the Spirit of God in himself, and you shall lay your hands upon him.”

In this way the apostles, although they had the life-giving Spirit from the Lord’s breathing upon them, receive the ruling Spirit sent down from heaven: not one spirit inside another spirit, one different spirit upon another, but an activity in the same Spirit, just as Paul teaches.

[Translation note: The clause describing Joshua’s reception of the “ruling Spirit” and succession to Moses is compressed in the source; “as his own charge in Moses’ place” gives its contextual force.]

<!-- source-locus: 4.12 -->
## Deuteronomy 4:12

**Verse 12.** “And the Lord spoke to you from the midst of the fire, a voice of words which you heard.”

Of the same author, on the same passage. Another says: It is clear that someone both sees and hears concepts with the eyes of the mind.

“A voice of words.” Here he has spoken of what occurs according to nature, because hearing receives a voice. For so far as the exact letter is concerned, only the Decalogue was written on the tablets; Moses taught the rest by God’s command.

## Deuteronomy 14:19

**Verse 19.** “Every creeping thing among the flying creatures shall be unclean.” Among flying creatures, I think, he intends those that have projections below the knees, above the feet, to be clean, but those that do not to be unclean.

The law therefore rejects the eagle, the griffin-vulture, the sea eagle, and also the vulture. Creatures of this kind fly high, delight greatly in the highest places, and hate the things below; for, it says, “The young of the vulture fly in the heights.” Of such a kind, in some manner, is the person who raises his brows excessively, has a mind lifted up within himself, is accustomed to despise an equitable and moderate disposition, and has very little love for being led down together with the lowly.

Together with these it condemns the kite and creatures like it. Through them is very clearly signified the rapacious sort and the human race that is formidable in wrongdoing. The kite is a pattern of these; the source’s next short contrast about “the other” and “the blackest and deep” is textually obscure. Dark in a certain way and not manifest to everyone are those who run toward wickedness and are harmful to the many, while providing misery for themselves.

The wish to take more is somehow always joined to those sick with accursed arrogance. No ordinary gain satisfies them; by devices of many-shaped wrongdoing they seek advantage and seize greedily. Therefore he immediately joins to this the words, “and the sparrow, and the gull.” These, I think, are greedy creatures, always gathering whatever falls in their way.

Then he names the hawk and creatures like it, the night raven, the diver, the ibis, the *prosphorion*, the pelican, and the swan. Through these in turn he would describe the cruel, those who savagely plot against others and rush without restraint upon whomever they happen to be able to attack.

[Translation note: This record contains several damaged constructions and uncertain ancient bird names. The clause after “kite” and part of the sentence about arrogant gain-seekers cannot be recovered with confidence, and the source ends the final sentence without a full stop.]

## Deuteronomy 20:5

A mind that is broken and has given itself to the desires of the flesh would be signified by betrothal to a woman. For when the voice of the army herald approaches, such people withdraw from the sacred and most warlike multitude. What it was likely for people caught in these things to think and say, the herald anticipates and cries aloud.

Or is it not true that they think such things? Those who love ease are accustomed in persecutions to love what is free from toil and to follow the exertions made for love of God, considering that, if they should suffer anything while contending, they will lose houses, possessions, and the desires held in honor among them.

[Translation note: The selected source begins this record in the middle of a sentence. The transmitted Greek has κατακολουθεῖν (“to follow”) with no negative; the context may suggest that a negative has fallen out, but “to lag behind” cannot be translated from the text as it stands.]

## Deuteronomy 20:8

**Verse 8.** “Who is the man who is afraid and cowardly in heart?” Of the same author. The person absorbed in earthly things, building houses and planting vineyards, defeated by the pleasures of love for the flesh, will surely be cowardly and without courage.

<!-- source-locus: 20.10 -->
## Deuteronomy 20:10

**Verse 10.** “If you approach a city to make war against it.” This is like: “Into whatever city you enter, examine who in it is worthy.” And the phrase “with peace” is like the apostolic saying that “a servant of the Lord must not fight.”

<!-- source-locus: 20.13 -->
## Deuteronomy 20:13

**Verse 13.** “And you shall strike every male in it with slaughter by the sword.” Of the same author. The sword of the Spirit would be the word of God. And “the sword of the Spirit, which is the utterance of God,” is that by which the divinely eloquent disciples killed every male thing of the opposing power—such as Simon and Elymas the magicians—and destroyed their strong doctrines.

But they preserved the women, that is, souls able to say, “Because of fear of you, Lord, we conceived in the womb and brought forth upon the earth”; and likewise the infants, to whom Paul also writes, “Little children, do not become infants in your minds, but be infants in evil.” “And preserve the livestock,” he says, for “a righteous person has compassion on the lives of his livestock,” intimating by the livestock those who are less rational. Cornelius too was livestock, according to the vision concerning four-footed animals that appeared to Peter.

## Deuteronomy 22:6

**Verse 6.** “You shall not take the mother together with the children,” because they cannot nourish themselves when the mother has been taken.

<!-- source-locus: 23.3 -->
## Deuteronomy 23:3

**Verse 3.** “An Ammonite shall not enter.” Why does he forbid intermingling with Moabites and Ammonites, not for a stated period but forever? First, because of their lawless root, for they were born from unlawful intercourse; then, because of the impiety in which they caused the Israelites to share by adorning their women and, through them, hunting the Israelites into the worship of idols.

Yet he commands that the Edomite and the Egyptian be admitted after the third generation: the former as a brother, the latter as one who had been a benefactor in a time of need. For when famine fell, their fathers entered Egypt and were sustained. The law therefore teaches us not to forget even a benefit received long ago.

<!-- source-locus: 23.14a -->
## Deuteronomy 23:14 (First Note)

**Verse 14.** What does it mean that “the Lord your God walks about in your camp”? Air filled with foul odors often causes a plague. He therefore commanded them, while encamped, to bury the excrement that was discharged. So that they might not disobey and receive harm, he necessarily added, “Because the Lord God walks about in your camp.” And he teaches the reason: “to deliver you and to hand your enemy over before you.” Through this he trains them to do nothing lawless, for he adds this: “And your camp shall be holy, and no shameful thing shall be found among you.”

## Deuteronomy 23:14 (Second Note)

**Verse 14.** “Because the Lord your God walks about in the camp to deliver you and to hand your enemy into your hands. And your camp shall be holy, and no shameful matter shall be seen among you.”

For it is fitting that those who desire to prevail over the intelligible enemies refuse every form of uncleanness. Yet, because we all stumble in many ways and human nature suffers from being prone to slip into wrongdoing, “no one will understand transgressions,” according to the voice of the Psalmist. The text at this point is damaged, but its probable force is: suppose someone is almost broken by one of the impurities innate and present within us and is caught suffering from something outside the law and a life befitting holiness.

For the law of sin dwells in the members of the flesh. First, the faults arising from weakness are not to be committed openly and without concealment, or in the sight of many, but as though secretly outside and as far as possible from the camp. Then, he says, let them be covered by corrective actions, for, as it is written, “the wise will cover their own shameful deeds.”

By ceasing from wrongdoings and, through inclinations toward better things, in a certain way remaking the mind so that it chooses what is beneficial, we remove the stumblings from our midst. Having overcome unseemly sin by the fragrances of the things that follow, we shall enter again into the camp of the saints, that is, into the church of the firstborn.

This is fulfilled in Christ if we take up his cross while employing a swift and fully girded disposition. For our having a peg—that is, the wood—upon the belt hints at this indirectly. We therefore make bodily things a spiritual representation of intelligible things. The next clause about physical uncleanness, going to the privies, and the eyes of God who made us contains a defective verb. We must therefore bury uncleannesses, “because the Lord walks about in the camp.” For Christ dwells in us and walks among us.

## Deuteronomy 24:6

**Verse 6.** “You shall not take a mill or an upper millstone as a pledge, because in this way one takes a soul as a pledge.” Another interpretation of the same passage: Neither a mill nor an upper millstone is a soul, but it contributes to the preparation of bread, through which human beings especially live. The same should be noted concerning blood, for about it too it was said, “The blood of the body is the soul.” These things must therefore be understood by an extended use of language, for neither the blood nor the mill is a soul, but they contribute to living.

## Deuteronomy 27:15

**Verse 15.** “And he shall set it in a hidden place.” Because it was likely that some, through fear of punishment, would be eager to escape notice and practice impiety secretly and by stealth, he subjects those who commit sin without witnesses to a curse, for the greater fear and warning of those doing these things. Such are those who pretend to a counterfeit piety while burying the passions within and, in the movement of the mind, raging toward every kind of absurdity. These are the people who set idols in a hidden place.

<!-- source-locus: 27.17 -->
## Deuteronomy 27:17

**Verse 17.** “Accursed is the one who moves a neighbor’s boundaries.” A person also moves a neighbor’s boundaries when he shakes someone found within them away from true knowledge of God and a God-inspired way of life. What follows makes this clear.

<!-- source-locus: 27.18 -->
## Deuteronomy 27:18

**Verse 18.** “Accursed is the one who misleads a blind person on the road.” Of the same author. Such are also those who draw people from idolatry and those from the circumcision into abominable heresies. For while they are blind and do not yet have the light of truth and of knowledge of God, they deceive and mislead them by displaying a different road in place of the apostolic one.

<!-- source-locus: 27.19 -->
## Deuteronomy 27:19

**Verse 19.** “Accursed is whoever turns aside the judgment of a proselyte, orphan, and widow.” He places under the same curse those who similarly turn aside the judgment of a proselyte, orphan, and widow, saying the same thing again in another manner. For although the literal sense has what is beneficial, contemplation also has the higher meaning.

The proselytes are those from the nations who enter the holy church of God. The orphans, properly speaking, are those who have renounced their own father, Satan, but have not yet put on Jesus and are still numbered among the catechumens. Likewise these are also widows: they have turned away from the one who sows in them the seeds of destruction but have not yet been joined to Christ as a pure virgin, according to the divine Apostle.

Those, therefore, who turn aside the judgment of these people and do not hand over to them the mystery of piety unadulterated are under a curse. And anyone who laboriously goes through the other sayings will be able to contemplate them spiritually while the interpretation is preserved.

<!-- source-locus: 27.26 -->
## Deuteronomy 27:26

**Verse 26.** “Accursed is every person who does not remain in all the words of the law.” The phrase “in all,” which stands in the Septuagint, is confirmed by the Samaritan copy, in which we found *chol*, which means “all” or “all things.” The Apostle also agrees with these words when he says, “For as many as are from works of the law are under a curse.” Hence those from the circumcision are shown to be under a curse by not doing all things.

<!-- source-locus: 33.6 -->
## Deuteronomy 33:6

**Verse 6.** “Let Reuben live, and let him not die.” Because Jacob, as he was dying, cursed Reuben, saying, “Do not boil over like water,” Moses says in blessing, “Let Reuben live, and let him not die.” Both are well said. For the father cursed him because he had intercourse with the concubine, thereby training and frightening those who came afterward; and Moses justly blesses him later, because he alone spared Joseph when his brothers wished to kill him. It would have been absurd for a curse to take effect against the man when he sinned but for a blessing not to be given when he acted rightly.
