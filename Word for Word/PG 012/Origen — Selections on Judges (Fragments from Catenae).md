## Fragment 1

And Jephthah made a vow to the Lord. If the Spirit of the Lord [was] upon him, how does he vow things that are not fitting? For it was possible even for a dog to meet Jephthah as he returned. Was the Spirit, then, mistaken? By no means. Rather, the Spirit came to help, not to suggest the improper vow; but he himself errs by making a promise without consideration.

<!-- source-locus: 2 -->
## Fragment 2

And they said to him: “Say now, *stachys*.” But they said *stachys*; it signifies *sebēla*, which is *astachys*. But they said *sebēlō* and were not able to say *sēdēla* plainly. For although they happened to be Hebrews, they differed in proportion through brevity in the pronunciation of their dialect. Therefore the password was *sebēla*; when those questioned said *sedēlō*, they were convicted of not being among Jephthae’s own people, but among those who had risen up [against him].

## Fragment 3

And his wife [was] barren and did not bear. He calls [her] barren for the present, not from nature but from the ministry.

## Fragment 4

Iron did not come up upon my head, because I am a Nazirite of God. Through the whole history concerning Samson, the hairs appear to preserve for him all his strength.

<!-- source-locus: 5 -->
## Fragment 5

Of the same [author]. He calls a Nazirite “the one kept,” according to the language of the Syrians. God commands Samson not to follow the custom of the Israelites, who at that time shaved the head in order to be conspicuous to all.

<!-- source-locus: 6 -->
## Fragment 6

And he made an ephod and teraphim. An ephod was a priestly garment; when the priest wore it, the grace of the Holy Spirit came upon [him]. Teraphim, however, was such a thing, yet not divine but idolatrous, as is shown in the Kingdoms.

<!-- source-locus: 7 -->
## Fragment 7

And the sons of Benjamin came out. For what reason are those who came against them for a just cause defeated? Clearly, because of a preceding cause. And the Psalm says: “When I receive the appointed time, I shall judge uprightly.” Then, after they had been defeated once and a second time, wrath passed over to those who had done wrong, and the Israelites win a just victory.
