## CHAPTER XI

[Note:  “I am found a denier.” A denier is one who, after finding the well-worn road of truth and accepting Christ’s faith with Catholics, abandons, innovates upon, or changes it before men. Christ will abandon and deny him before the angels. I will say it once: no one seeks unless he either never possessed or has lost. The old woman had lost one of ten drachmas (Luke 15:8), and therefore sought; when she found it, she ceased seeking. The neighbor had no bread (Luke 11:5), and therefore knocked; when the door was opened and he received it, he ceased knocking. Let him who always asks see to it, because he will never be heard [Col. 0025C] [Note:  “is heard,” Semler.]: he asks from one who does not hear.

[Col. 0026A] What slave expects rations from a stranger—to say nothing of his master’s enemy?  What soldier seeks a gift and pay from kings who are not allies—to say nothing of enemies—unless he is plainly a deserter, fugitive, and rebel? The old woman too sought the drachma within [Col. 0025C] her own roof; the man who knocked beat upon his neighbor’s [Note:  Variant corrupt readings.] door; the widow addressed a judge who, though harsh, was not an [Col. 0025C] enemy. No one can be instructed by that from which he is destroyed; no one is enlightened by him through whom he is darkened. Let us therefore seek among our own people, from our [Col. 0025C] own people, and concerning our own matters, and only what can come into question while the rule of faith remains safe.

## CHAPTER XIV

This rule, established by Christ as will be proved, raises no questions among us except those introduced by heresies and those that make heretics. So long as its form remains in its proper order, inquire and discuss as much as you like and pour out every desire of curiosity. If something seems to you suspended in ambiguity or veiled in obscurity, there is surely some brother  who is a teacher endowed with the [Col. 0027C] grace of knowledge, someone conversant among the experienced, someone seeking with you [Col. 0027B] but more inquisitively [Note:  Several corrupt variants: “someone,” “something”; Venetian, Rigaltius, Paris, and Pamelius [Col. 0027C] repeat “with you.”]. Faith is placed in the rule: you possess the law and salvation from observing the law. Training, however, consists in curiosity and has only the glory arising from zeal for expertise. Let curiosity yield to faith and glory to salvation. At least let them cease clamoring and be still. To know nothing contrary to the rule is to know everything.
