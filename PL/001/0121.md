That Tertullian omitted it, however, seems sufficiently suggested by his explanation; for by this indefinite name he shows not only that we are designated as sons of God, but also affirms that the divine Son is invoked there, since his name is tacitly signified when the general designation “Father” is expressed. Moreover, Tertullian says that, once mention has been made of Father and sons, the name of the Church must also be understood; for there is no father without sons, nor sons without father and mother. Although this mode of argument is fallacious when applied to divine things, it nevertheless seems to have pleased Tertullian with reference to us, the sons of God: whenever we are called sons, we are to understand, besides God our Father, also the Church our mother, who through Christ begot us for God. Thus by the one designation “Father” we honor God together with his own—that is, God the Father, his eternal Son, and Mother Church. We also remember the commandment, since Christ, as Matthew attests, declared that we should call no one on earth father except him whom we have in heaven.

We cannot readily embrace what the most learned Albaspinaeus has advanced concerning the name “Church” used here. Does one human being offend another? Whatever passes between them is assuredly human. How small is what is owed; how small what is forgiven! But does a human being offend God?
