## CHAPTER VII

Let the scorpion still strike, brandishing the charge that God is a murderer. I shall certainly shudder at the filthy posture of blasphemy reeking from a heretic’s mouth; yet I shall also embrace such a God with the confidence of reason—the same reason by which he himself pronounced himself more than a murderer, speaking in the person of his own Sophia, through the voice of Solomon: “Sophia,” he says (Prov. 9:2), “has slaughtered her sons.” *Sophia* is wisdom. And therefore, when setting out the first principles of his commandments, he says: “Blessed are those [Col. 0138C] who suffer persecution for righteousness, because theirs is the kingdom of heaven” (Matt. 5:10). This indeed is addressed without qualification to everyone; then, specifically to the apostles themselves: “You will be blessed when they dishonor and persecute you and say every evil thing against you because of me. Thus [Col. 0139A] he also prophesied that they themselves would have to be killed after the example of the prophets.

And even if he had directed this whole conditional persecution only toward the apostles of that time, yet through them, together with the entire sacrament, the propagation of the name, and the transmission of the Holy Spirit, the discipline of undergoing persecution would also have extended to us, as hereditary disciples and shoots of apostolic seed.

For again he directs his words to the apostles (Matt. 10:16): “Behold, I send you like sheep into the midst of wolves”; and (v. 17): “Beware of men, for they will hand you over to councils, and in their synagogues they will scourge you, and you will be brought before governors and kings for my sake, as a testimony to them and to the nations,” etc. But when he adds (v. 21), “Brother will hand brother over to death, and the father his son, and children will rise up against [Col. 0139B] their parents and put them to death,” he plainly pronounced this iniquity against others as well, since we do not find it among the apostles. For none of them suffered either a brother or a father as betrayer, whereas very many of our people already have.

He then turns back to the apostles (v. 22): “And you will be hated by everyone because of my name.” How much more shall we, who must also be betrayed by our parents? Thus, by means of this very intermingling, arranging his discourse now toward the apostles, now toward everyone, he poured out upon all the same consequence of bearing the name, wherever the name has settled together with the law of the hatred directed against it.

“But the one who endures to the end will be saved” (ibid.). By enduring what, except persecution, betrayal, and killing? For to endure to the end is nothing other than to suffer one’s end. And therefore: “The disciple is not above the master” (ibid., v. 25). Immediately there follows: “Nor is the servant [Col. 0139C] above his lord”; because, since the Master and Lord himself endured persecution, betrayal, and killing, much more will servants and disciples have to pay the same, lest, as though superior, they appear exempted from iniquity—when this very thing ought to suffice them for glory: to be made equal to the Lord and Master in his sufferings.

Building them up for endurance of these things, he warns (ibid., v. 28) that they must not fear those who kill only the body but are unable to kill the soul; rather, fear must be consecrated to him who can kill and destroy both body and soul in Gehenna. Who are these killers of the body alone, except the governors and kings mentioned above? Human beings, I think. And who is master of the soul as well, except God alone? Who is this threatener with fires, except he without whose will not even one of the sparrows [Col. 0139D] falls to the ground—that is, neither one of the two substances of the human being, flesh or soul? For even the number of the hairs is entered in his register (ibid., v. 31).

“Therefore do not fear,” [Col. 0140A] he says above, “for you surpass many sparrows.” He promises that we will fall to the ground not in vain—that is, not without reward—if we choose to be killed by human beings rather than by God.

“Therefore everyone who confesses in me before human beings, I too will confess in him before my Father who is in heaven. And everyone who denies me before human beings, I too will deny him before my Father who is in heaven” (ibid., v. 33). This is, I think, a manifest definition and rationale [Note: [Col. 0139D] *Both a definition and a rationale*, Seml.] both of confession and of denial, even though the formulation differs.

The person who confesses himself a Christian testifies that he belongs to Christ. One who belongs to Christ must necessarily be in Christ. If he is in Christ, he assuredly confesses in Christ when he confesses himself a Christian; for he cannot be this unless he is in Christ. Moreover, by confessing in Christ, one who is in Christ also confesses Christ, since Christ too is in him, inasmuch as he is a Christian. For even if [Note: [Col. 0139D] *As if*, Wouwer manuscript.] you say “day,” you show [Note: [Col. 0139D] *You have shown*, Seml.] the reality of the light that produces the day, though you have not said “light.”

Thus, although he did not pronounce directly, “The one who confesses me,” the act of everyday confession does not differ from the meaning of the Lord’s pronouncement. For the one who confesses what he is—that is, a Christian—also confesses that through which he is what he is—that is, Christ.

Likewise, the one who has denied himself to be a Christian has denied in Christ, by denying that he is in Christ when he denies that he is a Christian; and, by denying Christ in himself when he denies himself to be in Christ, he will also deny Christ himself. Thus both the one who denies in Christ will deny Christ, and the one who confesses in Christ will confess Christ.

It would therefore have sufficed even if the Lord had pronounced only concerning confession. For from the form of confession a preliminary judgment would also be rendered concerning its contrary, namely denial: that the Lord repays denial [Note: [Col. 0139D] *By denial*, Seml.] with denial, just as he repays confession [Note: [Col. 0140D] *By confession*, Seml.] with confession.

And therefore, since in the form of confession the condition of denial is also understood, it is evident that what the Lord pronounced differently concerning denial than concerning confession belongs to another mode of denial, since he said, “The one who denies me,” not “The one who denies in me.” For he had foreseen that this coercion too would very often follow in the assault upon the name: [Note: [Col. 0139D] namely, that the one who had denied himself to be a Christian would be compelled to deny Christ himself as well by blaspheming him. Something of this kind is related by Pliny: those named by an informer as people who called themselves Christians, after being arrested, denied it, and at last even cursed Christ. ## CHAPTER X

What gatekeeping powers do you affirm for me, in accordance with Roman superstition: [Note: [Col. 0142D] “Some Janus, Forculus, and Limentinus.” The copy reads “some Barnus.” RIG.] some Janus [Note: [Col. 0142C] *Some Cardus*, Seml.; *some Cardea*, Fran.], Forculus, and Limentinus? What powers do you station at the barriers?

If you have ever read in David (Ps. 24:7), “Lift up your gates, O princes [Note: [Col. 0142C] *Your princes*, Rig. Venet.], and let the eternal gates be raised, and the King of glory will enter”; if likewise you have heard in Amos (9:6), “He who builds his ascent into the heavens and establishes his outpouring upon the earth”—then know that that ascent has henceforth been leveled by the footsteps of the Lord and the entrance henceforth opened by the powers of Christ. No delay or interrogation will confront Christians at the threshold, [Col. 0142C] since they are not to be investigated there, but recognized; not questioned, but admitted.

For even if you still think heaven closed, remember that the Lord left its keys here to Peter, and through him to the Church—keys that each person here, after being questioned and making confession, will carry with him.

But the devil insists that confession must be made there, so that he may persuade us to deny here. Fine credentials, clearly, I shall send on ahead [Note: [Col. 0142D] “Fine credentials.” As though “documents supporting our case.” Thus Book XXIX, ch. *On Gifts*: “adequate credentials.” RIG.]; fine keys I shall carry with me [Note: [Col. 0142D] “Fine keys I shall carry with me”: constancy in the Christian faith. What follows concerning fear is added sarcastically. RIG.] I who could not stand in earthly realms; I shall withstand the greater powers, I who yielded to the lesser; finally, I shall deserve to be admitted, though already excluded.

There is still this to say: if confession is to be made in the heavenly realms, then denial must be made here [Note: [Col. 0143C] *Or*, Seml.]; for [Note: [Col. 0143C] *Assuredly*, Seml.] where the one occurs, there [Note: [Col. 0143C] *In the same place*, Wouwer manuscript, Jun.] both occur, since all opposites come into conflict. Persecution too will have to be carried on in heaven, since it supplies the occasion for confession or denial.

Why then do you delay, most audacious heretic, in transferring the entire order of the Christian ordeal to the realms above, and first of all in locating there the very hatred of the name, where Christ sits at the Father’s right hand? There you will also establish [Note: [Col. 0143C] *You should establish*, Seml.] the synagogues of the Jews, the fountains of persecutions, [Col. 0143B] in which the apostles suffered scourging; and the peoples of the nations, together, indeed, with their circus, where they readily cry out, [Note: [Col. 0143D] “How long must we endure the third race?” Ursinus’s manuscript has “the foul race”; but the Agobardine copy has “the third race.” Tertullian himself, in Book I *To the Nations*, shows that this is perfectly correct: “Plainly,” he says, “we are called the third race. There will assuredly also be a prison in heaven, deprived of sunlight—or perhaps, contrary to its occupants’ wishes, full of light—and perhaps chains made from the celestial belts, and the very axle that revolves will be the rack. Then, if the Christian is to be stoned, hailstones will be at hand; if burned, thunderbolts are readily available; if slaughtered, the hand [Note: [Col. 0143D] *Office*, Seml.
