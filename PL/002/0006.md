## CHAPTER XLVII

To these he adds the Ophites, Cainites, and Sethites.

To these were also added the heretics called Ophites. For they magnify the serpent to such a degree that they even prefer it to Christ himself. It was the serpent, they say, who gave us the origin of the knowledge of good and evil. Perceiving its power and majesty, they say, Moses set up the bronze serpent, and all who [Col. 0064A] looked upon it obtained health. Christ himself, they further claim, imitates in his Gospel the sacred power of that serpent when he says [Note: [Col. 0064C] “Teaches,” Semler.], “And just as Moses lifted up the serpent in the wilderness, so must the Son of Man be lifted up.” They bring in the serpent itself to bless their Eucharist. [Note: [Col. 0064C] “They bring it in to bless their Eucharist.” So too Jerome’s *Catalogue*: “And it sanctifies the Eucharist for them.” RIG.] [Note: [Col. 0064C] “Eucharistic things,” Franeker.]

But the whole setting and doctrine of this error flowed from the following source. They say that from that supreme, primordial aeon several other, lower aeons came into existence; yet an aeon whose name is Jaldabaoth presides over them all. [Note: [Col. 0064C] “Whose name is Jaldabaoth.” This name was drawn from the storehouse of the Nicolaitans, concerning whom Saint Epiphanius, *Heresy* 25, writes: Ἕτεροι δὲ τῶν προειρημένων Ἰαλδαβαὼθ δοξάζουσι, φάσκοντες αὐτὸν εἶναι πρῶτον υἱὸν τῆς Βαρβηλὼ καὶ διὰ τοῦτό φασι δεῖν ἄγειν αὐτῷ τὴν τιμήν, ὅτι πολλὰ ἀπεκάλυψεν· ὅθεν καὶ βιβλία τινὰ ἐξ ὀνόματος τοῦ Ἰαλδαβαὼθ ποιητεύονται ἐκτυποῦντες, “Others among the aforesaid chiefly venerate Jaldabaoth, asserting that he is the firstborn son of Barbelo and that honor ought therefore to be paid to him, as they say, because he revealed many things. This was the power [Col. 0065A] in paradise—that is, this was the serpent in which Eve had believed as though it were the Son of God. She plucked, they say, from the fruit of the tree, and thereby imparted to the human race knowledge of good and evil. They also hold that Christ did not exist in the substance of flesh, and that no salvation of the flesh whatever is to be hoped for.

Cain, they say, was produced by one power, namely the devil; but the most blessed Abel was born from another, namely God. The greater power, which was in Cain himself, so prevailed that he killed his brother. They similarly adore Judas the traitor, considering him to possess something divine—to such a degree, indeed, that they regard his detestable crime as a great benefaction. So true is it that, however wicked or criminal some have at one time been, they have never lacked persons to praise them and commend their crimes. [Note: [Col. 0066C] “For they too magnify Cain.” Jerome’s *Catalogue*: “They sanctify him.” RIG.] For they too magnify Cain, as though he had been conceived from some powerful force that operated within him. Those who assert this also defend Judas the traitor, commemorating him as admirable and great [Note: [Col. 0066C] “Commemorating him as admirable and great.” Jerome’s *Catalogue*: “He was more learned than all the apostles.” RIG.] because of the benefits he is boasted to have conferred upon the human race. Some of them think that thanksgiving should be rendered to Judas for this reason. For Judas, they say, perceiving that Christ wished to overthrow the truth, betrayed him so that the truth could not be overthrown. Others argue contrariwise in the following manner. Because the powers of this world did not wish Christ to suffer, lest salvation should be procured for the human race through his death, Judas, taking counsel for humanity’s salvation, betrayed Christ, so that the salvation which was being obstructed by the powers opposing Christ’s suffering could in no way be obstructed; and so that, through Christ’s passion, the salvation of the human race could not be delayed.

[Note: [Col. 0065C] Junius prefers “Setthites.”] The doctrine of this perversity is as follows. Two human beings, Cain and Abel, were fashioned by the angels. On their account great disputes and dissensions arose among the angels. For this reason, when Abel had been killed, that Power which was above all powers [Note: [Col. 0066D] “The Power which was above all powers.” In Jerome’s *Catalogue* it is called the “Threefold Power.” RIG.] [Col. 0066A]—whom they declare to be the Mother—wished Seth to be conceived and born in Abel’s place, so that those angels who had created the first two human beings might be emptied of their power as this seed died and was born in the world [materially uncertain syntax].

For they say that the minglings of angels and human beings were unlawful. For that reason the Power whom, as we have said, they declare to be the Mother also brought on the Flood in vengeance, so that the seed of that mingling might be removed and this seed alone, being pure, might be preserved intact. Yet, secretly and covertly, and without the knowledge of that Mother-Power, those who had fashioned the men of that former seed also sent the seed of Ham [Note: [Col. 0065C] “Cain,” Franeker and Paris.] into the ark with those eight souls, so that the seed of wickedness might not perish but, preserved with the others and restored to the earth after the Flood, might grow after their example, spread out, and fill and occupy the whole world. Concerning Christ, however, they hold that he was Seth alone, and that he himself existed on behalf of Seth. [Note: [Col. 0066C] Rhenanus reads: “Not on our behalf alone, but also on his own behalf he himself existed.” Semler: “But also on his own behalf he himself existed.”]

## CHAPTER XLVIII

In addition, Carpocrates, Cerinthus, and Ebion.

[Note: [Col. 0066D] “Carpocrates in addition,” etc. It is evident that Carpocrates was the father of the Gnostics. His errors, however, were extinguished in their cradle. Among his other fabrications, he rejected the Old Testament. I do not determine whether the same man was the founder of both the Gnostics and the Carpocratians. It is certain that a certain worthless woman named Marcellina was the associate or inventor of so many crimes; she worshipped images of Christ, Paul, Homer, and Pythagoras indiscriminately. The emperor Alexander Severus is likewise said absurdly to have bestowed this same worship upon profane divinities together with sacred figures, keeping statues of Abraham and Orpheus, Christ and Apollo, in his shrine. LE PR.] Carpocrates, in addition, introduced the following sect. He says that there is one principal Power in the higher regions, and that from it angels and powers were brought forth. These, being far removed from the higher powers [Note: [Col. 0066C] “Power,” Semler.], fashioned this world in the lower regions. Christ was not born of the Virgin Mary but from Joseph’s seed, and was begotten merely as a human being; nevertheless, he was better than others in his cultivation of righteousness [Note: [Col. 0066D] “Certainly above others in his cultivation of righteousness.” Jerome’s *Catalogue*: “But he possessed the prophetic spirit.” RIG.], [Col. 0067A] and in the integrity of his life. [Note: [Col. 0067B] Semler deletes “of his life.”] He suffered among the Jews. His soul alone was received into heaven, because it was firmer and stronger than the others. From this he would infer—since only the salvation of souls is maintained—that there are no resurrections of the body. [Note: [Col. 0067B] Junius reads “to infer” and “maintained.”]

After him the heretic Cerinthus burst forth, teaching similar doctrines. For he too says that the world was established by those beings; he declares that Christ was born from Joseph’s seed and contends that he was merely a human being without divinity. He also reports that the Law was given by angels and puts forward the God of the Jews not as the Lord but as an angel.

[Note: [Col. 0067B] “His successor was Ebion.” This was the impious man who said that the Blessed Virgin gave birth to a man, not God, and who accepted only the Hebrew Gospel. Against him Saint John, both apostle and evangelist, wrote his Gospel last of all. He did not admit the epistles of Blessed Paul, for he called the Apostle an apostate. Since, moreover, the Ebionites at first thought and spoke basely of Christ—or perhaps because by their heresy they betrayed the poverty of their intelligence—their founder was by some chance called “beggar”; for in Hebrew *Ebion* means “beggar.” LE PR.] His successor was Ebion, who did not agree with Cerinthus in every respect, because he says that the world was made by God, not by angels. And because it is written, “No disciple is above his teacher, [Col. 0067B] nor is a servant above his lord,” he also puts forward the Law, evidently in order to exclude the Gospel and vindicate Judaism. [Note: [Col. 0067C] “And because it is written, ‘No disciple is above his teacher.’” Jerome’s *Catalogue*: “Carpocrates, Cerinthus, and Ebion—these three succeeded one another, teaching that all things should be done according to the Law: circumcision, observance of Sabbaths and Jewish feast days; for Christ observed all these things. They said that it was enough for the disciple to be as the teacher.” Hence it is clear that the words should be punctuated thus: “And because it is written, ‘No disciple is above his teacher, [Col. 0067D] nor is a servant above his lord,’ he also puts forward the Law,” etc. RIG.] [Note: [Col. 0067B] “To exist,” Semler.]
