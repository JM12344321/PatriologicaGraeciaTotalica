The Son of God, however, has no mother from unchastity [Note: [Col. 0397C] **The Son of God, however, has no mother from unchastity.** The reading which Latinius and Pamelius testify is written in certain copies—“He has no mother from chastity”—is correct and should not have been altered. The Son of God, our Lord, was born from no marriage, not even a chaste or legitimate one. Therefore he has no mother “from chastity.” That is, the Virgin Mary became his mother not through the bond of any marriage, however chaste, but through the Word of God alone. Yet our Lord was commonly regarded as the son of Joseph and Mary. Hence Tertullian says: “Even the woman whom he appears to have as mother—that mother had not married.” RIGALTIUS.

**From chastity.** Rigaltius rightly supports this reading in his notes, and, in addition to the reasons he gives, it is confirmed by Rhenanus’ edition. He therefore means that our Jesus was born not only without impure and unlawful intercourse, but not even through pure and lawful marriage, by the power of his Spirit and the capacity of the divine nature, which built itself a dwelling among human beings and stirred the leaven of human generation in a virgin womb.

Those who did not understand this substituted “from unchastity.” Yet the Jews dared to assail his chastity, as is evident from the final chapter of *On the Shows*: “This is that son—I shall say it—of the carpenter or of the prostitute.” Thus Rigaltius emended it, though Prior dares to criticize him, while La Cerda chooses the common reading, “of the male prostitute.” What else is that but to eat acorns after grain has been discovered? For Rigaltius did not find this only in the Agobardinus copy; Josias Mercerus also noted it in his own book, which he compared most diligently with that codex. That book is today preserved in the Leiden Library.
