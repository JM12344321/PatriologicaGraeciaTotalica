## Leviticus 4:27
<!-- source-locus: 4.27 -->

**Verse 27.** “If a person sins unintentionally,” Scripture says, “and fails to do one of the Lord’s commands that must be done.” The next short clause is defective in the source. The note suggests that some divine commands are commands not to be carried out, citing, “I gave them statutes that were not good.” Thus someone sins unintentionally when an appeal to piety leads him to keep what ought not to be kept. Such a person needs a sacrifice for forgiveness. The text then contrasts deliberate action and cites Paul’s becoming a Jew to win Jews and circumcising Timothy; because the sentence is compressed, the precise force of that contrast is uncertain.

## Leviticus 4:28
<!-- source-locus: 4.28 -->

**Verse 28.** The word “sin” can also name the offering brought for sin. That is how the saying “They will eat the sins of my people” is to be understood. Repentance may be described as offering up one’s sin, but it must not be followed by falling again into the same acts, since sin then comes alive again. In that same sacrificial sense, the note says Christ can be called “sin,” because he was sacrificed for everyone’s sin.

## Leviticus 12:2
<!-- source-locus: 12.2 -->

**Verse 2.** “If a woman conceives and bears a male child.” The phrase “if she conceives” may look unnecessary. The note proposes a prophetic reason: it distinguishes Mary, who bore the Savior without conception by seed, so that she is not regarded as unclean for bearing him. Even without that phrase, Mary is not to be counted unclean, because she is not merely a woman but a virgin.

[Source note: A few words after “bearing the Savior” are defective; this rendering keeps only the argument that survives clearly.]

## Leviticus 13:45
<!-- source-locus: 13.45 -->

**Verse 45.** The leper’s uncovered head and loosened clothes make the condition visible, so that people who approach do not share the uncleanness. The note applies the same principle to apostolic warnings: keep apart from a disorderly brother, and do not even eat with one called a brother who persists in sexual immorality, greed, idolatry, drunkenness, abusive speech, or robbery.

## Leviticus 23:2
<!-- source-locus: 23.2 -->

**Verse 2.** A feast is a joyful day set apart for special holy practices. A public assembly is more specific: it is a feast at which people gather from everywhere. Every public assembly is therefore a feast, but not every feast is a public assembly. Sabbaths and New Moons are feasts; Unleavened Bread, the Feast of Weeks, and Tabernacles are both feasts and public assemblies.

[Source note: The source’s opening definition is elliptical, but this distinction is explicit.]

## Leviticus 23:40
<!-- source-locus: 23.40 -->

**Verse 40.** Palm branches point to God’s renewed gift, in Christ, of paradise’s delights. Evergreen branches signify grace that endures and hope that does not fade.

## Leviticus 27:15
<!-- source-locus: 27.15 -->

**Verse 15.** If someone redeems a consecrated house, the valuation must be increased by a fifth. The surviving commentary then moves through redemption, possession, sale, and Jubilee return for a house or field consecrated to God. Its wording is badly broken: it says the owner may retain the possession after valuation and redemption, speaks of a buyer holding it until Jubilee, and says that afterward it belongs to God. It does not securely preserve enough syntax to reconstruct the legal sequence more fully.

## Leviticus 27:16
<!-- source-locus: 27.16 -->

**Verse 16.** A purchased field is not permanently recovered by its purchaser. Once its yield has been accounted for before God, the field is restored to the seller at Jubilee.
