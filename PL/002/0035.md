For the present it is enough to have followed thus far the sequence of these matters concerning Christ, so that, once he has been shown to be such as he was proclaimed, [Col. 0349B] he ought not to be regarded as anyone other than the one proclaimed in those terms. From this agreement between the events concerning him and the Creator’s Scriptures, credibility must now be restored—even by the prejudgment of the greater part—to those passages that, because they admit of differing interpretations, are either called into doubt or denied. We now build further upon this foundation by adducing corresponding passages from the Creator’s Scriptures concerning what had been foretold as destined to occur after Christ. For the dispensation would not be found fulfilled unless the one had come after whom it was to occur.

Behold all the nations emerging thereafter from the abyss of human error toward God the Creator and [Note: [Col. 0349D] “And” omitted by Semler.] toward God’s Christ; and, if you dare, deny that this was prophesied. But immediately the Father’s promise will confront you in the Psalms (Ps. 2:7) [Note: [Col. 0349D] “Confronts,” Rhenanus, Semler, Oberthür.]:

> You are my Son; today I have begotten you. Ask of [Note: [Col. 0349D] Thus Franeker.] me, and I shall give you the nations as your inheritance, [Col. 0349C] and the ends of the earth as your possession.

You will no more be able to claim him as David’s son rather than Christ [Note: [Col. 0349D] “His,” Semler.], or that the ends of the earth were promised to David—who ruled within the single nation of the Jews—rather than to Christ, who has already taken the entire world by faith in his Gospel.

So too through Isaiah (Isa. 42:6–7):

> Behold, I have appointed you for the dispensation of my people [Note: [Col. 0349D] “My” rejected by Semler and Junius from the Septuagint.], as a light of the nations, to open the eyes of the blind—

that is, of those in error—

> to release those bound in chains—

that is, to free them from sins—

> and from the prison cell—

that is, from death—

> those sitting in darkness—

namely, the darkness of ignorance. If these things occur through Christ, they will not have been prophesied of anyone other than the one through whom they occur.

Again elsewhere (Isa. 55:4):

> Behold, I have appointed you as a witness among the nations, a leader and commander of the nations. Nations [Col. 0350A] that do not know you will call upon you, and peoples will take refuge with you.

You cannot interpret these words of David, because he prefaced them with:

> And I shall establish for you an everlasting covenant, the holy and faithful assurances of David.

Indeed, from this you ought all the more to understand Christ, reckoned as descended from David according to the flesh because of the registration of the Virgin Mary. For concerning this promise an oath is sworn to David in the Psalm (Ps. 132:11):

> From the fruit of your womb I shall set one upon your throne.

Whose womb is this? David’s own? Certainly not, for David was not going to give birth. Nor is it his wife’s, for Scripture would not have said, “From the fruit of your womb,” but rather, “From the fruit of your wife’s womb.” By speaking, therefore, of his own [Note: [Col. 0350D] “However,” Rhenanus, Semler, Oberthür.] womb, it remains that Scripture indicated someone from his line, the fruit of whose womb would be the flesh of Christ, which blossomed from Mary’s uterus. For this reason it named only “the fruit of the womb,” as belonging properly to the womb alone and not also to a man; and it traced that womb back to David as the founder of the line and father of the family. For because it could not ascribe the virgin’s womb to a husband [Note: [Col. 0350D] “That womb” was absent in Rhenanus.], it ascribed it to David, the father of her line.

Thus the new dispensation found today in Christ will be the one the Creator was then promising: [Col. 0350B] “the holy and faithful assurances of David,” now holy by its sacred character and faithful by virtue of the resurrection. For the prophet Nathan also, in the Second [Note: [Col. 0350D] “First,” Semler; “in the first,” Paris.] Book of Kingdoms, makes a declaration to David:

> To his seed, which shall come, he says, from his own womb (2 Sam. 7:12).  
> [Note: [Col. 0350D] “Your seed … your,” Franeker.]

If you were simply to expound this of Solomon, you would make me laugh. For it would appear that David gave birth to Solomon. Or is Christ indicated here too as David’s seed from that womb—namely Mary’s—which would be of David’s line? Indeed [Note: [Col. 0350D] “Because,” Rhenanus, Semler, Oberthür.], it was rather Christ who would build God’s temple, namely the holy human being in whom the Spirit of God would dwell as in a more excellent temple; and Christ was far more to be regarded as the Son of God than Solomon as the son of David. Finally, a throne forever and a kingdom forever are more applicable to Christ than to Solomon, who was plainly a temporal king. Moreover, [Note: [Col. 0350D] “And” omitted by Semler.] God’s mercy did not depart from [Col. 0350C] Christ; but God’s anger did come upon Solomon after his debauchery and idolatry. For Satan raised up against him [Note: [Col. 0350D] Franeker adds “that is.”] an Edomite enemy.

Since, therefore, none of these things applies to Solomon but all apply to Christ, the principle governing our interpretations will be certain, since the very outcome of events confirms that they were manifestly foretold of Christ; and thus in him will be found “the holy and faithful assurances of David.” God appointed him, not David, as a witness to the nations; him, not David, as leader and commander of the nations, for David commanded Israel alone. Today the nations that did not know Christ call upon him, and today peoples who formerly did not know him take refuge with Christ. What you see taking place cannot be called future.

## CHAPTER XXI

[Col. 0351A]

Nor can you maintain that objection of yours in order to distinguish two Christs—as though the Jewish Christ were destined by the Creator solely to gather back his people from dispersion, whereas your Christ had been bestowed by the supremely good God to liberate the entire human race—since, after all, the Creator’s Christians are found to be earlier than Marcion’s. From the time when all peoples were called into his kingdom, from the time when “God reigned from the tree,” there was as yet no Cerdo, much less Marcion.

But when you have been refuted regarding the calling of the nations, you next transform them into proselytes. You ask who from among the nations pass over to the Creator, although proselytes are named separately by the Prophet as people of a different and distinctive status:

> Behold, says Isaiah (Isa. 16:4), proselytes will come through me to you—

showing that the proselytes themselves [Col. 0351B] would also come to God through Christ. And [Note: [Col. 0351D] “So that,” Codex Wouwer.] the nations—that is, we ourselves—likewise had [Note: [Col. 0351D] “May have,” Semler.] their own designation, as those hoping in Christ:

> And in his name, he says (Isa. 42:4), the nations will hope.

But the proselytes whom you substitute in the proclamation concerning the nations are not accustomed to hope in Christ’s name, but in the ordinance of Moses, from whom their institution derives.

Besides, the summoning of the nations arose in the last days. Isaiah says in these very words (Isa. 2:2):

> And it shall be, he says, in the last days, that the mountain of the Lord shall be manifest—

that is, the loftiness of God—

> and the house of God above the highest mountains—

that is, Christ, God’s universal temple, in whom God is worshiped, established above all [Note: [Col. 0351D] Semler adds “for.”] eminences of virtues and powers—

> and all nations shall come to him; and many shall go and say: Come, let us ascend [Col. 0351C] the mountain of the Lord and the house of the God of Jacob; and he will proclaim his way to us, and we shall walk in it. For from Zion shall go forth the law, and the word of the Lord from Jerusalem.

This will be his way: the Gospel of a new law and a new word, now in Christ, not in Moses.

> And he shall judge among the nations—

that is, concerning their error—

> and shall convict a great people—

first and foremost the Jews themselves and the proselytes—

> and they shall beat their swords into ploughshares and their *sibynae* into sickles.  
> [Note: [Col. 0351D] “Their *sibynae* into sickles.” In Isaiah, according to the Septuagint version, the Greek reads καὶ τὰς ζιβύνας αὐτῶν εἰς δρέπανα, “and their *zibynae* into sickles.” According to [Col. 0352D] Tertullian, a ζιβύνη is a kind of hunting-spear. Suidas writes that it means ὁλοσίδηρον ἀκόντιον, that is, an entirely iron missile, and sometimes λόγχην or σπάθην, that is, a lance or sword. RHENANUS.]

That is, they will turn the contrivances of harmful minds, hostile tongues, and every kind of malice and blasphemy into pursuits of moderation and peace.

> And nation shall not take up sword against nation—

that is, the sword of discord—

> and they shall no longer learn to wage war—

that is, to carry hostilities through to completion. Thus here too you may learn that the Christ promised was powerful not in war but as a bringer of peace.

Either deny that these things were [Note: [Col. 0351D] “But,” another reading.] [Col. 0351D] prophesied, though they are seen before your eyes, or deny that they have been fulfilled, though they are read in Scripture. Or, if you deny neither, they will have been fulfilled in the one of whom they were prophesied. Consider further the very entry and onward course [Col. 0352A] of the calling among the nations, which in the last days are coming to God the Creator, not becoming proselytes, whose summoning belongs rather to the earliest days. [Note: [Col. 0351D] “Is” omitted by Semler; others read “the summoning is.”] For the apostles introduced this faith.

## CHAPTER XXII

You also have the work of the apostles foretold:

> How timely are the feet of those who proclaim good things!

—not war or evil. [Note: [Col. 0351D] “Evil,” singular, Semler.] The Psalm also responds (Ps. 19:4):

> Their sound has gone out into all the earth, and their voices to the ends of the earth—

the voices, namely, of those carrying about the law that had gone forth from Zion and the word of the Lord from Jerusalem, so that what was written might be fulfilled (Isa. 46:12–13):

> Those far from my righteousness have drawn near to my righteousness and truth.

When the apostles girded themselves for this undertaking, they renounced the elders, rulers, and priests [Col. 0352B] of the Jews. “Was it not especially,” you say [Note: [Col. 0352D] “You ask,” Junius.], “because they were preachers of another God?” On the contrary, [Note: [Col. 0352D] Franeker adds “he says.”] they were preachers of that very same God whose Scripture they were at that very moment fulfilling.

> Depart, depart, cries Isaiah (Isa. 52:11), go out from there and touch no unclean thing—

namely, blasphemy against Christ.

> Go out from the midst of it—

that is, of the synagogue—

> separate yourselves [Note: [Col. 0352D] “Be separated,” Franeker.], you who bear the Lord’s [Note: [Col. 0352D] “Of the Lord,” Semler.] vessels.

For by then, in accordance with what was written above (Isa. 52:10),

> the Lord had revealed his Holy One by his arm—

that is, Christ by his power—

> before the nations, so that all the nations and the ends of the earth might see the salvation that was from God.

Thus too, in departing from Judaism itself, when through the freedom of the Gospel they were exchanging the bonds and burdens of the Law, they were carrying out the Psalm (Ps. 2:3):

> Let us break their bonds apart and cast their yoke from us—

[Col. 0352C] certainly after

> the nations had raged and the peoples devised vain things; the kings of the earth took their stand, and the rulers assembled together against the Lord and against his Christ.

What did the apostles subsequently suffer? “Every injustice of persecution,” you say, “at the hands, of course, of the Creator’s people, because they were enemies of the one whom the apostles preached.” Did [Note: [Col. 0352D] “And those who,” Paris, Franeker; “and what,” Rhenanus.] the Creator—if he was Christ’s enemy—not only foretell that Christ’s apostles would suffer this, but even reproach people for it? For he would neither have foretold the dispensation of another God, whom, as you will have it, he did not know, nor would he have reproached conduct for which he himself had made provision.

> See how (Isa. 57:1) the righteous man perishes, and no one takes it to heart; righteous men are taken away, and no one notices. For the righteous man has been removed from the presence of injustice [Note: [Col. 0352D] “Justice,” another reading.].

Who is this, if not Christ?

> Come, they say (Wis. 2:12), [Col. 0352D] let us remove the righteous man, because he is useless to us.

Accordingly, by both placing before us and then adding the suffering of Christ himself, he likewise prophesied that Christ’s righteous ones would suffer the same things—both the apostles and thereafter all the faithful—marked [Col. 0353A] with that sign of which Ezekiel speaks (Ezek. 9:4):

> The Lord says to me: Pass through [Note: [Col. 0353D] Semler adds “in.”] the midst of the gate, into the midst [Note: [Col. 0353D] “The middle gate,” Franeker.] of Jerusalem, and place the sign [Note: [Col. 0353D] “Signs,” Semler.] Tau upon the foreheads of the men.

For the Greek letter Tau [Note: [Col. 0353D] “For the Greek letter Tau itself, and our own, etc.” Our fathers appear to have imitated this very practice when, at the beginning of the Canon in ancient missals, in the letter T where the words read, “Therefore, most merciful Father,” they commonly displayed a painted cross of Christ; in its place today the image of the cross itself has been introduced on the immediately preceding page. And indeed Blessed Jerome, imitating Tertullian almost word for word in his Commentary, says: “The final letter Tau has the likeness of the cross, which is drawn on the foreheads of Christians and marked by the frequent signing of the hand.” PAMELIUS.]—our T—is the form of the cross which he foretold would be upon our foreheads in the true and universal Jerusalem. There the twenty-first Psalm (Ps. 22:22), speaking in the person of Christ himself to the Father, sings that Christ’s brothers, namely God’s children, would render glory to God the Father:

> I shall declare your name to my brothers; in the midst of the church I shall sing a hymn to you.

For because this was to be done today in his name and Spirit, he rightly foretold that he himself would do it. And a little later:

> From you comes my praise in the great church.

And in the sixty-seventh Psalm (Ps. 68:26):

> In the churches bless the Lord [Col. 0353B] God—

so that the prophecy of Malachi might likewise concur (Mal. 1:10–11):

> I take no pleasure in you, says the Lord, and I shall not accept your sacrifices; for from the rising of the sun to its setting my name has been glorified among the nations, and in every place a sacrifice is offered to my name, a pure sacrifice—

namely, the rendering of glory, blessing, praise, and hymns. Since all these things are found among you too—the sign upon the foreheads, the sacraments of the churches, and the purity of the sacrifices—you must now burst out and say that the Creator’s Spirit prophesied for your Christ.

## CHAPTER XXIII

Now, since together with the Jews you deny that their Christ has come, take note also of the fate which they were foretold to incur after Christ because of the impiety with which they [Col. 0353C] both [Note: [Col. 0353D] “And” omitted by Semler.] despised and killed him.

First, from the day when, according to Isaiah (Isa. 2:20),

> man cast away his objects of contempt in gold and silver, which they had made for worshiping vain things and owls [Note: [Col. 0353D] “Harmful things,” another reading.]—

that is, from the time when the human race, once the truth had been made clear through Christ, cast away its idols—see whether what follows was fulfilled:

> For the Lord Sabaoth removed from Judea and Jerusalem, among other things, both the prophet and the wise master-builder (Isa. 3:1–2)—

namely, the Holy Spirit, who builds the Church, that is, God’s temple, house, and city; for from that time God’s grace ceased among them.

And (Isa. 5:6)

> the clouds were commanded not to rain upon the vineyard of Sorech—

that is, the heavenly benefits were commanded not to come upon the house of Israel. For it had produced thorns, [Col. 0354A] with which it had crowned the Lord; and it had produced not righteousness but the outcry by which it had dragged [Note: [Col. 0353D] Junius conjectures “thrust.”] him to the cross. Thus, when the dews of spiritual gifts had been withdrawn [Note: [Col. 0353D] “The earlier things of the charism,” another reading; others, “the principles of the charisms.”], the Law and the Prophets lasted until John.

Then, since through their persistent fury the Lord’s name was blasphemed among the nations because of them, as it is written (Isa. 52:5),

> Because of you my name is blasphemed among the nations—

for the disrepute began with them—and during the intervening period from Tiberius to Vespasian [Note: [Col. 0354D] “And the intervening period from Tiberius,” etc. He names Vespasian because Jerusalem was devastated under him, as is better known from the Jewish histories of Josephus and Philo than to require fuller treatment. PAMELIUS.], they did not understand repentance [Note: [Col. 0354D] Junius: “Since they had neglected repentance”; Latinus: “of repentance”],

> their land was made desolate (Isa. 1:7), their cities burned with fire [Note: [Col. 0354D] “With fire” omitted by Rhenanus, Semler, Oberthür.]; foreigners devour their country in their very sight; the daughter of Zion has been left [Note: [Col. 0354D] “Has been” omitted by Semler; he adds “and.”] like a watchtower in a vineyard, or like a little hut in a cucumber field.  
> [Note: [Col. 0354D] “A little hut in a cucumber field.” *Casula* is the diminutive of *casa*, which in Greek is σκηνή, “tent.” LE PRIEUR.]

This occurred because Israel did not know the Lord and the people did not [Col. 0354B] understand him, but abandoned him and provoked the Holy One of Israel to indignation.

So too the conditional threat of the sword:

> If you are unwilling and will not listen to me, the sword will devour you (Isa. 1:20),

proved that he was Christ, through their refusal to hear whom they perished. He also, in the fifty-eighth Psalm, asks the Father for their dispersion (Ps. 59:11):

> Scatter them in your power.

And again through Isaiah, pleading for their burning, he says:

> Because of me these things have happened to you; in anguish you shall sleep.

This would be utterly pointless if they suffered these things not because of the one who had pronounced that they would suffer on his account, but because of another God’s Christ.

“But,” you say, “another God’s Christ was driven to the cross by the Creator’s forces and powers, as by rivals.” Yet behold, he is shown to have been defended by the Creator (Isa. 53:9):

> and the wicked were given for his burial—

namely, those who had asserted that he was stolen away—

> and the rich for his death—

namely, those who had both purchased his betrayal from Judas and purchased from the soldiers the false testimony that the corpse had been stolen.

Therefore, either these events did not befall the Jews because of him—but you are refuted by the agreement [Note: [Col. 0354D] Semler adds “and.”] of the meaning of the Scriptures with the outcome of events and the sequence of the times—or, if they did befall them because of him, the Creator could not have avenged anyone but his own Christ. He should instead have rewarded Judas if the Jews had destroyed his Lord’s adversary.

Certainly, if the Creator’s Christ, because of whom these things are foretold as destined to be suffered, has not yet come, then they will suffer them when he does come. And where then is the daughter of Zion that is to be abandoned, when today she does not exist? Where are the cities that are to be burned, when they already lie in ruins? Where is the dispersion [Col. 0355A] of the nation, when it is already banished from its land? Restore the condition of Judea in which the Creator’s Christ may find it, and then contend that another Christ has come.

But what sort of thing is this—that the Creator admitted through his own heaven the one whom he was going to put to death upon his own earth, after the more honorable and glorious region of his kingdom had been violated, his very court and citadel trampled underfoot? Or was this rather what he intended? Plainly, though a jealous God, he nevertheless prevailed. Be ashamed, you who believe in a defeated God. What will you hope for from one who was unable to protect himself? For either he was overpowered through weakness by the Creator’s powers and human beings, or through malice he endured it so as to fasten so great a crime upon them.

## CHAPTER XXIV

“On the contrary,” you say, “I hope from him for that which itself bears witness to the distinction: the kingdom of God, an eternal and heavenly possession. Your Christ, however, promises the Jews their former condition through the restoration of their land and, after the course of life is completed, refreshment in Abraham’s bosom in the underworld.”

What an excellent God—if, once appeased, he restores what he had also taken away in anger! O your God, who both strikes and heals, creates evils and makes peace! O God merciful even as far as the underworld! [Note: [Col. 0355C] “But concerning Abraham’s bosom.” He alludes to the book *On Paradise*, or rather to Book IV *Against Marcion*, chapter 34, where he discusses the passage in Luke 16 concerning Abraham’s bosom, in which Lazarus finds refreshment, and the underworld, [Col. 0355D] where torments are expressly described, showing that the underworld is one thing and Abraham’s bosom another. PAMELIUS.]

But Abraham’s bosom will be treated in its proper place. As for the restoration of Judea, which the Jews themselves hope for just as it is described because they are led by the names of places and regions: it would take too long to trace how its allegorical interpretation applies spiritually to Christ, the Church, and its condition and fruit. Moreover, [Note: [Col. 0355D] “Set out in another work that we entitle *On the Hope of the Faithful*.” This work of Tertullian was contained in the Codex Agobardinus, or at least in the exemplar from which Agobard had his own copy made. Even today the title appears among the twenty-four books included in its index. Jerome also mentions this work in his account of Papias of Hierapolis, in chapter 36 of his Commentary on Ezekiel, and in the preface to the eighteenth book on Isaiah. RIGAULT.] it has been set out in another work that we entitle *On the Hope of the Faithful*; and the matter would be superfluous in the present discussion, because the question concerns not an earthly but a heavenly promise.

[Col. 0355C] For we also confess that a kingdom has been promised to us on earth, but before heaven and in another condition: namely, after the resurrection, for a thousand years, [Note: [Col. 0355D] “In Jerusalem, a city of divine workmanship brought down from heaven.” Overly credulous of Jewish tales—which they called δευτερώσεις (*deuterōseis*, “secondary traditions”)—and deceived by certain words of Ezekiel and Isaiah wrongly understood, they hoped for a Jerusalem of gold and precious stones on earth. First Papias held this view; soon afterward Justin, who calls men rejoicing in that hope ὀρθογνώμονας κατὰ πάντα Χριστιανούς, “Christians entirely orthodox in judgment”; then Irenaeus, our own Septimius, Victorinus of Poetovio, Lactantius, [Col. 0356C] and others. Jerome, in that preface to Isaiah, says that Dionysius of Alexandria composed a book against Irenaeus and that the tale was elegantly ridiculed in it. Augustine, in Book XX of *The City [Col. 0356D] of God*, says the opinion would have been tolerable in some measure if it were believed that some spiritual delights would be granted to the saints through the Lord’s presence on that Sabbath—that is, in the seventh millennium of years. He even admits that he himself once held this opinion. RIGAULT.] in Jerusalem, a city of divine workmanship brought down from heaven. The Apostle designates this city as “our mother above” and, by declaring that our *politeuma*—that is, our civic citizenship—is in heaven, plainly assigns it to some heavenly city.

Ezekiel also knows this city (Ezek. 48), and the apostle John saw it (Rev. 21); and the utterance of the new prophecy, which is acknowledged among us, testifies to it, inasmuch as it foretold, as a sign, that even the image of the city would be presented to sight before its actual manifestation. [Note: [Col. 0356D] “Indeed, this was recently fulfilled during Severus’s eastern campaign,” namely against the Parthians. RIGAULT.—I have thus far been unable to find this prodigy among pagan or even ecclesiastical historians; I therefore leave it to the diligent reader to investigate whether anything of this sort occurred in Judea during the eastern campaign of the emperor Severus, to which the Author appears to refer. PAMELIUS.] Indeed, this was recently [Note: [Col. 0355C] “Recently” rejected by Rhenanus, Semler, Oberthür.] fulfilled during the eastern campaign. [Note: [Col. 0356D] “For it is established even by pagan witnesses.” Since this was reported among the prodigies, it is credible that the opinion of the Chiliasts or Millenarians was greatly strengthened by it. RIGAULT.] For it is established even by pagan witnesses that for forty days in Judea, in the morning hours, a city hung down from heaven; as the day advanced, the whole configuration of its walls gradually vanished, and at other times no city was visible nearby.

We say that God has provided this city to receive the saints at their resurrection and refresh them with an abundance of every good thing—spiritual, of course—as compensation for those things which in this world we have either despised or lost. Indeed, it is both just and worthy of God that his servants should rejoice in that very place where [Col. 0356B] they were afflicted in his name.

This is the rationale of the earthly kingdom. After its thousand years—within which period the resurrection of the saints is completed, as they rise earlier or later according to their merits—then, when the destruction of the world and the conflagration of judgment have been accomplished [Note: [Col. 0356D] “The conflagration having been committed.” Fire having been applied: namely, that final ἐκπύρωσις (*ekpyrōsis*, “cosmic conflagration”). RIGAULT.], we shall be changed “in an instant” into angelic substance, namely through that clothing-over with incorruption, and shall be transferred into the heavenly kingdom.

This kingdom is now being discussed as though it had not been foretold by the Creator, and as though it therefore proved Christ to belong to another God, by whom it was first and uniquely revealed. Learn now from what follows both that it was foretold by the Creator and that, even without such foretelling, it ought to be believed as belonging to the Creator.

What do you think when Abraham’s seed, after the first promise in which [Note: [Col. 0356C] “Which,” Semler.] it is promised a multitude like the sand, is also appointed to be like the stars? Are these not auspices of both an earthly and a heavenly dispensation? When Isaac blesses his son Jacob, saying (Gen. 27:28),

> May God give you of the dew of heaven and of the richness of the earth,

are these not examples of both kinds of bounty?

Indeed, the very structure of the blessing should be observed here. In Jacob, who is the figure of the later and more highly favored people—that is, of us—the first promise is the heavenly dew, and the second the richness of the earth. [Col. 0357A] For we are first invited to heavenly things when we are torn away from the world, and afterward are found also to obtain earthly things. And [Note: [Col. 0357C] “Your Gospel also contains.” He cites Marcion’s Gospel, which he preserved after corrupting it for his own purposes. I conjecture that it was Luke’s Gospel, upon which he had laid rash hands. See the comments on the final chapter of the following book. LE PRIEUR.] your Gospel also contains:

> Seek first the kingdom of God, and these things shall be added to you (Luke 12:31).

In Esau’s case, however, Isaac first promises the earthly blessing and then adds the heavenly:

> From the richness of the earth, he says (Gen. 27:39), shall be your dwelling, and from the dew of heaven.

For the dispensation of the Jews is represented in Esau: sons earlier in birth but later in affection. Having first been imbued through the Law with earthly goods, they are afterward led toward heavenly things by believing through the Gospel.

When Jacob dreams (Gen. 28) of a ladder fixed upon earth and reaching to heaven, with some angels ascending and others descending and the Lord leaning upon it from above, shall we perhaps interpret rashly if we say that by this ladder the way to heaven is shown, by which some arrive there and from which others fall away, and that this is determined by the Lord’s judgment? [Note: [Col. 0357C] “Judgment,” Rhenanus, Semler, Oberthür.]

But why, as soon as he awoke and was first shaken with dread at the place, did he turn to interpreting the dream? For after saying,

> How terrible is this place!

he continues:

> It is nothing other than the house of God, and this is the gate of heaven.

For he had seen Christ the Lord, the temple of God and at the same time the gate through which heaven is entered. He certainly would not have named “the gate of heaven” if heaven were not entered under the Creator. But there is also a gate that receives and leads onward, a way already laid out by [Note: [Col. 0357C] Latinus: “A way laid out by Christ.”] Christ, of whom Amos says (Amos 9:6):

> He who builds his ascent into heaven—

plainly not for himself alone, but also for his people who will be with him. And:

> For you shall array them about yourself, he says (Isa. 49:18), like the adornment [Col. 0357C] of a bride.

Thus, as they press through him toward the heavenly kingdoms, the Spirit marvels and says (Isa. [Col. 0358A] 31:5; 60:8):

> They fly like kites. They fly like clouds and [Note: [Col. 0357C] Junius inserts: “Who are these who fly like clouds, and.”] like young doves toward me—

that is, as simply as doves. For according to the Apostle (1 Thess. 4:17),

# BOOK FOUR

[Col. 0357]

## [ARGUMENT]

[Col. 0357D] Tertullian is about to write against Marcion’s work, composed as a series of opposing contradictions and consequently entitled *Antitheses*. It was designed to separate the Law from the Gospel, thereby dividing two different Gods, one belonging to the Old Testament and the other to the New.

**Chapter 1.** First, without denying the difference between the commandments and disciplines, he demonstrates that they belong to one and the same God. For God the Creator himself foretold through his prophets that another and new law—namely, the Gospel—would arise in place of the Old, and an eternal covenant in place of a temporal one.

[Col. 0358D] **Chapter 2.** Next, he lays down that not only Luke’s Gospel—which Marcion admitted in adulterated form, though without assigning it any title—but also the Gospels of the apostles John and Matthew and of Mark, an apostolic man, possess authority no less than Luke’s.

**Chapter 3.** Nor is it an obstacle that the apostle Paul rebukes the apostles in Galatians. For they were censured not for corrupting or interpolating the Gospel, but only for their conduct; indeed, the very arrangement of their Gospel had been perverted by false apostles.

**Chapter 4.** When, however, the title of the Gospel is disputed—Marcion claiming the Gospel as his own, while the Author affirms that it is an adulterated form of his own Gospel, namely Luke’s—authority is to be claimed for the one found to be earlier, and corruption charged against the later one. This is especially so since Marcion had for some time believed the catholic Church and only afterward defected into heresy.

**Chapter 5.** For Luke’s Gospel possesses authority from the apostolic churches, among which it is established that whatever is held sacred was handed down by the apostles; and the remaining Gospels—John’s, Matthew’s, and Mark’s—do so likewise. The same is true also of the Apocalypse, as is shown by tracing the succession of bishops back to its origin.

**Chapter 6.** Then, proceeding to his subject, the Author establishes that there is no difference between the Old and New Testaments from the very fact that our Christ fulfilled the Creator’s prophecies, realized his promises, [Col. 0359B] and supported his laws.

**Chapter 7.** He first confirms this from the latter part of Luke 4, concerning the demon expelled in the synagogue at Capernaum, because Marcion had erased Luke’s first three chapters and the beginning of the fourth as contrary to his position.

**Chapter 8.** From the same chapter, partly before and partly after the aforementioned narrative—for Marcion had also transposed it—concerning Christ’s teaching in the synagogue at Nazareth and other demons.

**Chapter 9.** Likewise, from Luke 5, concerning the calling of the apostles Simon and the sons of Zebedee, and the leper who was cleansed.

**Chapter 10.** From the same chapter, concerning the paralytic who was healed and the designation “Son of Man.”

**Chapter 11.** From the same chapter, concerning the tax collector summoned by the Lord, fasting, Christ as bridegroom, and the parable of the new and old wine.

**Chapter 12.** Next, from Luke 6, concerning the disciples who gathered ears of grain and the withered hand healed on the Sabbath.

**Chapter 13.** From the same chapter, concerning the ascent of the mountain and the prayer there by night, the apostles’ number of twelve, the change of Peter’s name, and the assembly of the nations around Christ.

**Chapter 14.** From the same chapter, concerning the Beatitudes, or the Lord’s sermon on the mountain.

**Chapter 15.** From the same chapter, concerning the various curses in which he called down “Woe” upon the Pharisees.

**Chapter 16.** From the same chapter, concerning the command to love both enemies and one’s neighbor.

**Chapter 17.** From the same chapter, concerning the prohibition of interest and other sayings of the Lord almost to the end of the chapter.

[Col. 0359D] **Chapter 18.** Also from Luke 7, concerning the centurion’s faith, the raising of the widow’s son, John the Baptist, and the sinful woman.

**Chapter 19.** Thereafter, from Luke 8, concerning the wealthy women who accompanied Christ, the Lord’s sayings to his disciples, and Christ’s mother and brothers.

**Chapter 20.** From the same chapter, concerning Christ’s miracles, by which he commanded the winds and sea, cast out a legion of demons, and healed a woman’s flow of blood.

**Chapter 21.** Likewise from Luke 9, concerning the disciples sent out to preach, the feeding of the people in the wilderness, Peter’s confession, “You are the Christ,” and the foretold persecution of the faithful.

[Col. 0360A] **Chapter 22.** From the same chapter, concerning Christ’s appearance with Moses and Elijah in the solitude of the mountain.

**Chapter 23.** From the same chapter, concerning the Lord’s sayings to the Pharisees, the love of little children, and his rebuke of the man who said, “I shall follow you wherever you go.”

**Chapter 24.** Further, from Luke 10, concerning the selection of the seventy disciples and the power given to them.

**Chapter 25.** From the same chapter, concerning the Lord’s prayer to the Father, his sayings to the disciples, and his answer to the teacher of the Law.

**Chapter 26.** Then, from Luke 11, concerning the Lord’s Prayer, other forms of prayer, and the parables relating to it; the expulsion of a deaf demon; and the exclamation of the woman from the crowd.

**Chapter 27.** From the same chapter, concerning the Pharisees who sought a sign and his other rebukes of them.

[Col. 0360B] **Chapter 28.** From Luke 12, concerning various sayings of the Lord to the disciples and the parable of the rich man who flattered himself over the yield of his fields.

**Chapter 29.** From the same chapter, concerning the command that the soul should not be anxious about food, nor the body about clothing; the girding of the loins; the blessed servants; the fire sent upon the earth; and other sayings of the Lord.

**Chapter 30.** Again, from Luke 13, concerning a healing performed on the Sabbath, parables, and other sayings of the Lord.

**Chapter 31.** Also from Luke 14, concerning what kind of people should be invited to a dinner or luncheon, and the dinner to which a man invited many.
