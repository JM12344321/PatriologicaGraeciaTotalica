## CHAPTER XXXVII

Since, therefore, the truth is adjudged to those of us who walk according to that rule which the Church received from the Apostles, it follows that his proposed argument stands: heretics are not to be admitted to enter into an appeal to the Scriptures, since they are not Christians and consequently have been disinherited from possession of the Christian writings.

If these things are so, so that the truth is adjudged to us—all of us who walk according to that rule which the Church handed down from the Apostles, the Apostles from Christ, and Christ from God [Note: [Col. 0050C] *Accepit*, “received,” Pam.]—then the rationale of our proposal is established, which determines that heretics are not to be admitted [Col. 0051A] to enter into [Note: [Col. 0051C] *Ad eamdem*, “to the same,” Paris; *adeundam*, “to approach,” Rhen.] an appeal to the Scriptures: without the Scriptures we prove that they have no claim upon the Scriptures. For if they are heretics, they cannot be Christians, since what they follow by their own choosing and admit under the name [Note: [Col. 0051C] *Nomina*, “names,” Rig.; *nomen*, Jun.] of heretics is not something they possess from Christ. Thus, since they are not Christians, they acquire no right to the Christian writings. To them it may deservedly be said: Who are you? When and whence did you come? What are you doing on my property, when you are not mine? By what right, Marcion, do you cut down my woodland? By whose permission, Valentinus, do you divert my springs? By what authority, Apelles, do you move my boundaries [Note: [Col. 0051C] Rig., Fran., Paris, and Venet. add: “The possession is mine.”]? Why are the rest of you sowing and grazing here at your own pleasure? The possession is mine; I have possessed it from of old [Note: [Col. 0051C] The same editions add: “I possess it first.”]. I have secure title-deeds from the very [Note: [Col. 0051C] “and the very,” Rhen.] authors to whom the property belonged. I am the heir of the Apostles. [Col. 0051B] Just as they provided in their will, just as they entrusted it in faith [Note: [Col. 0051D] Jun., following legal usage, prefers the single word *fideicommiserunt*, “made it a fideicommissum.”], just as they bound me by solemn adjuration, so I hold it. You they certainly disinherited and repudiated forever, as strangers and enemies. But whence are heretics strangers and enemies to the Apostles, unless from their divergence in teaching, which each one, by his own judgment and against the Apostles, either produced or received?

## CHAPTER XXXVIII

PAM.] that punctiliousness of the Jewish law? He, therefore, who so emulously affected to reproduce in the affairs of idolatry the very things by which Christ’s sacraments are administered, certainly also desired and was able, being the same and acting with the same ingenuity, to adapt the documents of divine realities [Note: [Col. 0056C] “And of the holy Christians.” The meaning of this phrase must be gathered from the things that follow in chapter 44. He calls the Acts and Epistles of the Apostles “the documents of the holy Christians.” ALBASP.] and of the holy [Col. 0055B] Christians—meaning for meaning, words for words, parables for parables—to a profane and rival faith [Note: [Col. 0055C] *Attemperare*, “to accommodate,” Rig., Venet., Fran., Paris.].

And therefore no one ought to doubt either that the spiritual powers of wickedness, from whom heresies also come, were sent by the devil, or that heresies are indistinguishable from idolatry, since they belong to the same author and the same work as idolatry. They either fabricate another god in opposition to the Creator, or, if they confess one sole Creator, they describe him otherwise than he truly is. Accordingly, every falsehood they utter concerning God is, in a certain sense, a kind of idolatry [Note: [Col. 0056C] Rhen.: “Thus every falsehood concerning God—or nation, in a certain sense, sex—is idolatry.”].

## CHAPTER XLI

Finally, there must not be omitted, as a prescription we may advance against them, a description of heretical conduct itself: among them it is uncertain who is a catechumen and who a believer, for they enter alike, listen alike, and pray alike; they mingle the peace indiscriminately with everyone; their women dare to perform exorcisms and perhaps even to baptize; their ordinations are rash; one man is bishop today, another tomorrow; one is deacon today and reader tomorrow, presbyter today and layman tomorrow; for they impose priestly functions even upon laymen.

Nor shall I omit a description of heretical conduct itself: how futile, how earthly, how human it is—without seriousness, without authority, without discipline, as befits their faith. First of all, it is uncertain who is a catechumen and who a believer: they enter alike, [Col. 0056B] listen alike, and pray alike. Even if pagans should happen to come in, [Note: [Col. 0056D] “What is holy to the dogs.” We shall not pass over these words carelessly, for we think they refer to the Eucharist, concerning which he speaks as follows in chapter 25 of the book *On the Shows*: “From the mouth with which you have pronounced ‘Amen’ over that which is holy.” ALBASP.] they will cast what is holy to the dogs and pearls—though not genuine ones—before swine. They wish the overthrow of discipline to be regarded as simplicity, while the care we give to discipline they call pandering. They also mingle the peace indiscriminately with everyone; for it makes no difference to them, however divergent their treatments may be, provided that they conspire to overthrow the one truth. All are puffed up; all promise knowledge. Their catechumens are perfected before they have been instructed [Note: [Col. 0056C] *Educati*, “educated,” Paris.].

The heretical women themselves—how brazen they are! They dare to teach, to debate, to perform exorcisms, to promise healings, and perhaps [Note: [Col. 0056C] *Fortasse an*, Rhen.] even to baptize. Their ordinations are rash, frivolous, [Note: [Col. 0056D] “Inconstant.” All churchmen, whatever their rank might be, were assigned to fixed places; nor were they made bishops or priests for the sake of honor or a livelihood, but were ordained according to the necessity and use of the place or people. Just as they were not permitted to hasten elsewhere and change their sees, so neither could they be removed without cause from their office in the place to which they had been ordained and assigned. Among the heretics, however, sees were changed at will, and, as he says, one man is bishop today, another tomorrow. ALBASP.] inconstant. At one time [Note: [Col. 0056C] *Tunc*, “then,” Rhen.] [Col. 0057A] they appoint neophytes; at another, [Note: [Col. 0057C] “Those entangled with the world.” He means those who held some prefecture among the pagans. For many Christians who had attained some dignity or military rank nevertheless continued to discharge the duties of those honors, and it did not appear right to ordain such men to ecclesiastical honors. ALBASP.] men entangled with the world; at another, apostates from us—so that they may bind them by glory, since they cannot bind them by truth. Nowhere does a man advance more easily than in the camp of rebels, where merely to be present is to earn merit. Thus one man is bishop today, another tomorrow; one is deacon today and reader tomorrow; presbyter today and layman tomorrow; for they impose [Note: [Col. 0057C] *Laici*, “laymen,” Rhen.] priestly functions even upon laymen.

## CHAPTER XLII

In the ministry of the word also, their business is not to convert pagans but to subvert our people; nor do they show reverence to their own presiding officers. Hence schisms among them do not become apparent, however much they differ among themselves from their own rules and even from their own founders.

[Note: [Col. 0057C] “But concerning the ministry of the word.” I do not think he is speaking of the ministry conducted in the churches, since pagans were excluded from it, but of that conducted familiarly at home or elsewhere; for Christians had no greater concern than gradually to instill Christ’s precepts into pagans in order to draw them to the faith. ALBASP.] But what shall I say concerning the ministry of the word, [Col. 0057B] since their business is not to convert [Note: [Col. 0057C] *Evertendis*, “subverting,” Jun.; *convertendis*, “converting,” others.] pagans but to subvert our people? They seek this glory more eagerly: to bring ruin upon those who stand, rather than to raise up those who lie fallen. For their own work does not arise from a structure of their own, but from the destruction of the truth. They undermine our buildings in order to construct their own. [Note: [Col. 0057D] “Take away from them the Law of Moses, the Prophets, and God the Creator, and they have no accusation to utter.” He is marking Cerdon, Marcion, and Apelles, who dared to tear down even those foundations of the Christian religion. For in the *Catalogue of Heretics*, where he speaks of Cerdon, he says: “He rejects the prophecies and the Law; he renounces God the Creator.” Marcion, moreover, was Cerdon’s disciple, and Apelles Marcion’s. He therefore says that the minds of heretics are so constituted that they destroy rather than build. Take away from them the Law of Moses, the Prophets, and God the Creator, and they will have no legal ground on which to plead a case against you because of it; there will be nothing of which they can complain. For they long ago testified that these things in no way belonged to them. RIG.] Take away from them the Law [Note: [Col. 0057C] *In lege*, “in the Law,” Paris.] of Moses, the Prophets, and God the Creator, and they have no accusation to utter. Thus it comes about that they more readily produce the collapse of standing buildings than the reconstruction of fallen ruins. Only for works of this sort do they act humble, flattering, and submissive. For the rest, they do not know how to show reverence even to their own presiding officers.

And this is why schisms among heretics are scarcely [Col. 0057C] apparent: because, when they exist, they do not become apparent. [Note: [Col. 0057D] “Schism is unity for them.” This is the reading of Ursinus’ manuscript, and it is entirely true. There are indeed schisms among heretics, but we notice them less because of their common and completely harmonious disagreement with us. RIG.] Schism is [Col. 0058A] unity for them [Note: [Col. 0057C] *Ipsa*, “itself,” Seml.]. I am lying if they do not even differ among themselves from their own rules, while each man modifies what he has received according to his own judgment, just as the man who handed it down originally composed it according to his own judgment. The development of the thing recognizes its own nature and the customary character of its origin. The Valentinians claimed the same right that Valentinus had, and the Marcionites the same right that Marcion had: to innovate the faith according to their own judgment. Finally, when examined thoroughly, all heresies are found to disagree with their founders on many points. Most do not even have churches; motherless, without a see, orphaned in faith, banished, and without a home [Note: [Col. 0057C] *Quasi sibi late*, Fran., Jun.; *sibi late*, Paris; *sibilatae*, “hissed away,” Rhen.], they wander about.
