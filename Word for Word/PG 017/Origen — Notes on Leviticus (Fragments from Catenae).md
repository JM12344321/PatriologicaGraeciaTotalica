## Leviticus 4:27
<!-- source-locus: 4.27 -->

**Verse 27.** “If a soul sins involuntarily,” it says, “and does not do any of all the commandments of the Lord which must be done.” “But if it has according to nature” is obscure in the transmitted text. Perhaps there are certain commandments of the Lord which one must not do, according to the saying, “I gave them statutes that were not good.” A soul therefore sins involuntarily when, under a pretext of piety, it observes what ought not to be observed. And it needs a sacrifice for forgiveness; the person who acts deliberately does not need a sacrifice, as did the one who became a Jew in order to gain Jews and circumcised Timothy.

[Source note: The clause rendered “But if it has according to nature” (`Εἰ δὲ κατὰ φύσιν ἔχει`) is corrupt or elliptical in the source. The final contrast is retained as transmitted, although its relation to the preceding sentence is not fully secure.]

## Leviticus 4:28
<!-- source-locus: 4.28 -->

**Verse 28.** It signifies, then, that the very thing which is offered is also called “sin.” Thus the saying must be understood: “They will eat the sins of my people.” If, then, the sinner seems to sacrifice the sin itself when repenting, he ought not to fall into the same things, since sin comes to life again. Thus it is possible to call Christ “sin,” as having been sacrificed for the sin of all.

[Source note: The source repeatedly misspells the Greek word for “sin” in this locus; the sense is nevertheless clear enough to translate.]

## Leviticus 12:2
<!-- source-locus: 12.2 -->

**Verse 2.** “A woman, if she is impregnated and bears a male child.” The prefatory phrase “if she is impregnated” may seem superfluous before “and bears a male child.” But I suggest whether, prophetically, the whole wording was spoken so that Mary, who bore a male child not from being impregnated, might not be thought unclean for having borne the Savior. Even without the preceding phrase “if she is impregnated,” Mary could be understood as not unclean; for she was not simply a woman, but a virgin.

[Source note: After “having borne the Savior,” the source has the defective words `εἴρηκεν ὅλον`; the translation gives only the secure sense of the surrounding argument.]

## Leviticus 13:45
<!-- source-locus: 13.45 -->

**Verse 45.** “The leper in whom there is an infection: his garments shall be loosened, and his head uncovered.” Why does it command the leper to have his head uncovered and his garments loosened? So that he may be recognizable and those approaching may not share in the uncleanness. Thus the Apostle said concerning those who sin: “Keep away from every brother who walks disorderly and not according to the tradition which you received from us”; and, “If anyone called a brother is sexually immoral, or greedy, or an idolater, or a drunkard, or abusive, or a robber, do not even eat with such a person.”

## Leviticus 23:2
<!-- source-locus: 23.2 -->

**Verse 2.** “These are the feasts of the Lord.” Every cheerful day on which it has been prescribed, as on a holy day, to do certain special things beyond the ordinary is called a feast. But not every feast is a public assembly; rather, according to the name, a public assembly occurs when people gather together from every place. Thus every public assembly is also a feast, but not every feast is also a public assembly. For example, Sabbaths and New Moons are feasts; but Unleavened Bread, the days of Weeks, and Tabernacles are not only feasts but also public assemblies.

[Source note: The first sentence contains an elliptical/corrupt phrase after “on which it has been prescribed”; the feast/public-assembly distinction is secure.]

## Leviticus 23:40
<!-- source-locus: 23.40 -->

**Verse 40.** “And palm branches.” From these things it is possible to understand that God again grants us, in Christ, the delights in paradise. The branches of evergreen plants signify in themselves the continuance of grace and the unfading character of hope.

## Leviticus 27:15
<!-- source-locus: 27.15 -->

**Verse 15.** “If the one who consecrated it redeems his house, he shall add to it the fifth of the silver valuation, and it shall be his.” But if someone does not [set] the value of a house or a field, but consecrates the things themselves to God, he shall set the value and redeem them with the fifth, and he will retain the possessions; he will not sell them to others. But even if he sells them, the buyer will retain them until the Jubilee, and after this they will belong to God.

## Leviticus 27:16
<!-- source-locus: 27.16 -->

**Verse 16.** “But if [it is] from a field.” The person who buys a field by purchase will not recover it for himself; rather, after giving the produce to God according to calculation, at the Jubilee he will restore the field to the person who sold it.
