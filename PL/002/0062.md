## CHAPTER IV

For the Jews say that from the beginning God sanctified the seventh day by resting on it from all the works that he had made, and that Moses therefore also said to the people: “Remember the day of the Sabbaths; sanctify it. You shall do no servile work on it” (Exod. 20:8), except what pertains to life. From this we understand instead that we must always keep Sabbath from every servile work, and not only on every seventh day, but throughout all time. And for this reason we must inquire which Sabbath God wished us to observe. For the Scriptures indicate both an eternal Sabbath and a temporal Sabbath. The prophet Isaiah says: “My soul hates your Sabbaths.” [Col. 0605C] And elsewhere he says: “You have profaned my Sabbaths” (Ezek. 22:8). From this we recognize that the temporal Sabbath is human, while the eternal Sabbath is regarded as divine. Concerning the latter he proclaims through Isaiah: “And it will be,” he says, “month after month, day after day, and Sabbath after Sabbath, and all flesh will come to worship in Jerusalem, says the Lord” (Isa. 66:23).

We understand this to have been fulfilled in the time of Christ, when all flesh—that is, every nation—came to Jerusalem to worship God the Father through Jesus Christ his Son, as was foretold through the prophet: [Col. 0606A] “Behold, proselytes will go through me to you” (Isa. 52 [Note: [Col. 0605D] “Behold, proselytes will go through me to you.” He cites the same Scripture in book III *Against Marcion* and explains it a little more fully, expressly ascribing it to Isaiah. Finally, let them show, as we said at the outset, that Adam kept the Sabbath; or that Abel, when he offered a holy victim to God, pleased him through the religious observance of the Sabbath; or that Enoch, when he was translated, was an observer of the Sabbath; or that Noah, who built the ark because of the immeasurable flood, observed the Sabbath; or that Abraham offered his son Isaac while observing the Sabbath; or that Melchizedek received the law of the Sabbath in his priesthood.

This is what happened; and when the period of the seventh day was finished, the walls of the city fell, just as had been foretold.

From this it is clearly shown that the Sabbath day fell within the number of those seven days: for seven days, from whatever day they take their beginning, must necessarily include the Sabbath day. ## CHAPTER V

Thus we also show that sacrifices consisting of earthly offerings and spiritual sacrifices were foretold [Note: [Col. 0608C] “Previously stated,” the same witnesses.]. And indeed, from the beginning, the earthly sacrifices of the elder son—that is, Israel—were foreshown in Cain, while the different sacrifices of the younger son, Abel—that is, of our people—were demonstrated. For Cain, the elder by birth, offered gifts to God from the produce of the earth, while the younger son Abel offered from the produce of his sheep. God looked favorably upon Abel and his gifts, but he did not look favorably upon Cain and his gifts.

And God said to Cain: “Why has your face fallen? Surely, if you offer rightly but do not divide rightly, [Col. 0607B] you have sinned? ## CHAPTER VI

Therefore, since it is evident that both a temporal Sabbath has been shown and an eternal Sabbath foretold; that fleshly circumcision has been foretold and spiritual circumcision indicated beforehand; that both a temporal law and an eternal law have been announced; and that fleshly sacrifices and spiritual sacrifices have been foreshown—it follows that, after all these commandments had earlier been given in a fleshly manner to the people of Israel, a time should supervene in which the commandments of the ancient law and the old ceremonies would cease, and the promise of the new [Col. 0609A] law, the recognition of spiritual sacrifices, and the promise of the New Covenant would supervene, as light from on high shone upon us who sat in darkness and were held in the shadow of death [Note: [Col. 0609C] Rhen. and Obert. add “light would arise”] (Luke 1:78–79).

Accordingly, since we have said that a new law was foretold by the prophets, and one not like [Note: [Col. 0609C] “It, and not such,” Rhen., Seml.; “and it not such,” Obert.] that which had already been given to their fathers at the time when he brought them [Note: [Col. 0609C] “Brought forth from the land of Egypt,” for “brought out.” It is surprising, however, by what line of reasoning he argues against the Jews, in order to demonstrate the end of the Mosaic law and the [Col. 0609D] advent of the Gospel law, which contains the truth of which the old law was the figure, confined as it was to certain ceremonies or rites and to outward worship; for the new law, without despising those rites, requires something more perfect and draws the spirit toward the contemplation and love of God. And first of all, indeed, it must be asked whether there is still awaited a legislator of the new law, an heir of the New Covenant, a priest of the new sacrifices, a purifier by the new circumcision, and an observer of the eternal Sabbath, [Note: [Col. 0609D] “Who would restrain the old law.” That is, abolish it, so as to introduce the new. LE PR.] who would restrain the old law, establish the New Covenant, offer new sacrifices, suppress the ancient ceremonies, restrain the old circumcision together with its Sabbath, and announce a new kingdom that will not be corrupted. For it must also be asked whether [Note: [Col. 0609C] “Here indeed,” Rhen., Obert.; “here, I say, of the new,” Paris.] the legislator of the new law, the observer of the spiritual Sabbath, the high priest of eternal sacrifices, and the eternal ruler of the eternal kingdom has already come [Note: [Col. 0609C] “Whether or not,” Rhen., Obert.], or not.

And if he has already come, he must be served; if he has not yet come, he must be awaited—provided that it is evident that at his advent the commandments of the old [Note: [Col. 0609C] “When the former,” the same witnesses; “to restrain,” Fran., Paris.] law must be suppressed and the beginnings of the new law must come into operation. And first of all it must be established that the ancient law and the prophets could not have ceased unless he had come who was announced through that same law and those same prophets as the one who was to come.

## CHAPTER VII

Nicephorus, *Ecclesiastical History*, book VII, ch. 28, is witness to this. PAM.—Augustine likewise attests it against the epistle of Mani. Augustine has “Africa”; we have “Libya.” SEML.] Armenia, Phrygia, Cappadocia; those inhabiting Pontus, Asia, and Pamphylia; those dwelling in Egypt and inhabiting the region of Africa beyond Cyrene; Romans [Note: [Col. 0610C] “Rome,” Obert., Seml.] and residents; and then Jews in Jerusalem” (Acts 2:9–10), and [Col. 0610C] the other nations besides: including now [Note: [Col. 0610C] “Also,” the same witnesses.] the various tribes of the Gaetuli, the many territories of the Moors, all the boundaries of the Spains, the diverse nations of the Gauls, the regions of the Britons inaccessible to the Romans but subject to Christ, and those of the Sarmatians, [Col. 0611A] the Dacians, the Germans [Note: [Col. 0611D] Irenaeus too names the Germans; on what grounds is uncertain. SEML.], the Scythians, and many further nations, provinces, and many islands unknown to us and which we are less able to enumerate.

In all these places the name of Christ, who has already come, reigns, since before him the gates of all cities have been opened and none are closed to him; before him iron bars have been shattered and bronze gates opened [Note: [Col. 0611C] “Broken,” Jun.] (Isa. 45:1–2). Although these things are also to be understood spiritually, in that the hearts of individuals, besieged by the devil in various ways, have been unlocked by faith in Christ, they have nevertheless plainly been fulfilled, since in all these places a people bearing Christ’s name dwells [Note: [Col. 0611C] “A Christian people dwells,” Paris.].

For who could have reigned over all [Note: [Col. 0611C] Rhen. and Obert. add “nations”] except Christ, the Son of God, who was announced as destined to reign over all nations [Note: [Col. 0611C] “Nations” is absent from Rhen. and Obert.] forever (Ps. [Col. 0611B] 46:2, 9; 85:9; Isa. 2:4; 49:6)?

For although Solomon reigned, he reigned only within the boundaries of Judah: the boundaries of his kingdom are marked from Beersheba to Dan.

[Note: [Col. 0611D] “The boundaries of his kingdom are marked.” In 1 Kings 4:21 we read: “Solomon, moreover, had under his dominion all the kingdoms from the River to the land of the Philistines and as far as the land of Egypt.” And in 2 Chronicles 9:26: “He [Solomon] also exercised authority over all the kings from the River Euphrates to the land of the Philistines and as far as the boundaries of Egypt.” Perhaps the Author read the boundaries of Solomon’s empire in 1 Kings 4:25, which reads: “And Judah and Israel lived without any fear, each man beneath his vine and beneath his fig tree, from Dan to Beersheba, all the days of Solomon.” EDD.]

Although Darius reigned over the Babylonians and Parthians, [Note: [Col. 0611C] “He did not have authority any farther; he did not have authority beyond the boundaries of his kingdom,” Rhen., Obert.] he did not have authority over all nations. If Pharaoh reigned over the Egyptians—or whoever succeeded him in the inheritance of the kingdom—his royal dominion was confined only to that place. Likewise Nebuchadnezzar, together with his subordinate kings, had the boundaries of his kingdom from India to Ethiopia. Likewise Alexander the Macedonian never held all Asia and the other regions after he had conquered them. Likewise the Germans are still not permitted to cross their own [Note: [Col. 0611C] “Their own” is absent from Rhen. and Obert.] frontiers. The Britons [Note: [Col. 0611C] “The Britains . . . are enclosed,” Rhen., Obert.] are enclosed within the circuit of their ocean. The nation of the Moors and the barbarous tribes of the Gaetuli are blockaded by the Romans, lest they cross the boundaries of their own territories [Note: [Col. 0612C] “Religions,” Rhen.]. [Col. 0611C]

What shall I say of the Romans, who defend their empire with the garrisons of their legions and cannot extend the strength of their kingdom beyond those nations? But Christ’s kingdom and name extend everywhere, are believed everywhere, are worshiped by all the nations enumerated above; [Col. 0612A] everywhere he reigns, everywhere he is adored, and he is bestowed equally upon everyone everywhere. With him a king enjoys no greater favor; no barbarian has lesser joy [Note: [Col. 0612C] “Authority,” Obert., Seml.]; no one’s deserts are distinguished according to rank or birth. He is equal to all, king to all, judge to all, and God and Lord to all. Nor should you doubt what we assert [Note: [Col. 0612C] “You assert,” Rhen.], since we see it happening.
