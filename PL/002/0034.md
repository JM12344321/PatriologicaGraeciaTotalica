Let us therefore see whether that sword is another sort of sword, since its action is of another sort. For the apostle John also [Note: [Col. 0340D] “For John the apostle also, in the Apocalypse.” This passage likewise argues against those who deny that the apostle John was the author of the Apocalypse. Recognize the spoils also as figurative, since you have learned that his weapons too are allegorical. ## CHAPTER XV

Thus far concerning the question of the flesh and, through it, of the birth, and for the moment concerning the single name Emmanuel. But what will the opposing party answer concerning the other names, and first of all concerning *Christ*? If among you the name *Christ* is common, just as the name *God* is, so that, just as the Christ may suitably be called the Son of either God, so either Father may be called Lord, then reason will certainly [Col. 0341C] oppose this argument. For the name *God*, being as it were natural to divinity, may be shared by all to whom divinity is claimed, even by idols, as the Apostle says (1 Cor. 8:5): “For there are those who are called gods, whether in heaven or on earth.” But the name *Christ*, arising not from nature but from a dispensation, becomes the property of him by whom that dispensation is found to have been arranged. Nor is it subject to being shared with another god, especially a rival god who possesses his own dispensation, to which he will also owe distinctive names.

For what sort of thing is this—that those who have devised the different dispensations of two gods admit a partnership of names amid the discord of their dispensations? No stronger proof of two rival gods could be established than if a diversity of names were also found in their dispensations. [Col. 0341D] For no condition of differences is marked except by the distinctive qualities [Col. 0342A] of appellations. If these should ever fail, then [Note: [Col. 0342D] *Now*, Rhen., Seml., Oberth.] the Greek catachresis (*κατάχρησις*, misuse) comes to the rescue by making improper use of what belongs to another. But in God, I think, nothing ought to fail, nor ought his dispensations to be furnished from another’s property.

Who is this god who claims even the names of his Son from the Creator? I do not say merely foreign names, but ancient and commonplace ones, which for that very reason would not befit a new and unknown god. How, finally, does he teach that a new patch must not be sewn onto the Old Testament [Note: [Col. 0341D] “A new patch must not be sewn onto the Old Testament.” He has the same statement in *On Prayer*, chapter 1. Here he understands *plagula* as patches, for *plagula* is counted among articles of clothing by our Turnebus. He means that a patch must not be sewn onto the New Testament as though onto a garment; for deformity will thereby result, such as Themistius explains in Oration 1: “He says that it is never fitting to stitch things indiscriminately together, just as beggars’ outer garments are woven together from patches both new-made and worn out” (*συρράπτειν πρὸς ἕκαστα λέγει μηδὲ ἐοικὸς εἶναι τῶν προσαιτούντων αἱ ἐφεστρίδες συμπεπλεγμέναι ἐκ ῥακίων νεουργῶν ἅμα καὶ ἐκτετριμμένων*). LE PR.], nor new wine entrusted to old wineskins [Note: [Col. 0342D] “Nor new wine entrusted to old wineskins.” In the cited passage of *On Prayer* the same statement occurs in the same words. How does he sever the Gospel from the Law when he is clothed in the whole Law—namely, in the name *Christ*? Who prevented him, since he preached something different and came from elsewhere, from being called something different? Especially since he did not even assume the reality of a body precisely to avoid being believed to be the Creator’s Christ? [Col. 0342B]

Yet vainly did he refuse to appear to be the one whose name he wished to bear. Even if he had been truly embodied, he would have seemed less like the Creator’s Christ if he had not been so called. But now he rejected the substance whose name [Note: [Col. 0342D] Paris. and Fran. add *also*.] he accepted, even though the name would prove the substance. For if *Christ* means *anointed*, anointing is certainly an experience of the body. He who had no body could not possibly be anointed; he who could not possibly be anointed could in no way be called Christ. It is another matter if he aspired to the semblance of a name as well.

“But how,” he says, “could he have crept into the faith of the Jews except through a name customary and familiar among them?” You describe an inconsistent or deceitful god, pursuing a design either of distrust or of malice, in order to advance something by deception. The false prophets acted much more openly and straightforwardly when they came against the Creator in the name of their own god. But neither do I find that this design achieved its effect, since they would more readily have believed him either to be their own Christ or, rather, some impostor, than the Christ of another god, as the Gospel will prove.

## CHAPTER XVI

Now if he snatched the name *Christ* like a petty thief snatching a little basket, why did he also wish to be called Jesus, a name not so expectantly awaited among the Jews? For although we, having by God’s grace attained an understanding of his mysteries, also recognize that this name was appointed for Christ, it does not follow that the matter would have been known to the Jews, from whom wisdom had been taken away. Indeed, to the present day they await a Christ, not a Jesus; and they interpret Elijah, rather than Jesus, as the Christ. Therefore, he who came even under that name in which the Christ was not presumed could have come [Col. 0342D] under the one name that alone was presumed. [Col. 0343A]

But since he combined two names, the expected and the unexpected, both parts of his design are defeated. For if he was called *Christ* so that in the meantime he might creep in as though he were the Creator’s, *Jesus* gets in the way [Note: [Col. 0343C] *Creeps in*, Rhen., Seml., Oberth.], because Jesus was not expected in the Creator’s Christ. Or if he was called *Jesus* so that he might be regarded as belonging to another, *Christ* does not permit it, because no Christ was expected except the Creator’s. I do not know which of these can stand. Both, however, will stand in the Creator’s Christ, in whom Jesus also is found.

“How?” you ask. Here too, learn together with the Jews who share your error. When Hoshea the son of Nun was appointed as Moses’ successor, he was certainly transferred from his former name and [Note: [Col. 0343D] “He begins to be called Jesus.” He alludes to Matthew and Luke 1: “And you shall call his name Jesus,” with the interpretation of the name also added: “For he shall save his people from their sins.” This is what he says in the book *Against Praxeas*: “Jesus is his proper name, which was imposed by the angel.” PAM.] began to be called Jesus. “Certainly,” you say. We maintain first that this was a figure of what was to come. For Jesus Christ was to introduce the second people—that is, us, born in [Col. 0343B] the deserts of this age—into the land of promise flowing with milk and honey, that is, into the possession of eternal life, than which nothing is sweeter. And this was to come about not through Moses, that is, not through the discipline of the Law, but through Jesus, that is [Note: [Col. 0343C] Seml. omits *that is*.], through the grace of the Gospel, after we had been circumcised by a stone blade, that is, by Christ’s commandments [Note: [Col. 0343C] Rhen., Seml., and Oberth. omit *commandments*.]; for the Rock was Christ. Therefore that man, who was being prepared as an image of this mystery, was also inaugurated as a figure of the Lord’s name and surnamed Jesus.

Christ himself testified even then that this name was his own when he spoke to Moses. For who was speaking except the Creator’s Spirit, who is Christ? Thus, when he said by command to the people (Exod. 23:20), “Behold, I send my angel before your face, to guard you on the way and bring you into the land that I have prepared [Col. 0343C] for you. Attend to him and obey him; do not disobey him, for he will not overlook [Note: [Col. 0343C] *Will not spare*, Seml., Jun.] you, because my name is upon him,” he called him an angel because of the greatness of the mighty works he was to perform and because of the office of a prophet, namely one who announces the divine will; but he called him Jesus because of the mystery of his own future name. Again and again he confirmed his own name, which he himself had bestowed upon him, because he had commanded that from then onward he be called neither Angel nor Hoshea, but Jesus.

Therefore, if both names belong to the Creator’s Christ, so much the less does either belong to a Christ who is not the Creator’s, just as the remainder [Note: [Col. 0343D] Rhen., Seml., and Oberth. omit *not*.] of the order does not. [Col. 0344A] From this point onward, then, we must establish between us this firm and equitable preliminary rule, necessary to either side: it is settled that absolutely nothing should be common to the Christ of another god and the Creator’s Christ. For diversity must accordingly be defended by you, just as it must be opposed by us. You will not be able to prove that the Christ of another God came unless you demonstrate that he was far different from the Creator’s Christ; nor will we be able to claim him for the Creator unless we show him to be such as he is established by the Creator. We have now closed the issue concerning the names. I claim Christ for myself; I defend Jesus as mine.

## CHAPTER XVIII

[Col. 0345C]

Regarding his end, I think you plainly attempt to introduce a difference by denying that the passion of the cross was proclaimed for the Creator’s Christ, and by arguing further that it is not credible that the Creator exposed his Son to the kind of death upon which he himself had pronounced a curse: “Cursed,” he says (Deut. 21), “is everyone who hangs upon a tree.” But I defer the meaning of this curse to the treatment devoted exclusively to the proclamation of the cross, which is now [Note: [Col. 0345C] Lat. inserts *when*.] chiefly under inquiry, [Col. 0346A] because elsewhere too the proof supplied by events precedes the rationale.

## CHAPTER XIX

Come now: if you have read in David (Ps. 95:10), “The Lord has reigned from the tree,” I await what you understand by it—unless perhaps you suppose that some Jewish king who dealt in timber is signified, and not Christ, who from the time of his passion upon the tree reigned after overcoming death. For even if death reigned from Adam until Christ, why should Christ not be said to have reigned from the tree, since by dying upon the tree of the cross he abolished [Col. 0347C] the reign of death?

Isaiah likewise says (Isa. 9:6), “For a child,” he says, “has been born to us, and a Son has been given to us.” [Note: [Col. 0347D] Seml. and Jun. omit *and a Son has been given to us*.] What would be new in this, if he were not speaking of the Son of God? [Note: [Col. 0348D] Seml. adds *and has been given to us*.] “His government was placed upon his shoulder.” What king at all bears the emblem of his power upon his shoulder, instead of a diadem upon his head, a scepter in his hand, [Col. 0348A] or some badge upon his own garment? Only the new king of the new ages, Christ Jesus, raised upon his shoulder both the power and the sublimity of his new glory—namely, the cross—so that, according to the preceding prophecy, the Lord might thereafter reign from the tree.

Jeremiah too (Jer. 11:19) intimates this tree to you, foretelling that the Jews would say, “Come, let us cast [Note: [Col. 0348D] *Let us lay upon*, Seml.; others, *let us cast*.] wood into his bread”—that is, into his body. For thus God revealed the matter even in your Gospel, calling bread his body. [Note: [Col. 0347D] “Calling bread his body.” That the reality of the Lord’s body in the Eucharist is confirmed by these words—in accordance with the words of the Evangelists and Blessed Paul, to which the Author alludes—has been noted not only by us . . . but even by the Magdeburg Centuriators themselves. At this passage they affirm that the body of Christ is truly and substantially distributed in the Eucharist; they err only when they cite this passage to prove that the bread remains together with the Lord’s body. Add to this that one may reasonably doubt whether the words “so that from this too you may now,” etc., are genuinely the Author’s, at least in their entirety, because above, in the book *Against the Jews*, they are read neither in the printed editions nor in the manuscript codices. There he already sings of his own glory: “They pierced,” he says (Ps. 21:17), “my hands and my feet”—an atrocity belonging specifically [Note: [Col. 0348D] Seml. reads *particular atrocities*; Par., *specifically atrocities*.] to the cross. Again, when he implores the Father’s aid, he says, “Save me” (ibid. 22) “from the lion’s mouth”—that is, from death—“and my humiliation from the horns of the unicorns” [Note: [Col. 0348D] Fran. reads *unicorns*; *unicorns* is also read in the book *Against the Jews*.], namely, from the points of the cross, as we showed above.

Neither David himself nor any king of the Jews suffered this cross. You must not suppose that the passion of anyone other than him [Note: [Col. 0348D] Seml. omits *that the passion of anyone other than him was prophesied*.] was prophesied—the one who alone was crucified so conspicuously by the people.

Now even if heretical obstinacy rejects and mocks all these interpretations, I shall concede to it that no cross of Christ was signified [Note: [Col. 0348D] Rhen. reads *that I signify*; Seml. omits *spoils* and the remaining intervening material as though taken from the book *Against the Jews*.] by the Creator. Yet it will not thereby prove that the one crucified was another, unless perhaps it shows that this end of its Christ was proclaimed by its own god, so that a diversity of passions—and therefore of persons as well—may be maintained from a diversity of proclamations. Otherwise, even without its own Christ having been proclaimed, much less his cross, the prophecy of death alone is sufficient for my Christ. For because the manner of death was not disclosed, it could have occurred even through the cross. Only then would it have to be assigned to another, [Col. 0349A] if it had been proclaimed concerning another—unless, indeed, he is unwilling to admit that even the death of my Christ was prophesied. In that case he should be all the more ashamed if he announces that his own Christ died, though he denies that he was born, while denying that my Christ was mortal, though he confesses him capable of birth.

Moreover, I wish to demonstrate the death, burial, and resurrection of my Christ by a single saying of Isaiah (Isa. 57:2 [printed LVIII, 2]): “His burial was taken away from the midst.” For he would not have been buried unless he had died, nor would his burial have been taken away from the midst except through resurrection. Finally, he added (Isa. 53:12): “Therefore he himself will possess many as an inheritance and will divide the spoils of many.” Who else could this be but the one who was born, as we showed above? “Because his soul was delivered over to death.” For the cause of this favor is shown: namely, as compensation for the wrong of his death. At the same time it is shown that he would obtain these things on account of his death [Note: [Col. 0349D] Jun. rejects *would obtain these things on account of his death*.]—obtain them after death, certainly, through resurrection.
