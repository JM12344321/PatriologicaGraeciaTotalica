“But how shall we assemble,” you ask, “how shall we celebrate the Lord’s solemn rites?” Just as the apostles did, surely: protected by faith, not by money. If that faith can move a mountain, much more can it move a soldier. Be secured by wisdom, not by payment.

These things, brother, perhaps seem harsh and intolerable to you. But recall that God said: “Let him who can receive it, receive it” (Matt. 19:12); that is, let him who cannot receive it depart. One who fears to suffer cannot belong to him who suffered. But one who does not fear to suffer will be perfect in love—surely the love of God. For perfect love casts out fear (1 John 4:18). And therefore many are called, but few chosen (Matt. 22:14). The question is not who is prepared to follow the broad way, but who is prepared to follow the narrow (Matt. 7:13; Luke 13:24).

And therefore the Paraclete is necessary—the guide into every truth, the encourager of every endurance. Those who have received him know neither how to flee persecution nor how to purchase immunity from it, for they possess him who will be there for us: just as he will speak during interrogation, so he will give aid during suffering.

# QUINTUS SEPTIMIUS FLORENS TERTULLIAN  
## AGAINST THE GNOSTICS: *SCORPIACE*

[Note: [Col. 0121C] *Scorpiace*. In the codex of Agobard, *Scorpiace* immediately follows the book *On the Prescription of Heretics*. In the common editions this book is entitled *Against the Gnostics: Scorpiacum, on the Good of Martyrdom*. Following the ancient manuscripts, we have called it *Scorpiace*, or *On Scorpiace*. For it is also entitled in this fashion, like *On the Prescription of Heretics* and *On the Exhortation to Chastity*, although in actual fact it is itself a *Scorpiace*, a “Prescription of Heretics,” and an “Exhortation to Chastity.” Tertullian gave this admittedly elegant little work the title *Scorpiace*, after the Greek manner, since the Greeks also speak of a “scorpion antidote” (*σκορπιακὴ ἀντίδοτος*). In the common editions of Jerome, however, the reading *Scorpiacum* occurs in the book *Against Vigilantius*: “Tertullian, a most learned man, writes against your heresy—which long ago erupted against the Church—a notable volume that he calls *Scorpiacum* by a most appropriate name, because it has poured poisons into the Church’s body through a curved wound.” RIG.] (C)

## SUBJECT

[Col. 0121A]

He attacks the Gnostics, who rejected martyrdom, and proves its usefulness and necessity. This book is called *Scorpiace* or *Scorpiacum* because they stealthily deceived the more simple-minded after the manner of scorpions, saying that God does not thirst for the blood of Christians and that Christ does not expect salvation from our death. And now the present state of affairs is the very height of the heat, the dog days of persecution themselves—at the instigation, of course, of the dog-headed one. [Note: [Col. 0124D] “At the instigation, of course, of the dog-headed one”: the devil, who torments the human race with canine envy. RIG.]

Fire has proved some to be Christians, the sword others, and beasts still others. [Col. 0125A] Some, meanwhile, after tasting martyrdom under clubs and claws, hunger for it in prison.

[Note: [Col. 0125C] Rhen. and Seml. read “after tasting martyrdom.”]

[Note: [Col. 0125C] “After tasting martyrdom under clubs and claws, they hunger for it in prison.” That is, they long to complete the martyrdoms that they have only tasted, having been beaten and torn by clubs or claws, as though merely grazed upon the outermost skin. RIG.]

We ourselves, like hares—the appointed quarry—are besieged from afar, while the heretics range about as usual. And so the season has warned me to compound with my pen an antidote readily available against our own little beasts. [Note: [Col. 0125C] Seml. reads: “a *Scorpiacum* against our native little beasts.”]

[Note: [Col. 0125D] “To compound with my pen an antidote readily available against our own little beasts.” We have restored the text by following traces in the exemplar such as these: “Against our native little beasts ... to temper my pen into an antidote for healing.” By “our own little beasts” he means the exceedingly dangerous little heretical scorpions born from those who are not true Christians. Of them he said above, “From a small thing a great evil,” and “a bitter smallness.” Among the serpent kind, scorpions are small and moderate in size; and Tertullian is fond of calling a small thing “moderate.” RIG.]

You who read it have drunk it. The potion is not bitter. [Note: [Col. 0125C] Rhen. and Seml. read “Nor is the potion bitter”; Latinus reads, “For the potion is not bitter.”] If the Lord’s utterances are sweeter than honey and the honeycomb (Ps. 19:10), its ingredients come from them. If God’s promise flows with milk and honey, the things that lead there have this taste.

But woe to those who turn sweetness into bitterness and light into darkness (Isa. 5:20). For those who oppose martyrdom likewise transform salvation into destruction, turning sweetness into bitterness just as they turn light into darkness; and thus, by preferring this most wretched life [Col. 0125B] to that most blessed one, they substitute bitterness for sweetness just as they substitute darkness for light.
