## Source locus 1

“You, and all this people.” He promised to give the things that he had previously said to those who keep the law; for [he promised them] not to Jesus son of Nun. For the Israelites, not having kept [it], did not receive [them], as what follows makes clear.

## Source locus 2

“Every place upon which you may set foot.” Those at Ai stood against [them] because of Achar’s lawlessness. And in another way: because, although God handed the nations over into their hands, they did not destroy [them] utterly, and they became thorns for them.

<!-- source-locus: 3 -->
## Source locus 3

“And they entered into the house of a prostitute woman, [whose] name [was] Rahab.” The spies entrust their own salvation to a prostitute—and very reasonably. For from the things which the prostitute says, she is shown [to be] alone faithful and fearing God; for which reason her kindred too is saved through her.

<!-- source-locus: 4 -->
## Source locus 4

Of the same [author]. Rahab is interpreted “broadening,” being a symbol of the Church from the nations, which says: “The place is narrow for me; make me a place, that I may dwell.”

<!-- source-locus: 5 -->
## Source locus 5

Of the same [author]. She is saved not from the reception alone, but beforehand from faith and from her disposition toward God.

<!-- source-locus: 6 -->
## Source locus 6

“And she hid them among the stalks of flax.” She hid them with the woody stalks of the flax which she had upon the roof, preparing to work the fine linen for them, since they were worthy of a high-priestly garment. For the stalk of flax produces the fine linen from which the priestly garment is made.

<!-- source-locus: 7 -->
## Source locus 7

“And she let them down through the window.” It must be known that her house was in the wall. The new things are sisters of the old: for the case of Paul is similar, when they let him down in a basket through the wall.

<!-- source-locus: 8 -->
## Source locus 8

Of the same author. Secondly, the Apostle was saved from his enemies in the same manner, as he says in his Second Epistle to the Corinthians: “And through a window, in a basket, I was let down through the wall, and escaped.” Again, in the Acts: “They were watching the gates also by day and by night, in order that they might kill him; but his disciples, taking [him] by night, let him down by the wall, lowering [him] in a basket.”

<!-- source-locus: 9 -->
## Source locus 9

“And they remained there three days.” Not because the journey was of three days—for the Jordan is very near Jericho—but because of the careful search for the spies. “For,” he says, “the pursuers searched all the roads and did not find [them].”

## Source locus 10

“Four times ten thousand, well-girded.” It should be noted that the four, whether of units, or of hundreds, or of some other greater number, is used for affliction.

## Source locus 11

“Make for yourself stone knives from sharp-cut rock.” One must inquire why those begotten in the wilderness were not circumcised; or why, on the Sabbath, someone who gathered wood in Moses’ time became stoned by God’s command. Yet so great a multitude remaining uncircumcised in the wilderness was not even blamed, although God had declared that the soul not circumcised on the eighth day should be destroyed, while he had no longer decreed this concerning those transgressing the Sabbath. Already, then, even from this it is clear that circumcision and Sabbath are not good in themselves, but [were good] because they were useful for a time.

And it must be known that, for the sake of a mark, he wished [them] to be recognized by circumcision, and [not] to appear to fall away from faith; as irrational sheep, by a branding-iron—even if one should stray—has an easy finding; so he wished the Jews, mingling with the nations, to be manifest from circumcision. And proof of this is that in Egypt they were circumcised, but in the wilderness, because they were alone and separated from Egyptians and from the other nations in Palestine, they remained uncircumcised. But when, having crossed the Jordan, they were about to be in the midst of the nations, they were again commanded to be circumcised. Therefore he said “a second [time],” because it was not eight-day-olds who were being circumcised, but those already grown into men, and some inclining toward old age.

But the stone knife signifies to us the Lord who gave us the intelligible circumcision, the [circumcision] unto mind and heart. For this reason the Apostle too spoke to us of the circumcision of Christ, not in the stripping off of our flesh.

*The source’s `ἀμροτόμου` is an evident dropped-kappa form of `ἀκροτόμου`, the reading in the Joshua 5:2 lemma and upstream PG text; it is rendered “sharp-cut.” The sheep-and-branding comparison remains syntactically disturbed and is not silently repaired.*

## Source locus 12

“I am commander-in-chief of the Lord’s host; now I have come.” One must remember that when they made the calf, God says to Moses: “Lead up this people; for I certainly will not go up with [you], because the people is stiff-necked. And behold, I shall send my angel before your face. Therefore take heed to yourself, and do not disobey.” But Moses says to him: “If you do not go up with us, do not lead me up from here.” And, having said this, he persuaded [him]; and he was with the people until Moses’ end.

But after this man’s death, he appears and speaks with Jesus son of Nun. After the charge of the people was handed over to Moses’ disciple, he then gives him over to the commander-in-chief, Michael. This one was ruler of the people, as another angel appears saying in Daniel.

<!-- source-locus: 13 -->
## Source locus 13

“Going and sounding trumpets.” The trumpets themselves are a symbol of the things on the last day. “For the Lord himself,” he says, “with a command, with an archangel’s voice, and with God’s trumpet, will descend from heaven.” And the priests’ encircling the city for six days would be a sign that, throughout the six days of the present age, all the saints sound the trumpet and proclaim forgiveness of sins to those wishing to repent.

<!-- source-locus: 14 -->
## Source locus 14

“And Jesus said to the sons of Israel, ‘Cry out!’” The cry, however, is likely a glorification in words and a voice of praise toward God, fighting against the error of the Jerichoites and winning the extraordinary victory, full of wonder.

<!-- source-locus: 15 -->
## Source locus 15

“Thus Ozan did.” From this it is shown that it was not Jesus son of Nun, but some other [writer], after many generations, who wrote these things. Understood truly, this is not contrary to what we said earlier. For Jesus wrote the things that happened in his own time, or were revealed [to him], just as Moses [wrote] the things concerning the times of his own life.

But after this, when the temple had already been built by wise Solomon, and the people had bowed down into great impiety, and God had left the temple deserted—so that Deuteronomy was scarcely found, thrown down as it happened—therefore, after the people had undergone the Babylonian captivity, Ezra, being most learned in the law and reciting the whole Old Testament from memory, wrote the law and certain other things that had happened and been revealed. These things were prophesied long ago, but after this came to pass. Such, surely, is also the thing we have now mentioned.

<!-- source-locus: 16 -->
## Source locus 16

“Even today I am strong, as when Moses sent me.” Therefore the saint is strong in both kinds of words. For he is strong in old and in new mysteries. This is what it is to be strong in relation to Moses and in relation to Jesus.
