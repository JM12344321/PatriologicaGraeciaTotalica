Our author, *On Idolatry* IX: “We need not even speak of astrologers. But because someone has challenged us on the subject in these days, defending his perseverance in that profession, I shall say a few words. I do not allege that he honors idols, whose names he has inscribed upon heaven and to whom he has assigned all God’s power; or that people consequently suppose there is no need to seek God, presuming that we are driven by the unchangeable judgment of the stars,” etc. In the same place he says, following Suetonius, that astrologers had once been expelled from the city and from Italy.

Valerius Maximus I.4: “When Marcus Popillius Laenas and Gnaeus Calpurnius were consuls, Gaius Cornelius Hispallus, praetor for foreigners, ordered by edict that the Chaldaeans depart from the city and from Italy within ten days, because by their deceitful interpretation of the stars they cast a profitable fog over frivolous and foolish minds through their falsehoods.”

I have thought it proper to record the verses of that outstanding ancient theologian Prosper of Aquitaine concerning this vanity. They come from the golden poem *On the Providence of God*:

> But where does your headlong course carry you? Do you wish to be good  
> Without labor of your own? Do you believe that this can happen  
> If the stars of your birth are changed—the stars whose course,  
> You say, drives you to wickedness? Why seek vain refuges  
> For ancient error in the Chaldaeans’ stars?  
> Although with anxious care you approach the heavenly things  
> And search deeply into the revealed causes of things,  
> One does not refuse to know God more fully [the syntax of the supplied Latin is uncertain]: what nature  
> He gave to each element, by what law  
> He commanded the sea to move, what power he sowed in the tender breezes,  
> And for what motions he created the starry fires.  
> Since he is the pure source of justice and goodness,  
> Did he prescribe a pitiless law with unjust commands,  
> If he had first bound human conduct to the stars?  
> For these things oppose and contradict one another utterly:  
> To demand innocent actions, to punish offenses,  
> And to call everyone to the same promises,  
> [Col. 0269A] Yet on the other hand to fasten upon those born a violent star  
> Which yields neither to human will nor power, while from above  
> Virtue and error alike lead everyone unwittingly.  
> Therefore either the fires of heaven have no authority over us,  
> Or, if they possess some part of us, they can lose it.
