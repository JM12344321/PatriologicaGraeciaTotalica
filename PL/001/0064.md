### 5

As for what you therefore say—that Christians are utterly wicked and disgraceful in avarice, luxury, and depravity—we shall not deny that some are so. Even this is sufficient testimony to our name: if not all are such, nor even the majority. In a body, however whole or pure you may wish it to be, some mole must sprout, or a little wart rise, or a freckle stain it. No refinement so thoroughly purges even the serenity of heaven that it is not marked by the wisp of some little cloud. A small blemish seen upon the forehead, upon some particular part, serves as an example of how the whole is clean: [Col. 0565B] the greater portion of goodness uses a little evil as testimony to itself.

Yet when you prove that some of our people are wicked, by that very fact you prove that they are not Christians. Ask to which sect the wickedness is to be assigned. In your own conversations, whenever you speak against us, you say: “Why is that man a cheat, if Christians are self-restrained? Why is he cruel, if they are compassionate?” To such a degree you bear witness that such men are not Christians, when you retort by asking why those who are called Christians are such men. There is a great distance between the charge and the name, between opinion and truth. For names too have been instituted in such a way that they have their own boundaries between being called something and actually being it. How many, after all, are called philosophers, yet do not fulfill the law of philosophy? All bear a name derived from their professions. Let those who disgrace the truth with the surface of a word deceive the name by the excellence claimed for their profession. Those who [Col. 0565C] are called something are not immediately what they are called; because they are not what they are called, they are called it in vain and deceive those who subject the reality to the name, whereas the standing of the name ought to accord with the reality.

And yet people of this kind neither assemble nor share fellowship with us, having become yours again through their offenses; for we do not mingle even with those whom your violence and cruelty have compelled to deny the faith. Unwilling deserters of the discipline would, of course, be more easily retained among us than willing ones. In any case, you call those people Christians without cause whom Christians themselves deny—Christians who do not know how to deny themselves [Note: [Col. 0565D] **Who do not know how to deny themselves.** For they were prepared to die for one another, for their brothers; and the pagans marveled at this steadfastness of mutual love. Hence the words in the *Apologeticum*: “See,” they say, “how they love one another, and how they are prepared to die for one another.” **RIG.**].

### 6

Whenever your conscience is constrained and hemmed in by these propositions and by our answers, which truth supplies from its own resources, it becomes the silent witness of its own ignorance; and in your agitation you take refuge at a certain little altar, [Col. 0565D] that is, in the authority of the laws: namely, that they surely would not punish [Col. 0566A] this sect unless its deserts had been established in the judgment of the lawgivers.

What, then, prevented those deserts from being established in like manner before the executors of the laws, since, in the case of all other crimes that are similarly forbidden and punished by the laws, the penalty is suspended unless an inquiry is first made? For example, the law identifies a murderer or an adulterer, yet the circumstances of the offense are still investigated, even though the general nature of the deed is known to everyone. The laws punish a Christian. If there is some deed committed by a Christian, it ought to be brought to light; no law forbids investigation. Indeed, investigation acts in the interests of the laws. For how will you observe a law by avoiding what the law forbids, if the diligence needed for avoidance is taken away through your failure to recognize what you are to observe?

No law owes the consciousness of its own justice to itself, but to those from whom it seeks obedience. Besides, a law is suspect if it does not wish to have itself proved. Thus the laws are rightly regarded as just against Christians, and as worthy of reverence and observance, only so long as what they persecute remains unknown. Once they are understood, they are rightly found to be utterly unjust and are spat upon, together with their swords, crosses, and lions. An unjust law is entitled to no honor. Yet I suppose the injustice of certain laws is admitted, since every day you moderate their harshness and viciousness by new legal opinions and enactments.

What of the fact that its condition is such that it persists only while it lies? ### 10

Let the gods therefore be ashamed to be defended by a human being. Pour forth now every poison; hurl every weapon of slander against this name. Henceforth I shall not cease to repel them. Afterward, however, they will be blunted by an exposition of our entire discipline. For now I shall tear those very charges from our body and turn them back upon you; I shall show those same wounds of crime sunk into you, so that you may fall by your own swords and javelins [Note: [Col. 0572C] Rigault: *admenta*, that is, javelins.].

First of all, [Col. 0572B] consider again and again whether we share with you the very charge you direct against us as a general accusation: separation from the institutions of the ancestors. For behold, I recognize that antiquity has been corrupted in you throughout every aspect of life and discipline—indeed, not merely corrupted but destroyed. As for the laws, it has already been said above that day by day you bury them beneath new legal opinions and enactments. As for the remaining ordering of human conduct, it plainly lies open how far you have changed from your ancestors in mode of life, dress, equipment, food itself, and even speech itself.

You reject what is former as rancid. Antiquity is everywhere excluded from business and public duties; your own authority has cast down the entire authority of your ancestors. To be sure—and this is more disgraceful for you—you are always praising ancient customs, yet you nonetheless reject them. By what perversity [text lost] ought the customs of your ancestors to be proved to remain among you, when you reject the very things you approve?

But [Col. 0572C] I shall also show that the very practice you appear most faithfully to preserve and defend as handed down from your ancestors—the matter in which above all you arraign us as transgressors, and from which all hatred of the Christian name is inflamed, namely the worship of the gods—is destroyed and despised by you just as much as by us. Or rather, not just as much: for there is no reason for us to be regarded as despisers of the gods, because no one despises what he knows does not exist at all. What exists can be despised; what is nothing suffers nothing. Therefore, if gods exist for anyone, they must suffer contempt from those people. You are burdened all the more heavily because you believe they exist and yet despise them, worship and spurn them, honor and provoke them!

This may be recognized even from the following consideration. First, since different people among you worship different gods, you necessarily despise those whom you do not worship. [Col. 0573A] Preference for one cannot exist without insult to another, nor can any choice be constituted without [Note: [Col. 0573C] From *Apologeticum* 13, correct to “without rejection.”] rejection. Whoever accepts one from among many has despised the one he does not accept. But so many gods, and such great ones, cannot be worshiped by everyone. Thus you despised them from the very beginning, since you were evidently unafraid to establish matters so that they could not all be worshiped.

Yet those wisest and most prudent ancestors, whose institutions you claim not to renounce—especially as embodied in your gods—are themselves also discovered to be impious. I am lying if they did not once decree that no emperor should dedicate a temple he had vowed in battle before the senate had approved it, as happened to Marcus Aemilius, who had vowed one to the god Alburnus. For it is surely an utterly impious—indeed, an utterly insulting—act to place [Col. 0573B] the honor of divinity at the discretion and pleasure of human judgment, so that a god is not a god unless the senate has permitted him to be one.
