The chief crime of the human race, the supreme guilt of the age, the entire ground of judgment, is idolatry. For although each offense retains its own particular character, although each is also assigned to judgment under its own name [Note: [Col. 0663D] “Each under its name,” Junius], it is nevertheless entered in the account under the crime of idolatry. Set aside the titles; examine the deeds [Note: [Col. 0663D] “Examine the deed,” Junius, Latinus]: the idolater is also a murderer. Do you ask whom he has killed? If it adds anything to the fullness of the indictment, not a stranger or an enemy, but himself. By what treachery? By his own error. With what weapon? An offense against God. With how many blows? As many as his acts of idolatry. Whoever denies that the idolater has perished will deny that the idolater has committed murder. Accordingly [Note: [Col. 0663D] “Likewise,” Paris], you may recognize adultery [Col. 0663C] and fornication in the same man: for he who serves false gods is undoubtedly an adulterer against the truth, because every falsehood is an adulteration. Thus he is also plunged into [Note: [Col. 0663D] “Into” is absent from Rigault] fornication. For who can minister to unclean spirits without going about defiled and debauched (Ezek. 23)? Indeed, Holy Scripture uses the term “fornication” in its reproach of idolatry.

The nature of fraud, I suppose, is that someone takes what belongs to another or denies another what is owed to him; and certainly fraud committed against a human being bears the name of a most serious crime. But idolatry defrauds God, denying Him His own honors and conferring them upon others, so that it joins insult to fraud. And if fraud, fornication, and adultery alike bring death, then in these respects too [Note: [Col. 0663D] “Now in these respects alike,” Junius] idolatry is not absolved from the charge of murder.

After crimes of this kind, so destructive, [Col. 0663D] so devouring of salvation, all the others as well, though in some fashion arranged separately in their own right, display their character in idolatry. In it are also the lusts of the world. For what solemn observance of idolatry is without ostentatious display in its ceremonial and adornment? In it are wantonness and drunkenness, since such solemnities are attended chiefly for the sake of feasting, gluttony, and lust [Note: [Col. 0663D] “Are attended,” Rigault]. In it is injustice. For what is more unjust than that which does not know the Father of justice? In it also is vanity, since its whole rationale is vain; in it is falsehood, [Col. 0664A] since its whole substance is false. Thus it comes about that everything is found in idolatry, and idolatry in everything.

But there is also another consideration: since all offenses savor of opposition to the Lord, and nothing that savors of opposition to the Lord [Note: [Col. 0664D] “God—God,” Rigault] fails to be assigned to demons and unclean spirits, to whom idols are enslaved, whoever commits an offense undoubtedly commits idolatry; for he does what pertains to the owners of idols.

## CHAPTER II

But let all the names of crimes withdraw into the particular characteristics of their deeds; let idolatry remain what it is in itself. A name so hostile to God is sufficient unto itself, as is so abundant a substance of crime, which extends so many branches and spreads forth [Note: [Col. 0664D] “Diffuses,” Wouwer manuscript] so many veins, that this subject has now especially been undertaken: in how many ways we must guard against the breadth of idolatry. For in manifold ways it overthrows the servants of God, not only when unrecognized but also when willfully disregarded.

Most people suppose that idolatry should be understood simply in these ways alone: if someone burns incense, sacrifices, presents a sacred feast, or binds himself to some sacred rites or priesthoods. This is as though someone supposed that adultery should be reckoned only in kisses, embraces, and actual carnal intercourse, or that murder should be accounted only in the shedding of blood and the taking of life (Matt. 5:22). Yet we know how much more broadly the Lord defines these things: when He designates adultery even in lust, if anyone has fixed his eye lasciviously and stirred his soul impurely (ibid.); when He judges murder to exist even in a word of curse or abuse [Note: [Col. 0664D] “Indicates,” Junius], and in every [Col. 0664C] impulse of anger, and in neglect of charity toward a brother. Thus John teaches that whoever hates his brother is a murderer (1 John 3:15).

Otherwise both the devil’s ingenuity in malice and that of the Lord God [Note: [Col. 0664D] “The Lord,” Junius] in discipline, by which He arms us against the heights [Note: [Col. 0664D] “Breadths,” Wouwer manuscript, Rigault] of the devil (Matt. 5; cf. Rev. 2:24), would consist in something trivial, if we were judged only for those offenses which even the nations have decreed should be punished [Note: [Col. 0664D] “If for these,” others]. How will our righteousness abound beyond that of the scribes and Pharisees, as the Lord prescribed, unless we have perceived the abundance of its opposite, that is, injustice? But if idolatry is the head of injustice, our first task is to fortify ourselves against the abundance of idolatry by recognizing it not only in its manifest forms.

## CHAPTER III

[Col. 0664D]

At one time in the past there was no idol. Before the artisans of this monstrosity had burst forth, there were only temples and empty shrines, just as traces of antiquity remain in certain places to the present day. Idolatry was nevertheless practiced—not under that name, but in that deed. For even today it can be practiced outside a temple and without an idol.

But when the devil brought into the world the makers of statues and images and representations of every kind, [Col. 0665A] that primitive business of human ruin acquired both its name from idols and its further development. From that time onward, every art that in any way produces an idol became the head of idolatry. For it makes no difference whether a modeler shapes it, an engraver carves it, or an embroiderer works it out in thread; nor does its material matter—whether an idol is formed of plaster, pigments, stone, bronze, silver, or thread. For since idolatry can be committed even without an idol, it certainly makes no difference, when an idol is present, what sort it is, of what material or of what form [Note: [Col. 0665C] “Or of what form,” Junius], lest anyone suppose that only something consecrated in human form is to be regarded as an idol.

For this purpose an explanation of the word is necessary: [non-Latin text omitted] in Greek signifies “form”; from it, by diminution [Note: [Col. 0665C] “Diminution,” Junius, from the manuscript], [non-Latin text omitted] was derived, and likewise furnished us with the term “little form.” Therefore every form or little form demands to be called an idol. Hence [Col. 0665B] all servitude and service surrounding every idol is idolatry. Hence also every maker of an idol is guilty of the same single crime—unless the people committed only a little idolatry because they consecrated for themselves the image of a calf and not of a human being.
