# Second Part of the Works of Quintus Septimius Florens Tertullian, Containing the Books Written by the Author During His Montanist Period

[Col. 1389D] The book **On Prescriptions against Heretics** is placed first. col. 10.

Preface. Ibid.

Outline of the first part. 11

First proposition. That heresies must exist and exercise very great power, and that they accomplish much in the overthrow of souls. Chapters I–III. 12–15

Proposition II. That heresies must be avoided, since they are worse than dissensions and persecutions. Chapters IV–V. 16–17

Proposition III. That heresy is a choice (*haeresis/electio*), and that from the very matter in which the choice is made comes condemnation. Chapter VI. 18

Proposition IV. That all heresies flow from one or the other of two sources: either secular wisdom or an itching curiosity. Chapters VII–XII. 19–25

Proposition V. That the rule of faith must be preserved intact and must never be torn apart in controversy. Chapters XIII–XIV. 26–27

Outline of the second part.

First prescription. That heretics are not to be heard when they seek to debate from Scripture. Chapters XV–XIX. 28–30

Prescription II. That the apostles received sound teaching from Christ himself, and the churches from the apostles, to be handed down to posterity, [Col. 1390D] and that no teaching is to be received from any other source. Chapters XX–XXVI, cols. 31–39.

Prescription III. That the Church’s doctrine is true precisely because it is one, while that of the heretics is false because it is diverse. Chapter XXVIII. 40

Prescription IV. That what was handed down earlier is true and divine. Chapters XXIX–XXXI. 41–44

Prescription V. That the uninterrupted succession of bishops from the apostles is a firm testimony to the true Church. Chapter XXXII. 44–45

Prescription VI. That our teaching is true, since it is certainly not condemned by the apostles but, on the contrary, defended by them; while every other teaching is false, since it is foreign or unknown to the apostles. Chapters XXXIII–XXXV. 45–48

Prescription VII. That, in order to discover the truth, the apostolic churches must be consulted, and above all that blessed Church upon which the apostles poured out their entire doctrine together with their blood. Chapters XXXVI–XXXVII. 48–51

Prescription VIII. That heretics possess no right whatever to the sacred Scriptures. Chapters XXXVIII–XL, cols. 51–55.

Prescription IX. That no truth is found where the heretics’ conduct is perverse; but where there is fear of God, there are [Col. 1391A] honorable gravity, considered fellowship, advancement by merit, religious submission, devoted attendance, modest bearing, a united Church, and all that belongs to God. Chapters XLI–XLV. 55–60

Catalogue of heretics: Simon Magus, Menander, Saturninus, Basilides, Nicolaus. Chapter XLVI. 61

The Ophites, Cainites, and Sethians. Chapter XLVII. 64

Carpocrates, Cerinthus, Hebion. Chapter XLVIII. 66

Valentinus, Ptolemy, Secundus, Heracleon. Chapter XLIX. 67

Marcus, Colarbasus. Chapter L. 70

Cerdo, Marcion, Lucanus, Apelles. Chapter LI. Ibid.

Blastus, both men named Theodotus, Praxeas, Victorinus. Chapter LII. 72
