In the grain a tree lies hidden; and unless the grain, buried in the earth,

Decays, it does not yield the fruits that adorn the tree.

Now liquid waters are hardened; beneath the creaking frost

They become rocks, and will remain so forever unless a mighty power

[Col. 1068C] Dissolves them by bringing in a gently breathing warmth.

Within the slender body of the little vine lies hidden a mighty cluster;

If you seek it, it is not there; when God wills, it is seen to exist.

The leaves on trees, the rose on thorns, the shoots in the fields

Die and disappear, and once again rise alive.

God restores these things before man’s eyes after bringing them back,

For whose sake the bountiful First One richly made all things.

All things fall naked; he clothes each in its own body:

Why should he not likewise recall man alone to himself,

Upon whom he lavished such honors and whom he made above all things?

[Col. 1069A] Therefore flesh and blood are denied to be worthy of God’s kingdom [Note: [Col. 1069B] *Therefore flesh and blood are denied to be worthy of God’s kingdom*, etc. He gives an excellent interpretation of the Apostle’s words, 1 Cor. 15: “Flesh and blood cannot possess the kingdom of God”; this is discussed more fully above, *On the Resurrection of the Flesh*, chs. 46, 49, and 51. We have presently corrected *Carnali sermone putent*, deriving *putent* from *putere*, “to be putrid,” in place of *putant*; and a little later, once again, *et debito mortis*, in place of *debita*. Likewise *humanum*—that is, the human race—[Col. 1070B] *rursus debere renasci*, in place of *humano*.],

As though Paul had been speaking specifically about the flesh.

He indeed taught great things, but empty hearts

Stink with carnal discourse; for by the name

Of blood and flesh he meant deeds belonging to the former state,

Calling to mind the words of the great heavenly Master,

Who gave those begotten of himself through water his own name as an honor,

And poured out the pledge of his own Spirit,

So that those who had been redeemed by his power

Might also, when renewed, receive the honor of his name.

Therefore, because he denied the heavenly kingdom to peoples

Not yet reborn from his own font, covered in their ancient stains

And liable to death through the old cause,

Saying that the human race must be born again—

[Col. 1069B] “What is born of flesh is flesh; what is born of the Spirit is life”—

[Col. 1070A] And because glory, transforming the new shoots of the ancient root,

Declared that the washed body should no longer be called “flesh,”

Paul follows this teaching; in this sense he spoke concerning flesh.

Finally, he said that this frail condition, this mortal form,

Must be clothed over with a garment [Note: [Col. 1070B] Finally he said: “The flesh must be clothed over,” etc. Concerning this passage of the Apostle, 2 Cor. 5, see also the book cited above, more fully, chs. 41, 42, and 43; likewise concerning the words “in an atom, in the momentary movement of an eye,” in the same place. By “red wax,” moreover, he means reddish wax, according to his custom, and uses *glutitam* in the sense of *deglutitam*, “swallowed up.”].

Not that another body is given, but that the former body, defenseless,

Is wholly surrounded on every side by the far-reaching kingdom of God.

“In the moment of an eye’s glance it will be changed,” he says,

As the surface of red wax is overspread by a green plant

And changes its own color beneath the shining sun.

Thus the same flesh, receiving splendor from glory,

Will rejoice forever in gladness and will be free from death,

Crying aloud that the cruel enemy of the body has been conquered:

“Death has been swallowed up; Christ is the mighty victory,”

[Col. 1070B] And bearing immense praises of God to the highest stars.

## BOOK THREE: ON THE AGREEMENT OF THE FATHERS OF THE OLD AND NEW TESTAMENTS

[Col. 1069]

### SUMMARY, ALSO BY THE AUTHOR HIMSELF

The third book teaches that the people created from a freeborn mother

Have the prophets and patriarchs as their consecrated ministers.

### [NOTE]

Here one must understand “TEACHES,” or something similar. For in the third book—which we have therefore also added to the title—he treats of the agreement of the fathers of the Old and New Testaments, taking his occasion from the Apostle’s words (Gal. 4, from Isa. 54): “FOR IT IS WRITTEN: REJOICE, BARREN WOMAN WHO DOES NOT BEAR; BREAK FORTH AND CRY ALOUD, YOU WHO ARE NOT IN LABOR,” etc.; and from: “THE JERUSALEM ABOVE IS FREE, AND SHE IS OUR MOTHER.” This is what is meant by the words of the summary, “THE PEOPLE CREATED FROM A FREEBORN MOTHER.” The author has more at the beginning of Book V, but it is so obscure that it is better to discuss it only in its proper place.

### CHAPTER I

[Col. 1069C]

Now the mother who was once surnamed barren has given birth [Note: [Col. 1069D] *Now the mother who was once surnamed barren has given birth*, etc.—the Church both of the ancient Fathers and of our own time. He has rendered Gal. 4 in terms of the flesh. Likewise, when he adds, “the waters of the living fountain,” etc., he alludes to Gen. 21, where Hagar is said to have wandered in the wilderness with a skin of water placed upon her shoulders. Meanwhile, some other word appears to need substitution for *tepidis*, “tepid waters,” but it was not possible [Col. 1070D] to correct it for lack of a manuscript codex.],

And the new people, born of the free woman, now rejoices,

After the slave woman was deservedly expelled with her haughty offspring.

Ungratefully she abandoned the waters of the living fountain,

And, wandering beneath the blazing constellation, drinks from tepid waters.

Now the nations can call Abraham their father,

For they followed the Lord’s voice in a similar way,

Abandoning everything to establish themselves as pilgrims in this life.

[Col. 1070C] Rejoice, O barren woman; conceive the promised people;

Break forth and cry aloud, you who were blessed by no offspring.

Of her the Spirit once said through the prophets:

“She has begotten from many nations a single people,

Whose devout members have always suffered from the beginning.”

### CHAPTER II

To her belongs righteous Abel, shepherd and master of the flocks,

Whom the violence of a brother’s hand once slew.

To her belongs Enoch, a distinguished glory, a member of her body [Note: [Col. 1070D] *To her belongs Enoch*, etc. It is peculiar to the Author to write that Enoch recalled by his counsel the peoples who had departed from God. By anticipation he also calls “giants” the impious servants of the devil’s city, of whom the Scriptures first make mention under Noah in Gen. 6.],

Who sought by counsel to recall peoples who had departed

[Col. 1071A] From God [Note: [Col. 1071D] “From” is absent in Obert’s edition.] and were pursuing crime,

While a band of robber-refugees raged against God upon earth,

So that they might flee the cruel, sacrilegious race of giants;

He himself remained faithful in all things.

Through immense lamentation and meritorious labor he found favor,

And, translated from earth, is preserved as a pledge in great honor.

Noah was found perfect in praise and without blame [Note: [Col. 1071D] *Noah was found perfect in praise and without blame*, etc. What he relates of Noah agrees more closely with Scripture, where the words “Their days shall be one hundred and twenty years” are read. He tacitly interprets this—omitting only twenty years, perhaps because they could not be fitted into verse so conveniently—as the period left to the impious for repentance, when he says:

“He spent twice fifty years roofing the ark.”],

Righteous by God’s testimony amid an adulterous people.

For twice fifty years he roofed the ark,

Proclaiming by deed and word the destruction that was to come.

Through such merits he was rescued from the waves of death

And preserved with his offspring. Next in the line

Is Abraham, whose children you deny that you are.

He was the first, after leaving kindred, homeland, and father,

To be persuaded by God’s voice and depart into foreign realms.

[Col. 1071B] He was exalted and judged worthy of so great an honor from God [Note: [Col. 1071D] “So great honors,” Obert.],

That he became the father of nations and of believing peoples.

With the fathers stands Jacob, himself their father, who throughout

The entire span of his life foretold the most joyful age of Christ

In words, action, virtue, and labor.

Joseph follows him, without the stain of shameful youth,

Though subjected to a fabricated accusation and the harsh punishment of prison:

After his lamentations came glory, the second crown of the kingdom,

And ample power to distribute bread in famine.

So fitting, so radiant an image of Christ, the Light,

Is manifest to all who possess eyes of the mind,

That in this mirror they may see his countenance and a steadfast hope.

Judah himself is the father and source of the royal line,

From whom leaders were begotten, and from whose seed kings

Never ceased until the promised power,

Long awaited by the nations, should come.

### CHAPTER III

[Col. 1071C]

Moses, leader of the people, abandoned the royal threshold

And despised riches that flourish only briefly.

With steadfast neck he preferred to bear

The afflictions of the people, terrified by no threats,

Rather than reserve delights for himself and remit their many punishments.

For his great faith and such marvelous love,

Armed with God’s power, he performed mighty deeds:

He struck the nation with plagues and left the land;

He confounded the hard king and led the people back;

He trod upon the waves and drowned the enemies in the waters;

Through wood he made sweet the water that had always been bitter,

Clearly speaking many things concerning Christ with Christ’s people.

Light shone from his face and splendor from his countenance;

[Col. 1071D] Through a few men he poured the Law he had received throughout the world,

[Col. 1072A] Veiled images that surely represented his own labors.

He struck the rock and, at command, poured forth streams;

And he extended his hands so that by this sign he might conquer the enemy.

All things speak of Christ; through Christ all things speak.

This upright and great man rested in the peace of praise.

But Joshua, son of Nun, previously called Hoshea—

The Holy Spirit joined him to himself by name.

Then he divided the river and compelled the people to cross;

He distributed the land freely, fulfilling the ancestral promises;

He halted the sun together with the moon while conquering the enemy;

He expelled foreign nations and the offspring of giants;

He destroyed sacred groves and subdued altars and temples;

He fulfilled in spirit every lawful solemn rite:

An exemplar of Christ’s name, an image of his power.

### CHAPTER IV

[Col. 1072B]

Why should I speak individually of the people’s Judges?

If their deeds of valor were gathered into one account,

They would fill numerous volumes with the expanse of words.

Yet the arrangement of the discourse, if its body is to be completed,

Demands that the lives of a few among the many be recounted.

Among these was Gideon, leader of the host, fierce against the enemy.

Not wishing to obtain protection for the nation by his own strength,

But strengthened in faith, he sought a sign for his stirred mind,

By which he might know whether he could or could not win the war:

That the fleece laid out at night should be wet with dew,

While all the earth around it lay dry—

This meant that the victory over the enemies was gathering upon the world;

And then again, while the fleece alone remained dry [Note: [Col. 1072D] *Fuco*, Paris edition.],

That the same earth should be watered with moisture that night.

[Col. 1072C] By this sign he indeed laid low heaps of plunderers,

Entering battle for Christ’s people without a large army,

With three hundred horsemen—the number represented by the Greek letter tau, Τ [Note: [Col. 1071D] *The number represented by the Greek letter tau*, etc. His meaning is that the Greek letter tau represents the number three hundred. Besides being too well known to require proof, this has also been shown above from Tertullian, Irenaeus, and others, in *On Prescription Against Heretics*, [Col. 1072D] ch. 50, heresy 15, concerning the heresy of Marcus and the Greek alphabet. He introduces it here to prove that Gideon, in the figure of tau, the sign of the cross, laid low heaps of plunderers with three hundred horsemen. Here, for metrical reasons, he takes “horseman” to mean any soldier, for in Judges 7 one reads only of thirty men [sic]. Concerning this sign tau, see more above, Book III *Against Marcion*, ch. 23, and *Against the Jews*, ch. 11. A little later the Author uses *testamen*, “testament,” in place of *testamentum*.],

Armed with torches and horns sounded by their mouths.

The fleece was the people of sheep from the holy seed;

The earth, however, was the various nations spread throughout the world;

The Word is that which nourishes, while night is an image of death;

Tau is the sign of the cross, the horn the proclamations of life,

And the shining torches in the lamp are the burning Spirit.

This testament, plainly, was an image of power:

The savage angelic battles of death were not to be conquered

Before the unteachable people, through its own fault, had deservedly been abandoned

And the nations flourishing in faith had been received into praise.

And Deborah, a woman greatest beyond every model,

Who, sustaining the martial labor for her homeland,

[Col. 1072D] Sang beneath the trunk of a palm that the people had conquered [Note: [Col. 1072D] *Sang beneath the trunk of a palm that the people had conquered*, etc. He alludes to the Song of Deborah, in which she celebrates Sisera as struck in the temples with a nail—or, as the Septuagint has it, with a tent-peg. The Author indicates that these were formerly customarily made from palm wood. He also teaches that this was a sign of Christ’s victory upon the wood, although in rather obscure words that appear corrupt.].

[Col. 1073A] Therefore the enemies, defeated together, turned their backs.

Their leader Sisera fled; no man and no hand

Resisted him. Here Baal, a fugitive before the exemplary sign

Of Christ’s victory, was conquered by a woman with wood.

Jephthah, firm in faith, who, beneath the grievous wound of his vow,

Dared to promise God a great recompense for the war—

Because in his madness he had promised what the Lord does not desire,

The pledge dear to his heart was the first to meet him.

What no one expected suddenly fell to him by lot;

That his promise might stand, he discharged the devout obligations of a parent,

And with a violent mind fulfilled his sinful vow [Note: [Col. 1073D] *With a violent mind fulfilled his sinful vow*, etc. To the same point belong the following words:

“For his wicked deed he obtained renown, for his crime praise.”

These concern Jephthah, who, in fulfillment of the vow he had madly promised to God, sacrificed his daughter as a burnt offering. How the crime may be imputed to him, although he is nevertheless included by the Apostle (Heb. 11) in the catalogue of the righteous, is treated at length by the author of the *Questions and Answers* among the works of Blessed Justin Martyr. He says that God permitted this crime so that posterity might receive a lesson never to make vows to God without qualification; and that Jephthah’s name was inserted in the register of the righteous because he demonstrated his observance of devotion toward God through the slaughter of his daughter. Blessed Augustine has something similar in *Questions on the Book of Judges*, ch. 49, and partly in *The City of God*, Book I, ch. 21. The form *coperuit* at the end of a heroic verse also belongs to poetic license. A little later, concerning Samson, we corrected “though conquered, he redeemed his enemies by his own death,” in place of “the enemy redeemed the conquered.” For he himself, already conquered since he had been placed in bonds, slew his enemies together with himself by shaking the columns.].

Overwhelming dread, the solace of a bereaved life,

Obtained renown for his wicked deed, praise for his crime.

There were also Samson’s bodily powers, surpassing all others:

This gift of the Spirit was a power granted to his head.

[Col. 1073B] Alone on behalf of the people, with neither sword nor weapons,

But holding the jawbone of an ass, he laid low a thousand bodies.

No bonds could hold the man once bound;

But after the glory that had stirred him departed from the captive,

He fell, and, though conquered, redeemed his enemies by his own death.

### CHAPTER V

The marvelous Samuel [Note: [Col. 1074D] *The marvelous Samuel*, etc. He means the anointing of the kings Saul and David, 1 Sam. 10 and 16; he then alludes to 1 Sam. 12, concerning Samuel’s innocence, and ch. 25, concerning his death.], to whom was first given the command

To anoint kings and bestow chrism, showing men to be “anointed ones,”

Performed such praiseworthy deeds throughout the span of his life

That everywhere [Note: [Col. 1073D] “Likewise,” Obert.] even after his rest he retained prophetic authority.

David, psalmist, great king and prophet [Note: [Col. 1074D] *David, psalmist, great king and prophet*, etc. In a few words he encompasses all David’s qualities. [Col. 1075C] We have corrected the following verse concerning him:

“He sang in a subdued voice that Christ would suffer,” in place of “by whose voice he sang of himself.” Likewise, concerning Hezekiah:

“Who restored the Law after the wicked things had been removed,” in place of “forgotten”; for by “wicked things” he means what Scripture calls the high places. A little later the statement concerning Josiah is obscure:

“He burned the altars, with corpses to be consumed upon the wood,” unless “for the purpose of consuming” is understood.],

Sang in a subdued voice that Christ would suffer

What the ungrateful, lawless people willingly carried out.

God had once promised him fruit from his womb,

A holy offspring to place upon his own lofty throne.

[Col. 1073C] The steadfast faith of the Lord fulfilled everything it had promised.

Zealous Hezekiah, reformer of the indolent people,

Restored the Law after the wicked things had been removed [Note: [Col. 1073D] “Forgotten,” Venice edition.];

He was the first to command all these ancient ordinances of God.

By prayers, not by the point of iron, he ended war.

When dying, through tears he received additional years and a span of life;

By his conduct he deservedly obtained such an honor.

Josiah too was a ruler of immense zeal:

No one before or afterward acted in such a manner.

He cast down idols, destroyed impious temples,

And burned the priests with fire upon their altars;

[Col. 1073D] He dug up all the bones of the false prophets

[Col. 1074A] And burned the altars, with corpses to be consumed upon the wood.

Noble Elijah—distinguished by faith, memorable in praise—

Has not yet tasted the death that was his due,

Because he is to come again into the world.

His faith was therefore unbroken. The raging people and king,

Who had deserted the blessed service of the Lord,

He chastised as enemies with bitter blows and flames.

He closed the heavens and held back the rain within the clouds.

He showed to all that God exists, and error was exposed:

For the portions of the offering, drenched with a stream upon the altar,

Were consumed by a flame that came forcefully from heaven in answer to prayer.

As often as he willed, fire fell from on high.

He divided the river, crossed over, and made a path through trackless places;

Taken up in a chariot, he was carried into the court of paradise.

His disciple Elisha succeeded to his lot,

[Col. 1074B] And asked to receive a double portion of Elijah

For the chastisement of the people with a violent scourge.

So great and such was the love he breathed for the Lord:

After he struck the Jordan, a road was made beneath his feet for his return;

With a staff he raised the axe submerged in the river;

He transformed deadly food into life-giving nourishment;

For a second period twice as long he withheld the rains;

He cleansed leprosies and captured enemies in blindness.

After his death, when his limbs had already been laid in the tomb,

A certain man, cast there after being slaughtered by robbers,

Touched him while dead and lived again as light returned.

### CHAPTER VI

Isaiah, the bountiful prophet, to whom the fountains were opened,

Poured forth God’s word from his mouth with faith so manifest.

Through Christ the Father’s generous will was promised abundantly,

[Col. 1074C] And the way of life was attested beforehand and confirmed through him.

The people, in their madness, found him without fault yet cut him apart with wood,

And undeservedly destroyed him by a cruel death.

Holy Jeremiah, whom the power of the Eternal

Commanded and declared would be a prophet to the nations,

Recounted the people’s deeds, unworthy of praise,

And declared prophetically that the people would be taken captive

And made slaves unless they repented of their wickedness.

He bore harsh bonds, confined in a filthy [Note: [Col. 1074D] “To run,” in other editions.] prison,

And in the mud of a cistern wasting hunger consumed his limbs.

But after he proved what they had refused [Note: [Col. 1074D] “Had wished,” in other editions.] to hear,

[Col. 1074D] And enemies led the bound people away in triumph,

[Col. 1075A] At last his own right hand was scarcely freed from dreadful chains.

It is agreed that the man was not destroyed by any death or slaughter.

Faithful Ezekiel [Note: [Col. 1075C] “Star,” in other editions.], to whom the rich grace of the word

Was granted: to see the secrets of sinners,

To lament his afflicted people and pray for pardon,

To perceive the vengeance that would follow the slaughter of the saints,

The saints’ place in the kingdom and the salvation of the flesh,

And, enveloped in the Spirit, to see its course and entrances.

Hosea, Amos, Micah, Joel [Note: [Col. 1076C] “Together,” Obert.], Obadiah, Jonah [Note: [Col. 1075D] *Hosea, Amos, Micah, Joel, Obadiah, Jonah*, etc. Here too we necessarily substituted “Joel” for “together,” since he alone among the twelve Minor Prophets whom the author names would otherwise be missing.],

And Nahum, Habakkuk, Zephaniah, and Haggai,

Zechariah, who suffered violence, and Malachi, himself the messenger:

These are the Lord’s prophets, whose choir sings without end,

And who alike deserved the crown proper to their praise.

How great was Daniel! What a man! How great his power!

By their own mouths he exposed the false witnesses [Note: [Col. 1075D] *By their own mouths he exposed the false witnesses*, etc. This also confirms the story of Susanna, Daniel 13, which the Author, with the Greeks, numbers among Daniel’s earliest deeds. Concerning its authority, see more fully above, vol. II, *On the Soldier’s Crown*, ch. 4, no. 43. The following verse, however, is obscure [Col. 1076D] and perhaps corrupt:

“After all had manifestly been preserved, he rested in peace.”];

[Col. 1075B] He saved the life condemned through a fabricated charge;

He was the first to explain with his mouth the king’s secret dreams;

He foresaw Christ dissolving the limbs of kingdoms;

He was made a defendant for the Lord and became the lions’ prey;

After all had manifestly been preserved, he rested in peace.

His three companions can scarcely be celebrated with worthy praise [Note: [Col. 1076D] *His three companions*, etc. He means Hananiah, Azariah, and Mishael. Where he says, “to surrender themselves to punishments,” etc., “preferring,” or something similar, must be understood.].

Among so great a multitude, these devout men despised

The unrighteous decree of the king. Their bodies were given to the flames,

Preferring to surrender themselves to punishment for the great Name

Rather than bend their knees and spread their hands toward an image.

Now their illustrious faith, now their hope shining above all things,

Extinguished the raging fires and conquered the wicked.

Ezra, prophet, teacher of the Law, and himself a priest,

Led the captive people back when the full period had elapsed.

Though many volumes of the prophets had been consumed by fire [Note: [Col. 1076D] *Though many volumes of the prophets had been consumed by fire*, etc. Concerning the entire body of Jewish literature restored through Ezra, see above, vol. II, *On the Apparel of Women*, ch. 3, no. 33. Add that this is sufficiently indicated by the words of 1 Ezra 7: “Ezra prepared his heart to seek the Law of the Lord,” and “to Ezra the priest, the scribe of the book of the words and commandments of the Lord.”],

[Col. 1075C] Filled with the Spirit, he restored everything from his remembering mouth.

### CHAPTER VII

Great is John above all those born from seed;

We shall scarcely be able to recount his praises:

Baptizer of the flesh, manifest forerunner of the Lord,

Baptizer of Christ, himself first reborn by him,

The first of the new covenant and the last of the old,

And the first victim slain for the true Way, by which he died.

Behold Christ, God; and with him twice six young men,

All possessing one faith, one love, and one power,

[Col. 1076A] The flower of mankind, shining upon the world with Christ’s light,

Both companions and apostles, who spoke the truth.

With their ears they heard salvation; with their eyes they saw it;

With their hands they handled the body restored from death;

They continually shared food and life with him, as they themselves testify.

Paul too, the preeminently chosen apostle sent in another’s place [Note: [Col. 1076D] *Paul too, the preeminently chosen apostle*, etc. We corrected this verse to read *Hunc*, “him,” in place of *Hinc*, “hence.” For he is speaking of Christ, whom Paul saw when caught up into heaven; likewise, “sent by him.” Again, “for the Name” means the name of Christ.],

Saw him when caught up into heaven and was sent by him.

With Barnabas as his companion and with his earlier associates,

United in one covenant, he teaches everywhere among the nations

That Christ is the head whose members are the Church;

He is the body’s salvation and the members’ everlasting life.

He himself became flesh; he himself was slain for all;

He himself first rose again, the one hope of salvation.

He gave his disciples an example; all these together

Suffered various undeserved punishments for the Name.

### CHAPTER VIII

[Col. 1076B]

Such are the members that the free mother bears in her beautiful body,

Because she never abandoned the Lord’s commandments,

But grew old in his house, always most elect to the Lord,

Suffering envy and punishments for his name.

For although she was barren and not yet assured of the future,

She brought forth a people born from heavenly seed.

Though despised, she bore the sorrow inflicted by her slave woman.

The time has now come to see her who was once barren as a mother,

Possessing as heir the child born of her own freedom,

Not one like the slave woman’s child, although that woman bore a child

Conceived from heaven’s seed yet bound to herself in servile condition.

Far be it from you, with unlawful words and a reckless voice,

To call those eminent men, the burning stars of heaven,

Merely related to the people by seed or blood,

[Col. 1076C] Altogether and indiscriminately a savage slave;

Or that anyone should think the Lord’s people, which followed

The Law’s commandments, ought to be spoken of as servile in character.

Rather, this concerns the empty, mindless multitude of sinners,

Who lodged God’s promises in their hearts with wavering hope

And, conquered by the wretched sweetness of the present life,

Would through their own fault have incurred the bondage owed to their head,

If the Law had subjected the people because of sin.

For to serve God and lean upon him with one’s whole heart

Is undefiled faith; freedom is thereby willingly obtained.

[Col. 1077A] Therefore the righteous fathers and holy, blameless prophets

Sang in great numbers of the Lord’s future advent.

By faith they bear witness to the heavenly commands among the profane.

Together with them, the mighty men, filled with the glory of Christ

And made sharers in his power, fulfilled the sacred words

And strengthened faith by proving through deeds what had been foretold.

### CHAPTER IX

Their disciples, who succeeded them throughout the world,

Men forged by power and our own teachers,

Bestowed upon us honors joined to their work.

From among them Linus, a great man chosen and approved by the people,

Was placed upon this chair, on which Peter himself had sat:

Mighty Rome commanded him to be the first to sit there.

After him Cletus himself took charge of the flock in the fold.

Anacletus was appointed by lot as his successor;

[Col. 1077B] Clement follows him, well known to the apostles.

After him Evaristus directed the rule without reproach.

Alexander, the sixth, entrusted the fold to Sixtus;

When the latter had completed the period of his five-year term, he handed it

[Col. 1078A] To Telesphorus, an excellent man and faithful martyr.

After him came a partner in the rule and a sure teacher.

When the accomplice, forerunner, and author of your wickedness—

Cerdo—came to Rome, bringing new wounds,

He was exposed because he was secretly scattering

Poisonous utterances and words. Consequently, expelled from the company,

He begot this sacrilegious race by the dragon’s breath.

The Church at Rome stood firm, vigorous in devotion,

Established by Peter. His successor,

Hyginus, now received the chair as the ninth in succession.

After him came Pius, whose brother by birth was Hermas,

The angelic Shepherd, because he spoke the words entrusted to him.

Anicetus then devoutly received the office in due order.

Under him this Marcion arrived, a novel Pontic plague,

Not yet disclosing the secret crime hidden in his heart,

But speaking everywhere among the common people, treacherous in his covert art.

Yet after he began to bring forth the arrows of death,

The author of so savage a crime was deservedly cast out,

Rejected by the saints, and the astonishing monster stood revealed.

## BOOK FOUR, BY AN UNCERTAIN AUTHOR: ON MARCION’S ANTITHESES

[Col. 1077]

The fourth book likewise relates that the obscure expiatory rites

Of the old Law were a type to which the true victim conformed,

Long awaited by the devout together with the holy seed.

### [NOTE]

By these words, which are otherwise sufficiently obscure, he indicates that the expiatory rites—that is, the sacrifices of the Old Law—were a type of the true sacrifice, Christ. Since by this explanation he refutes Marcion’s *Antitheses*, which in this book he calls “rival propositions,” we have added to the title of this book: “ON MARCION’S ANTITHESES.”

### CHAPTER I

[Col. 1077C]

To the youthful people, rich, free, and an heir [Note: [Col. 1077D] *To the youthful people*, etc. The words “Not that teachers—Christ alone would give all things” are exceedingly obscure and perhaps corrupt. We have corrected:

“The lofty towers and the savage walls of evils,” etc., and “to pull down,” in place of “that he might pull down.” As was also said in the summary, he uses “rival propositions” to mean the *Antitheses*, which are frequently mentioned above in vol. III, the five books *Against Marcion*.],

Which rightly possesses the eternal hope of appointed glory,

The inviolable Power does not command teachers—

Christ alone would give all things—but servants of Christ,

Burning with immense zeal, armed with love of peace,

To bring the mass of war down to the ground,

To pull down the lofty towers and the savage walls of evils,

And the threats rising against the ranks of the holy people,

And to disperse empty words upon the wind.

Therefore we have rightly spoken of the “rival propositions,”

And from his own words strive also to set forth the memorials of salvation

Which abundant grace has poured out,

[Col. 1077D] And to expose to the saints the robber’s concealed wound,

[Col. 1078C] Lest some untaught and ignorant man unexpectedly dare

To fall into it and, once captured, abandon the heavenly gifts.

### CHAPTER II

God, therefore, is everywhere one for all mortals [Note: [Col. 1078D] *God, therefore, is everywhere one for all mortals*, etc. Here we punctuated “a drink fashioned with every wisdom,” so that “wisdom” is an ablative, according to the Author’s usage, with the final syllable shortened. We corrected “fashioned” in place of “believed”; we also read “so as to be everlasting of itself” in place of “everlasting,” etc.; and “the disposer of the underworld” in place of “is placed.”]:

The eternal kingdom, source of profound light,

Fountain of life, a drink fashioned with every wisdom.

He himself brought forth the world, whose bosom encompasses all things.

No region confines him; no place encloses him within its bounds.

No matter was made so as to be everlasting of itself,

As though it had always existed, recreated by no maker.

He is the disposer of heaven, earth, sea, and the underworld;

The divider, founder, and author of the airy spirit.

He alone is the everlasting God, immeasurable power.

[Col. 1078D] The Law had shown the people that he is the one God,

[Col. 1079A] Whose mighty voice spoke to Moses upon the mountain.

His power, wisdom, glory, and Word—

The Son, Light begotten from immeasurable Light—

Proclaims through the prophets’ voices that he is the One.

Receiving this in due order, Paul himself likewise teaches

That this one is the Father through whom all things were made,

And that there is one Christ through whom God made all things,

Before whom he declares that every knee must plainly bend,

From whom every *patria* in heaven and on earth is named [Note: [Col. 1079D] *From whom every patria in heaven and on earth is named.* In this text of Eph. 3 the Author retains the Greek word πατριά (*patria*, “family” or “lineage”). Blessed Jerome, writing against Helvidius, acknowledges that the Latins call “paternities” what the Greeks call πατριαί (*patriai*), when a numerous company of a family spreads from a single root.].

Like a father, he is jealous for the people with the highest love of devotion,

And in holiness wishes all flesh to live

And a people clean and without crime to appear before him.

With this jealousy he guards our salvation by the Law,

Admonishing, commanding, chastising, and insisting that we be lawful.

The same Apostle, when correcting the Galatian brethren,

[Col. 1079B] Wrote that he, Paul, possessed such jealousy.

### CHAPTER III

God therefore freely requited the sins of the fathers,

Killing parents together with their offspring in the Flood,

And grandchildren born from their stock to the fourth generation,

Although he had then prolonged their lives for almost nine hundred years.

The judgment seems harsh, the sentence savage:

That a little child in Sodom, still innocent, tender, and defenseless,

Should lose its life. For what sin had the infant committed?

What you might think cruel is the honorable work of devotion.

He extinguished the authors of the crime and the offspring

Who followed their fathers’ sins, lest the evil grow further.

Yet the innocent infant does not suffer everlasting punishment with its fathers;

Ignorant and not increased in guilt,

Lest it become a sharer in crime upon reaching maturity,

An untimely death releases it from the evils that would otherwise come in the future.

### CHAPTER IV

Why, then, does God command that the blood of animals be poured out to him [Note: [Col. 1079D] *Why, then, does God command that the blood of animals be poured out to him?* etc. He gives an excellent explanation of the sacrifices of the bullock, the heifer, and both goats, again alluding to Heb. 9.]?

Why does he bind by Law, threatening anyone among the people

Who transgressed in error with immediate death by stoning?

And then again he rejects these things, denying that these gifts please him,

As he rebukes the people weighed down by the proof of sin.

He himself, being true, commands; he himself, being just, rejects.

If you seek the reasons, cease to be troubled in error;

For the ground of faith is greater than any image supplied by reason.

Look through the mirror at the shadow of the shining light:

What mean the bullocks’ blood, the heifer’s ashes, and the two goats?

One is released and departs; the other falls as a sacrifice at the Temple.

[Col. 1079D] For the prophet, so commanded from on high, sprinkled the people

With the blood of the bullock mixed with water, together with all the vessels,

The priests, and the written volumes of the Law—

[Col. 1080A] Not an individual hope or an appearance devoid of power,

But, through the bullock’s example, Christ destined to suffer in the body.

Bearing the harsh yoke upon his shoulders, beneath the plow’s beam

He bravely tore open his breast with an iron plowshare,

Pouring a stream of his own blood into the furrows of souls.

The Temple’s vessels, indeed, signify our bodies.

He is God’s true Temple, not previously consecrated;

For by his own blood he made human beings his companions

And willed them to be priests of his body [Note: [Col. 1079D] *And willed them to be priests of his body*, etc. We observed above, Book IV *Against Marcion*, ch. 40, no. 662, where the matter is discussed more fully, that these words confirm the reality of the Lord’s body and blood. A little later, when explaining the sacrifice of the heifer, he uses *contamine* for *contaminatione*, “contamination.” He then proceeds to discuss the two goats.];

He himself is by right the perfect priest of the supreme Father.

He entrusted hearing, sight, and an unmoving step [meaning uncertain],

And sprinkled the book, speaking prophetically to the witnesses,

Showing that the Law was bound to his holy blood.

[Col. 1080B] The sacrifice of the heifer also manifests this meaning.

The chief Levite took expiation for the people from its blood

And carried it inside the veil;

Outside the camp’s gates he burned its body at command,

And from its ashes cleansed bodies that had fallen.

Our Lord, who redeemed us by his death,

Fulfilled this Law when, willingly suffering violence

From the unrighteous people outside the camp,

He proved by deeds what had been foretold.

He truly cleanses a people full of contamination,

Granting all things as the bountiful author of the body.

Passing within heaven’s veil with his blood,

He poured it out alone for the deaths of many.

A holy victim therefore befits the great priest,

Which he, possessing it worthily in perfection, is shown to offer.

He has a body: this is the living sacrifice for mortals.

[Col. 1080C] He offered this worthy victim, one sacrifice for all great things.

Let the figure of the goats signify rejected human beings

From two peoples, both barren and without fruit.

In the Gospel the Lord likewise said that the kids

Were seen standing apart from the sheep on the left.

There are indeed some who suffered for the Lord’s name;

Thus, because a barren deed concealed its fruit, it is fitting

That these prophets be deemed worthy of the final altar.

Others were cast away, like the unrighteous rich man from Lazarus,

Rejected because they persisted in their hardness.

### CHAPTER V

For the veil hanging in the middle distinguished the two regions [Note: [Col. 1080D] *For the veil hanging in the middle distinguished the two regions*, etc. *Cernebat*, “perceived,” is taken to mean *discernebat*, “distinguished,” since it separated the Holy of Holies, which the high priest alone entered, from the Holy Place, or Tabernacle, into which the other priests were admitted.],

And divided the one sanctuary into two parts.

The inner region was called the Holy of Holies.

There stood the altar, gleaming nobly with gold,

[Col. 1080D] Together with the covenants, the tablets of the Law, and the ark,

Covered with skins bearing the recognized color of heaven,

[Col. 1081A] Clothed inwardly in gold, with wood throughout its middle.

Here were the tablets of the Law; here the jar filled with manna;

Here was Aaron’s rod, which produces the shoots of the cross [Note: [Col. 1081C] *Here was Aaron’s rod*, etc. In the added words “which produces the shoots of the cross,” the reading *crucis*, “of the cross,” appears corrupt. The Author interprets the Greek word κάρυα (*karya*), which the Vulgate translator at Num. 17 renders from the Hebrew as “almonds,” to mean “nuts of the storax tree.” The Greek word is indeed common to both, since it signifies nuts of every kind. That Aaron’s rod signified the Virgin Mary may be found below, ch. 7.],

Though unlike that tree, yet sprung from the storax tree.
