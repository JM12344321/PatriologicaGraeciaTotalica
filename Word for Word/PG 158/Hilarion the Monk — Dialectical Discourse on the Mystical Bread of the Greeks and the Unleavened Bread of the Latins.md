Let us therefore conclude from the opinion and judgment of the learned Latin theologians that Christ was crucified on the very day of Passover—namely, on the Preparation—having chosen to suffer and to sacrifice himself as the true Lamb at the time of the typological lamb [OCR: `τοπικοῦ`, probably `τυπικοῦ`]; and, according to the astronomers, that on that very Preparation the moon was on its fifteenth day from the spring equinox.

The transfer of Passover to the Sabbath arose from the ancient Jewish custom. Those who looked to the inconvenience concerning foods joined two feasts into one Sabbath; and for this reason John wrote, “For that day of the Sabbath was great,” as though he called it a double feast. This may be learned from the Jews, who never celebrate Passover on the Preparation. And again concerning unleavened [bread] and the Paschal sacrifice.
