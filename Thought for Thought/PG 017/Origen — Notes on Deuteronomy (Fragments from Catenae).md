## Deuteronomy 1:31

**Verse 31.** “As a man carries and cares for his child.” Aquila rendered the line, “He carried the son as a nurse might pick him up.” In his homilies on Jeremiah, Origen explains: “He put up with you and stooped to your level, taking our weakness upon himself. He was like a teacher sounding out syllables with small children, or a father tending his own children and adapting himself to their ways, gradually leading them toward things more perfect and sublime.”

<!-- source-locus: 3.18 -->
## Deuteronomy 3:18

**Verse 18.** “I commanded you.” Moses is addressing the tribe of Reuben, the tribe of Gad, and half the tribe of Manasseh.

<!-- source-locus: 3.27 -->
## Deuteronomy 3:27–28

**Verses 27–28.** God grants Moses something more than Aaron received: Moses may see the land with his own eyes. Yet God does not permit him to enter it. Both men die outside the promised land as a penalty for their failure of faith, despite having come within reach of it, because the final goal is attained through faith. This also symbolizes the law: it can neither bring perfection nor fulfill the promise, since “the righteous person will live by faith.” Even so, it is an honor to understand God’s judgment and to know one’s death beforehand.

The same author continues: God brings Joshua forward. Following and attending upon Moses had already sanctified him; now he was to receive the Spirit of leadership through Moses and take responsibility for the people in Moses’ place. Elisha likewise carried on Elijah’s work in Elijah’s spirit, since the same works must be accomplished in the same Spirit. As Numbers says, “Take Joshua son of Nun to yourself, a man who has God’s Spirit within him, and lay your hands on him.”

In the same way, the apostles already possessed the life-giving Spirit through the Lord’s breath, yet they received the Spirit of leadership sent from heaven. This was not one spirit placed inside another, or a different spirit added on top of the first, but a new activity of the same Spirit, as Paul teaches.

[Translation note: The source compresses the clause about Joshua’s reception of the “ruling Spirit” and succession to Moses; the rendering follows the comparison with Elisha and the apostles.]

<!-- source-locus: 4.12 -->
## Deuteronomy 4:12

**Verse 12.** “The Lord spoke to you from the middle of the fire, and you heard the sound of words.”

On the same passage the source says, “Another says: It is clear that a person sees and hears meanings with the eyes of the mind.” The phrase “sound of words,” however, describes ordinary sense perception, since the ear receives sound. If the wording is taken with complete strictness, only the Ten Commandments were written on the tablets; Moses taught everything else at God’s direction.

<!-- source-locus: 14.19 -->
## Deuteronomy 14:19

**Verse 19.** “Every winged creeping creature shall be unclean.” The note first distinguishes creatures with projections on their legs below the knees, which it calls clean, from those without them, which it calls unclean.

The law rejects the eagle, the griffin-vulture, the sea eagle, and the vulture. These birds soar high, delight in the heights, and hate what lies below; as the text says, “The young of the vulture fly in the heights.” In the same way, a haughty person raises his brows and his thoughts, despises a fair and moderate frame of mind, and refuses to associate with the lowly.

The kite and similar birds portray rapacious people skilled in wrongdoing. Such people rush toward evil without making their purpose obvious to everyone; they harm the public and bring misery on themselves. A desire to take more always accompanies the disease of accursed arrogance. Ordinary gain never satisfies such people, so they devise wrongdoing in many forms and seize things greedily. The sparrow and gull likewise represent greed because they gather whatever comes their way.

The list continues with the hawk, night raven, diver, ibis, a bird called the *prosphorion*, the pelican, and the swan. These signify cruel people who plot savagely against others and rush without restraint upon anyone they can attack.

[Translation note: The Greek contains uncertain ancient bird names and several damaged constructions. In particular, a brief contrast after “kite” cannot be translated securely, and the final sentence breaks off; the readable rendering retains the recoverable argument without supplying a lost conclusion.]

## Deuteronomy 20:5

Betrothal to a woman can signify a mind broken down and surrendered to bodily desires. When the military herald calls, people caught in such attachments withdraw from the holy and warlike host. The herald voices in advance what they are likely thinking and saying.

Surely this is how they reason: in times of persecution, people attached to comfort choose the path that costs them least. The transmitted Greek then says that they “follow the exertions demanded by love for God,” although the context seems to require “do not follow.” They fear that suffering in the struggle will cost them their homes, possessions, and cherished desires.

[Translation note: The selected source begins in the middle of a sentence. The Greek has κατακολουθεῖν (“to follow”) with no negative; reading “do not follow” requires supposing that a negative has dropped out.]

<!-- source-locus: 20.8 -->
## Deuteronomy 20:8

**Verse 8.** “Who is afraid and fainthearted?” The same author explains that anyone absorbed in earthly concerns—building houses, planting vineyards, and yielding to bodily pleasures—will inevitably prove cowardly and weak.

<!-- source-locus: 20.10 -->
## Deuteronomy 20:10

**Verse 10.** “When you approach a city to wage war against it.” Compare the Gospel’s command: “When you enter a city, find out who in it is worthy.” The instruction to approach “with peace” likewise agrees with the Apostle’s statement that “a servant of the Lord must not quarrel.”

<!-- source-locus: 20.13 -->
## Deuteronomy 20:13

**Verse 13.** “You shall strike every male in it with the sword.” The same author comments: The spiritual sword is God’s word. With “the sword of the Spirit, which is the word of God,” the divinely inspired disciples killed every “male” element of the hostile power—Simon and Elymas the magicians, for example—and demolished their powerful teachings.

They spared the “women,” meaning souls able to say, “Because we feared you, Lord, we conceived and gave birth upon the earth.” They likewise spared the infants addressed by Paul: “Little children, do not be childish in your thinking; be infants only in evil.” Scripture also says to preserve the livestock, for “a righteous person has compassion on the lives of his animals.” Here the animals signify people whose understanding is less rational. Even Cornelius was represented by an animal in Peter’s vision of the four-footed creatures.

<!-- source-locus: 22.6 -->
## Deuteronomy 22:6

**Verse 6.** “Do not take the mother together with her young.” Once their mother is taken, the young cannot feed themselves.

<!-- source-locus: 23.3 -->
## Deuteronomy 23:3

**Verse 3.** “No Ammonite shall enter.” Why are Moabites and Ammonites excluded forever rather than for a specified period? First, because their ancestry began in unlawful intercourse; second, because they drew the Israelites into their impiety. They adorned their women and used them to ensnare Israel into idolatrous worship.

Edomites and Egyptians, by contrast, are to be admitted after three generations: Edomites because they are Israel’s brothers, and Egyptians because they once acted as benefactors. When famine came, Israel’s ancestors entered Egypt and received food. The law thus teaches us not to forget even a kindness received long ago.

<!-- source-locus: 23.14a -->
## Deuteronomy 23:14 (First Note)

**Verse 14.** What does it mean that “the Lord your God walks through your camp”? Foul-smelling air can cause disease. God therefore told the Israelites to bury their excrement while they were encamped. To keep them from disobeying and suffering harm, he added that the Lord walked through their camp. He was there “to deliver you and hand your enemy over before you.” This also trained them to commit no lawless act, for their camp was to be holy, with nothing shameful found in it.

## Deuteronomy 23:14 (Second Note)

**Verse 14.** “The Lord your God walks through the camp to deliver you and hand your enemy over to you. Your camp must therefore be holy, and nothing shameful may be seen among you.”

Anyone who hopes to conquer spiritual enemies must reject every kind of impurity. Yet all of us stumble in many ways, and human nature is sick with a tendency to slide into sin. As the Psalmist says, “No one can understand every transgression.” The next sentence is damaged, but it appears to describe a person overcome by one of the impurities innate in us and caught in conduct outside God’s law and a holy life.

The law of sin dwells in the members of our bodies. The note first says that faults arising from weakness should not be committed openly or in front of many people, but secretly, as far outside the camp as possible. It then says that acts of correction must cover them: “The wise will cover their own shame.”

When we stop doing wrong and reshape our minds by turning toward better things, we remove these stumbling blocks from our midst. By overcoming the stench of sin with the fragrant deeds that follow it, we can return to the saints’ camp—the church of the firstborn.

This is fulfilled in Christ when we take up his cross with an alert and ready mind. Carrying a wooden peg on one’s belt alludes indirectly to the wood of the cross. Bodily practices thus serve as images of spiritual realities. A damaged sentence then contrasts this spiritual meaning with physical uncleanness and going to the latrine before the eyes of our Creator. The conclusion is clear: we must bury our impurities because the Lord walks through the camp. Christ dwells within us and walks among us.

[Translation note: Several phrases in this record are textually damaged. The translation retains the source’s difficult advice about concealing faults before correcting them, but it does not guess at the defective clause near the end.]

## Deuteronomy 24:6

**Verse 6.** “Do not take a mill or upper millstone as security, because that is taking a life as security.” Another interpretation of the same passage: A mill and an upper millstone are not literally a soul; they help make bread, the principal food by which people live. Scripture uses the same extended language when it calls the body’s blood its soul. Neither blood nor a millstone is literally a soul, but both contribute to the preservation of life.

<!-- source-locus: 27.15 -->
## Deuteronomy 27:15

**Verse 15.** “He sets it up in secret.” Some people, fearing punishment, try to escape notice and practice impiety in hiding. The law therefore intensifies its warning by placing under a curse those who sin without witnesses. Such people put on a counterfeit show of reverence while burying their passions within; in their thoughts they run riot toward every kind of perversion. They are the people who set up idols in secret.

<!-- source-locus: 27.17 -->
## Deuteronomy 27:17

**Verse 17.** “Cursed is anyone who moves a neighbor’s boundary.” A person also moves a neighbor’s spiritual boundaries by shaking someone loose from true knowledge of God and a God-inspired life. The next verse makes this meaning clear.

<!-- source-locus: 27.18 -->
## Deuteronomy 27:18

**Verse 18.** “Cursed is anyone who leads a blind person astray on the road.” The same author applies this to people who draw others from idolatry or Judaism into detestable heresies. These people are still blind because they do not yet possess the light of truth and the knowledge of God. Heretical teachers deceive them by pointing to a road different from the apostolic way.

<!-- source-locus: 27.19 -->
## Deuteronomy 27:19

**Verse 19.** “Cursed is anyone who perverts justice for the proselyte, orphan, and widow.” He places under the same curse those who likewise pervert the judgment of a proselyte, orphan, and widow, restating the same point in another way. The literal command is beneficial, but spiritual contemplation yields a higher meaning as well.

The proselytes are Gentiles entering God’s holy church. The orphans are those who have renounced their former father, Satan, but have not yet put on Jesus and are still catechumens. These people are also like widows: they have turned away from the one who sowed seeds of destruction in them, but have not yet been united to Christ as a pure virgin, in the Apostle’s words.

Anyone who perverts justice for these people by failing to transmit the mystery of godliness without adulteration lies under the curse. A diligent reader can interpret the remaining sayings spiritually in the same way while still preserving their literal meaning.

<!-- source-locus: 27.26 -->
## Deuteronomy 27:26

**Verse 26.** “Cursed is everyone who does not continue in all the words of the law.” The Samaritan copy confirms the Septuagint’s words “in all”: it contains *chol*, meaning “all” or “everything.” Paul agrees when he says, “All who rely on works of the law are under a curse.” Those of the circumcision are therefore shown to be under the curse because they do not do everything the law commands.

<!-- source-locus: 33.6 -->
## Deuteronomy 33:6

**Verse 6.** “Let Reuben live and not die.” As Jacob was dying, he cursed Reuben, saying, “Do not boil over like water.” Moses later blessed him: “Let Reuben live and not die.” Both statements are appropriate. Jacob cursed Reuben for sleeping with his father’s concubine, thereby warning later generations; Moses blessed him because he alone spared Joseph when his brothers wanted to kill him. It would be absurd for the curse to take effect when Reuben sinned but for no blessing to follow when he did what was right.
