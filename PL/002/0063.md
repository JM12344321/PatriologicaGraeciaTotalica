Accordingly, the times of the foretold and future birth of Christ, of his passion, and of the destruction of the city of Jerusalem—that is, its devastation—must be investigated. For Daniel says that both the holy city and the sanctuary are to be destroyed together with the leader who is to come, and that the pinnacle is to be destroyed unto annihilation. Accordingly, the times of the coming of Christ the leader must be investigated. These we shall examine in Daniel; and when they have been calculated, we shall prove that he has come, both from the times prescribed, from the corresponding signs, and from his works [Col. 0612B]. These things we prove [Note: [Col. 0612C] “Works” in Par.] from the events that followed, which were announced as destined to occur after his advent, so that we may believe all things both fulfilled and foreseen [Note: [Col. 0612C] “Thus we shall prove” in Jun. [Col. 0612C] Rhen. adds “and.” [Col. 0612C] “Perceived” in Rhen.].

Thus, therefore, Daniel prophesied concerning him in such a way as to show both when and at what time he would liberate the nations, and that after the passion of Christ this city was to be destroyed. For he speaks as follows:

> In the first year under Darius, son of Ahasuerus, of the seed of the Medes, who reigned over the kingdom of the Chaldeans, I, Daniel, understood from the books the number of the years. And while I was still speaking in prayer, behold, the man Gabriel, whom I had seen in the vision at the beginning, came flying and touched me, as it were at the hour of the evening sacrifice; and he made me understand, and spoke [Col. 0612C] with me, and said: “Daniel, I have now come forth to imbue you with understanding. At the beginning of your supplication [Note: [Col. 0612C] “Observation” in Rhen. and Oberth.] the word went forth. And I have come to announce it to you, because you are a man of desires; therefore consider the word and understand the vision.”

[Note: [Col. 0611D] “Seventy weeks have been shortened concerning your people,” etc. This is a celebrated passage against the Jews for proving the advent of the Messiah. The passage occurs in Daniel 9 and is commonly adduced for this purpose by theologians. Another passage conforming to this one is from Genesis 49; that is, the famous prophecy of the patriarch Jacob: [Col. 0612D] “The scepter shall not be taken away from Judah, nor a leader from his thigh, until he who is to be sent comes.” In the Hebrew codices it reads, “until Shiloh comes.” But Shiloh is the Messiah—a point not difficult to demonstrate against the Jews, since the ancient Jews attest it. The three Chaldean paraphrases also—those of Onkelos, Jonathan, and the Jerusalem paraphrase—have “King Messiah” in place of “Shiloh.” Likewise, in the very ancient and highly authoritative book called *Bereshith Rabbah*, [text absent in the supplied source]; that is, “Shiloh is the King Messiah.” Among more recent writers, Rabbi Solomon, Rabbi Kimchi, and Rabbi Bechai hold the same view. Hence contemporary Jews are readily convicted from this passage of their folly and stubborn impiety. In the Greek text, however, it reads: Οὐκ ἐκλείψει ἐξ Ἰούδα ἕως ἂν ἔλθῃ ᾧ ἀπόκειται, “A ruler shall not fail from Judah until he comes for whom it is reserved”; that is, as St. Epiphanius interprets it, “for whom it is reserved that he should be Christ.” This occurs, of course, in his treatment of the heresy of the Herodians, who were so called because they professed that Herod was Christ. LE PR.]

> Seventy weeks have been shortened concerning your people [Col. 0613A] and concerning the holy city, until transgression grows old, sins are sealed, acts of injustice are expiated, and eternal righteousness is introduced; so that [Note: [Col. 0613C] The same witnesses read “and so that.”] vision and prophet may be sealed, and the Holy of Holies may be anointed. And you shall know, perceive, and understand that from the going forth of the word to restore and rebuild Jerusalem until Christ the leader there shall be seven and a half weeks, and seventy-two and a half weeks; and it shall return and be built in joy and with an enclosing rampart, and the times shall be renewed. And after these seventy-two weeks the anointing shall be destroyed and shall be no more; and the city and the sanctuary he shall destroy together with the leader who is coming, and they shall be cut down as in a flood, until the end of the war, which shall be cut short unto annihilation. And he shall confirm the covenant among [Note: [Col. 0613C] “Among” is absent from Fran.] many. For one week and half a week my sacrifice [Col. 0613B] and libation shall be taken away; and in the sanctuary there shall be the abomination of desolation, and until the end of the time a consummation shall be imposed upon this devastation.

Let us therefore attend to the limit: how truly he foretold [Note: [Col. 0613C] Rhen. and Oberth. read “in the word which he spoke.”] that seventy weeks were to come, during which, if they received him, the city would be built in breadth and length and the times would be renewed. But God, foreseeing what was to happen—that they would not merely refuse to receive him, but would even persecute him and hand him over to death—summed the matter up again and said that within sixty-two weeks he would be born and the Holy of Holies would be anointed; but when the seven and a half weeks were fulfilled, he would have to suffer, and the city would be destroyed after one and a half weeks, namely at the time [Note: [Col. 0613C] Pam. omits “time.”] when the seven and a half weeks had been completed. For he speaks as follows:

> And the city [Col. 0613C] and the sanctuary shall be destroyed together with the leader who is to come; and they shall be cut down as in a flood, and he shall destroy the pinnacle unto annihilation.

[Note: [Col. 0613C] “How, then, do we show that Christ came within the seventieth and a half week?” The author of the *Questions from the Old Testament*, appended to volume IV of the works [Col. 0613D] of Augustine, says: “From the time of the first year of Darius, king of the Persians, until the birth and passion of the Lord and the destruction of Jerusalem, which was accomplished by Vespasian, ruler of the Roman Empire, the seventy weeks were fulfilled. These amount to 490 years; and this calculation through the reigns and successions of the empire is manifest, just as it is found calculated by Tertullian in the book he wrote *Against the Jews*. Lest it should redound to his injury, because he calculated correctly, we have omitted it. And who could doubt this number, when approximately three hundred years are now found to have elapsed beyond it?” From these words it is inferred that the age of this writer was somewhat earlier than Augustine’s. RIG.]

How, then, do we show [Note: [Col. 0613C] Others have “shall we show.”] that Christ came within the sixty-two and a half weeks? We shall count from the first year of Darius, since [Note: [Col. 0613C] The Dijon codex and Jerome on Daniel 9 have “because.”] [Col. 0614A] at that very time the vision itself was shown to Daniel. For he says:

> And understand, and reckon unto the completion [Note: [Col. 0613C] Rhen. and Oberth. have “from the going forth.” The Dijon codex reads, “From the prophecy of this word, therefore, from the first,” etc. [Col. 0614C] “From the prophecy of this word, therefore, from the first,” etc., in the Dijon codex. “From the prophecy” is also read in Jerome.] of the word by which I answer [Note: [Col. 0614C] The same witnesses read “to answer.”] these things to you.

We must therefore calculate from the first year of Darius, when Daniel saw this vision. Let us therefore see how [Note: [Col. 0614C] Fran. reads “that.”] the years are fulfilled up to the advent of Christ.

Darius reigned for nineteen years; Artaxerxes reigned for forty years; then King Ochus, who was also Cyrus, reigned for twenty-four years; Argus [Note: [Col. 0613D] “Argus, for one year.” Eusebius calls this man Arses, and Sulpicius calls him Arse; yet they assign him either four or three years, while they assign Darius only six. PAM.] for one year; another Darius, who was also named Melas, for twenty-two years; Alexander the Macedonian for twelve years.

[Note: [Col. 0613D] “Alexander the Macedonian, for twelve years.” This must undoubtedly be read thus on the authority of Blessed Jerome (*On Daniel*, chapter 2), [Col. 0614C] Berossus, Sulpicius Severus (*Sacred History*, book II), and the *Glossa Ordinaria*, rather than the “ten” previously printed both here and in Pomoerius. These figures can be reconciled with the mere six years given by Eusebius, because Eusebius assigns forty years to the successor, Ptolemy Lagus—who here, and in Josephus, *Antiquities*, book XII, chapter 1, is called SOTER—whereas the author assigns him only thirty-five, so that they differ by only one year overall. PAM.]

Then, after Alexander—who had ruled over the Medes and Persians, whom he had reconquered [Note: [Col. 0614C] Jerome and Jun. read “conquered.”], and had established his kingdom at Alexandria, which at that time he also called by his own name—Soter afterward [Note: [Col. 0614C] “Afterward” is absent from Ven.] reigned there in Alexandria for thirty-five years. Philadelphus succeeded him and reigned [Col. 0614B] for thirty-nine years [Note: [Col. 0614D] “He reigned for thirty-nine years.” Jerome reads: “Philadelphus succeeded him, reigning for thirty-eight years.” RIG.]. Euergetes succeeded him for twenty-five years; then Philopator for seventeen years; after him Epiphanes for twenty-four years; likewise another Euergetes for twenty-nine years, Soter for thirty-eight years, Ptolemy for thirty-eight years, and Cleopatra for twenty years and six months.

Cleopatra also reigned jointly with Augustus for thirteen years, and after Cleopatra Augustus reigned for another forty-three years. For all the years of Augustus’s rule amounted to fifty-six. We see, moreover, that in the forty-first year of Augustus’s rule [Note: [Col. 0614D] “We see, moreover, that in the forty-first year of Augustus.” This passage is cited in Eusebius’s *Chronicle* and examined in Joseph Scaliger’s *Annotation* at number 2015. Irenaeus, book [omitted], chapter 25: “Around the first-and-fortieth year of Augustus’s rule.” RIG.], during which [Note: [Col. 0614C] Jerome reads “who.”] he ruled after Cleopatra’s death, Christ was born. And this same Augustus survived for fifteen years after Christ was born.

[Note: [Col. 0614D] “And the remaining span of years up to the day of Christ’s birth shall be....” The Fulda manuscript reads: “Up to the day of Christ’s birth, the forty-first year; after Cleopatra’s death, the twenty-eighth year of Augustus: the total is 437 years and five months at the time of the birth.” Jerome, however, reads more correctly: “And the remaining span of years up to the day of Christ’s birth, in the forty-first year of Augustus after [Col. 0615C] Cleopatra’s death, shall be 437 years and five months,” etc. RIG.]

And the remaining span of years up to the day of Christ’s birth shall be forty-one years. The total is therefore 437 years and six months. Thus the sixty-two and a half weeks are fulfilled, which amount to 437 years [Col. 0614C] and six months, up to the day of Christ’s birth. And eternal righteousness was manifested, and the Holy of Holies—that is, Christ—was anointed; and vision and prophecy were sealed, and sins were forgiven, which through faith in Christ’s name are remitted [Note: [Col. 0614C] Rhen. reads “granted.”] to all who believe in him.

What, then, does his statement that vision and prophecy [Col. 0615A] are sealed mean [Note: [Col. 0615C] Rhen. and Oberth. read “the prophet.”], since all the prophets announced concerning him that he was to come and would have to suffer? Therefore, since prophecy was fulfilled through his advent, for this reason he said that vision and prophecy were sealed: because he is himself the seal of all the prophets, fulfilling everything that the prophets of old had announced concerning him.

For after [Note: [Col. 0615C] “For” is absent from Rhen. and Oberth.] the advent of Christ and his passion, there is now neither vision nor a prophet [Note: [Col. 0615C] Others read “nor is there prophecy.”] to announce that Christ is to come. Finally, if this is not so, let the Jews produce any writings of prophets subsequent to Christ, or visible miracles of angels such as the patriarchs of old saw up to the advent of Christ—who has now come, and by whose coming vision and prophecy have been sealed, that is, established.

And rightly does the Evangelist say [Col. 0615B]:

> The Law and the Prophets were until John the Baptist (Matt. 11:13).

For when Christ was baptized—that is, when he sanctified the waters in his baptism [Note: [Col. 0615C] “Sanctifying the waters in his baptism.” A similar statement occurs in Blessed Cyprian’s sermon *On the Baptism of Christ*: “Christ came to baptism, not because he needed washing, since there was no sin in him, but so that perennial authority might be given to the sacrament.” These passages should be noted against Calvin and others who deny that water is necessary in baptism. Another such passage is Blessed Cyprian’s second letter to Donatus; the reader may consult our annotations on it, number 8. PAM.]—all the fullness of the spiritual gifts of former times came to an end in Christ, as he sealed the vision and all the prophecies that he fulfilled by his advent. Hence he says with complete assurance that his advent seals vision and prophecy.

Accordingly, having shown both the number of years and that the period of sixty-two and a half weeks had been fulfilled, and that Christ then came—that is, was born—let us see [Note: [Col. 0615C] Seml. adds “in what.”] in what event the other seven and a half weeks, which were subdivided when they were cut off from the preceding weeks, were fulfilled.

After Augustus, who survived for fifteen years following Christ’s birth, Tiberius Caesar succeeded him and held the empire for twenty-two years, seven months [Col. 0615C], and twenty days [Note: [Col. 0616C] Rhen., following Jerome, reads “twenty-eight.”]. In the fifteenth year [Col. 0616A] of his reign Christ suffered, being approximately thirty years old when he suffered.

Likewise Gaius Caesar, also called Caligula, reigned for three years, eight months, and thirteen days [Note: [Col. 0615D] “Thirteen days.” No one is unaware that Claudius reigned after Caligula. Rhen. and Oberth. therefore restore his reign here as follows: “Tiberius Claudius, for thirteen years, seven months, and twenty days”; although we do not read this either in Jerome or in Pomoerius. Indeed, it ought to be omitted, so that the author’s reckoning of only fifty-two years and seven months may stand, as Pamelius says. He immediately adds: “It can therefore be seen even from this that he—Tertullian—was not very attentive to history, perhaps imitating in this respect Matthew the Evangelist, who in the book of Christ’s genealogy omitted three generations, so that, according to Blessed Jerome’s interpretation, he might not exceed three groups of fourteen. Meanwhile, the number of years agrees sufficiently with Clement of Alexandria.” EDD.]; Nero Caesar for nine years, nine months, and thirteen days; Galba for seven months [Note: [Col. 0616C] Jerome reads “twenty-nine days.”] and six days; Otho for three months and five days; Vitellius for eight months and ten days [Note: [Col. 0616C] Jerome reads “twenty-eight days.”].

In the first year of his reign Vespasian defeated the Jews; and the total comes to fifty-two years and six months. For he reigned for nine years [Note: [Col. 0616C] The same witnesses read “eleven years.”]. And thus, by the day of their conquest, the Jews had fulfilled the seventy weeks foretold by Daniel.

Therefore, when these periods too had been completed and the Jews defeated, libations and sacrifices afterward ceased there, since from that time onward they could no longer be celebrated there. For the anointing there was also destroyed after the passion of Christ.

[Note: [Col. 0615D] “For it had been foretold that the anointing there would be destroyed.” Ἐνδελεχισμόν: “the continual sacrifice.” Jerome on Daniel 8. Josephus, *The Jewish War*, book VII, chapter 8: τὸν ἐνδελεχισμὸν καλούμενον ἀνδρῶν ἀπορίᾳ διαλελοιπέναι τῷ Θεῷ, “the offering called the continual sacrifice had ceased to be offered to God for lack of men.” PAM.]

For it had been foretold that the anointing there would be destroyed [Col. 0616B], just as it was prophesied in the Psalms:

> They destroyed my hands and feet (Ps. 21:17).

The suffering involved in this destruction was completed within the period of the seventy weeks, under Tiberius Caesar, when Rubellius Geminus and Fufius Geminus were consuls [Note: [Col. 0615D] “Rubellius Geminus and Rufinus Geminus.” Rhenanus had printed it thus [Col. 0616C] in his second edition; the first and third editions read “Rufinus Geminus.” The spelling of these names varies in the ancient books: the Sicilian *Fasti* have “Rufus and Rubellinus”; the Capitoline *Fasti*, “Rubellius and Fufius,” as does Tacitus; Sulpicius Severus has “Rubellius”; others, “Rubellio.” Some have “the two Gemini.” The Sicilian *Fasti* have “Geminus and Geminus,” and immediately afterward, “Rufus and Rubellinus.” This gave rise to the error of those who thought that Rufus and Rubellinus had been appointed substitute consuls for the two Gemini, although this means nothing other than “the two Gemini,” that is, Gaius Rubellius Geminus and Gaius Fufius Geminus. Thus Tacitus states it most clearly at the beginning of book V: “When Rubellius and Fufius were consuls”; and each of these, of course, had the cognomen Geminus. RIG.], in the month of March, at the time of Passover, on the eighth day before the Kalends of April,

[Note: [Col. 0616D] “On the first day of Unleavened Bread.” I have not yet been able to agree with Pamelius, who thinks that this passage can prove the practice of our Church whereby the body of Christ the Lord is consecrated in the Eucharist with unleavened bread. Granted that Moses commanded in Exodus 12 that everyone should use unleavened bread on those days, does that therefore prove the Church’s custom? Many think that leavened bread was formerly used in the sacred rites. On this subject there is an elegant treatise by Father Jacques Sirmond, of blessed memory (*μακαρίτου*). Demetrius Chomatenus, Archbishop of Bulgaria, formerly maintained this in his *Response to the Bishop of Dyrrachium*, saying: λατινικῶν ἀζύμων οὐδεὶς οὐδαμοῦ κανὼν μέμνηται, “No canon anywhere has ever mentioned the unleavened bread used by the Latins.” Nevertheless, St. Cyprian says in the book *On the Lord’s Supper*: “This unleavened bread, true and sincere food in outward form and sacrament, sanctifies us by touch, illuminates us by faith, and [Col. 0617C] conforms us to Christ by truth”—if indeed this book is by Blessed Cyprian. LE PR.—“On the eighth day before the Kalends of April.” From this, first of all, the opinion of those Fathers is confirmed who write that Christ suffered on the same day—namely, the eighth day before the Kalends of April—on which the annunciation was made to the Blessed Virgin. Secondly, it confirms the consistent interpretation given by all Latin writers of the three Evangelists—Matthew 26, Mark 14, and Luke 22—namely that, on the fourteenth day of the moon, the first day of Unleavened Bread having already begun in the evening, Christ celebrated the Passover [Col. 0617D] with his disciples. PAM.]

on the first day of Unleavened Bread, on which Moses had commanded that they slaughter the lamb toward evening. Accordingly, the whole synagogue of the children of Israel killed him, saying to Pilate when he wished to release him:

> His blood be upon us and upon our children (Matt. 27:25);

and:

> If you release this man, you are not Caesar’s friend (John 19:12),

so that everything written concerning him might be fulfilled.
