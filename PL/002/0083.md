# QUINTUS SEPTIMIUS FLORENS TERTULLIAN  
## ON THE RESURRECTION OF THE FLESH (C)

## SUMMARY

—Preparing to write on the resurrection of the flesh, the author first teaches that it was not unknown even to the secular world, and especially to Platonists and Pythagoreans. Chapter I.

Although the heretics do not acknowledge it, any more than the Sadducees do, they are nevertheless refuted both by the preceding book, *On the Flesh of Christ*, and, concerning God as the author of the flesh and Christ as the redeemer of the flesh, by the books written *Against Marcion*. For they do not deny the resurrection of the soul, with the sole exception of Lucanus; against him the author defended the soul’s immortality in the book *On the Soul*. Chapter II.

Accordingly, the heretics ought not to follow the common people’s ignorance in denying it. Chapter III.

[Col. 0792D] Nor does the indignity of the flesh, which they allege, stand in the way. Chapter IV.

First, because it was made by God. Chapter V.

Likewise, because it was fashioned by God’s hand. Chapter VI.

Because clay has already been refashioned and transformed into flesh. Chapter VII.

Because among Christians the flesh is washed, anointed, signed, overshadowed by the laying on of hands, and fed with the body and blood of Christ; and finally, after the soul’s struggles, fasts, virginity, and widowhood, it dies for faith in Christ’s name. Chapter VIII.

Because Christ loved the flesh, though weak and sinful. Chapter IX.

[Col. 0793A] Finally, because the flesh is honored in the Scriptures. Chapter X.

Nor does the resurrection of the flesh exceed God’s power. Chapter XI.

For examples of divine power are found in the alternations of day and night, winter and summer, spring and autumn. Chapter XII.

There is also the phoenix, the bird of the East, which renews itself by conducting its own funeral. Chapter XIII.

Moreover, an adequate ground for restoring the flesh is found in God’s judgment, which rewards good and punishes evil. Chapter XIV.

For since soul and flesh share partnership in their works, they must also share the recompense either of punishment or of grace. Chapter XV.

Although the flesh is called the vessel of the soul, [Col. 0793B] it does not follow that it does not also share in the soul’s merits. Chapter XVI.

Indeed, since the soul’s deeds are performed through the flesh, it is not sufficient for the soul to be comforted or tormented without the flesh for works done by means of the flesh. Chapter XVII.

Next, God’s decree is displayed before everyone: the resurrection of the dead. Chapter XVIII.

Some wrongly twist this into a figurative meaning. Chapter XIX.

For the prophets did not proclaim everything through figures: first, because if everything is a figure, what will remain as the reality of which these things are figures? Second, because all the more notable prophecies were fulfilled in Christ himself more clearly than daylight—truly, not figuratively. Chapter XX.

There are also so many divine instruments attesting bodily [Col. 0793C] resurrection so openly that they admit no suspicion of figurative meaning. Chapter XXI.

Finally, attention must be paid to those Scriptures which do not permit the belief that the resurrection has either already occurred or takes place immediately upon departure from this life. Chapter XXII.

Although the apostle says to the Colossians that we have been spiritually raised, he nevertheless does not deny that we shall also rise bodily. Chapter XXIII.

For in his own epistles to the Thessalonians he openly confirms the resurrection. Chapter XXIV.

Likewise, in John’s Apocalypse the very times of the resurrection are indicated. Chapter XXV.

What then? Bodily resurrection may also be vindicated with the support of figurative prophetic language. Chapter XXVI.

[Col. 0793D] This is done under the names both of land and of garments. Chapter XXVII.

Indeed, the resurrection was prophesied not only through words but also through things and deeds. Chapter XXVIII.

Finally, Ezekiel’s determination concerning it is exceedingly plain. Chapter XXIX.

And it is such that it cannot be understood figuratively or allegorically. Chapter XXX.

It is also proved by other manifest testimonies of the prophets. Chapter XXXI.

These are of such a kind that even the resurrection of unburied bodies is proclaimed in them. Chapter XXXII.

Moreover, in the Gospels the Lord did not pronounce everything in parables. Chapter XXXIII.

Indeed, in every mention of the resurrection the Lord properly signifies the bodily nature [Col. 0794A] of the flesh; and the whole human being whom the devil wholly struck down is to be raised by God. Chapter XXXIV.

Both body and soul are distinctly presented as destined either for Gehenna or for eternal life. Chapter XXXV.

Hence the Sadducees’ doctrine is turned completely upside down. Chapter XXXVI.

Although Christ does say that the flesh profits nothing, the sense must be determined from the subject matter of the statement. Chapter XXXVII.

Especially since, by raising the dead in deed as well, he signified the future resurrection. Chapter XXXVIII.

The documentary accounts of the Acts of the Apostles also testify to the resurrection. Chapter XXXIX.

Accordingly, the heresies wrongly interpret for their own purposes, in their customary fashion, the “inner” and “outer” human being, as though by “corruption” the apostle meant the decay of the outer flesh. Chapter XL.

They likewise misinterpret the statement concerning the house of our tabernacle which is dissolved. Chapter XLI.

But corpses are merely changed, not devoured by death, since the skeletons of the most ancient giants still survive, as the author has also said elsewhere. Chapter XLII.

Nor does absence from the body support their case, because the apostle adds that each person must receive through the body according to what he has done, whether good or evil. Chapter XLIII.

Likewise, because he says that the life of Jesus is to be manifested in the body. Chapter XLIV.

In another manifestation of blindness, they also stumble over the old and new [Col. 0794C] human being. Chapter XLV.

Similarly, when the same apostle appears to condemn the flesh, he condemns the works of the flesh. Chapter XLVI.

For he promises salvation to the flesh. Chapter XLVII.

Finally, in 1 Corinthians 15 the resurrection of the flesh is most plainly confirmed by him. Chapter XLVIII.

Indeed, from the preceding discussion one learns in what condition he disinherited flesh and blood from God’s kingdom—namely, as the works of the flesh. Chapter XLIX.

Indeed, even setting aside interpretations of this sort, the very substances of flesh and blood, understood just as they are, may be vindicated for the resurrection. Chapter L.

The same point may also be proved through Christ, who carried flesh and blood with him into heaven. Chapter LI.

And indeed the apostle argues that the dead will return in the same body. Chapter LII.

For the “animate body” cannot be understood as the soul, but as the flesh. Chapter LIII.

Nor, as he touched upon above, is the flesh itself devoured by life, but that which is mortal in it. Chapter LIV.

Furthermore, the transformation of the flesh is one thing; its destruction is another. Chapter LV.

Otherwise it would be absurd for one substance—namely the flesh—to perform the work, and another—namely the soul—to receive payment of the reward. Chapter LVI.

The resurrection must also be believed to be complete, as the Scriptures affirm. Chapter LVII.

Thus both flesh and soul will undergo the sentence of salvation or punishment. Chapter LVIII.

[Col. 0795A] In the age to come the constitution of the human being will not differ in substance. Chapter LIX.

The human being will also rise with sex and members preserved, even though these are free from their customary functions necessary to life. Chapter LX.

This is especially so since certain functions of those members—by which we praise God and prophesy—will remain; even in this life those who fast abstain from food, while voluntary eunuchs and virgins wedded to Christ withdraw their sex from procreation. Chapter LXI.

Finally, we shall become not angels, but like God’s angels. Chapter LXII.

Therefore the conclusion is that the flesh will rise—indeed, all of it; indeed, the selfsame flesh; indeed, the whole of it. Chapter LXIII.
