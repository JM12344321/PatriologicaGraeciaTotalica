## Genesis 7:4

**Verse 4.** “I am bringing rain upon the earth for forty days and forty nights. And I will wipe away from the face of the earth every rising thing that I have made, from human being to livestock.” Why, indeed, did he destroy the multitudes of human beings by the flood? He wished to wipe away the race of Cain, but the tribe of the pious had also mingled with it; therefore it too shared in the punishment.

Wishing to make a certain new beginning of life, he preserved Noah and his children together with their wives. Noah was pious and righteous, had sprung from the piety of his relatives, and had hated the intermingling of lawlessness. Yet he did not do these things, as some say, from some anger and regret. These are human passions, while the divine nature is free from passions.

Moreover, regret is fitting for those who learn the nature of things after experience and then, learning by experience that they did not deliberate rightly, regret it. But God sees the things that will happen after many generations as though they had already happened. Since he foresees and foreknows, he directs all things in this way. Why, then, would he regret, when he orders all things in accordance with his own foreknowledge?

Consequently, in the case of God, regret is a change of dispensation. For he says, “I regret that I appointed Saul as king”—that is, “I resolved to remove this man and appoint another.” So also here: “I considered that I had made the human being” means, “I resolved to destroy the race of human beings.” But because he is humane, he preserved Noah as seed for human nature.

And because he created the kinds of irrational animals for the sake of human need, these also were destroyed together with human beings, except for those preserved with Noah in the ark. For the Master God commanded that two of every kind regarded as unclean should be saved, but seven of the clean. Since he was going to permit human beings to partake of meat, while those nourished in piety were also going to offer sacrifices to him, he commanded that more of the clean animals be preserved: the three pairs for the increase of the kind, and the one animal left over for sacrifice.

For immediately after the [cessation] of the punishment Noah offered God a sacrifice of thanksgiving “from all,” it says, “of the clean livestock and from all of the clean birds.” It is therefore clear that for this reason he commanded seven of each clean kind to be brought into the ark, so that Noah, by offering one animal from each kind, would not destroy the pairs.

[Translation note: The source has an anomalous word before “of the punishment.” The translation follows the contextually probable sense “cessation” and marks it as supplied.]

## Genesis 7:16

**Verse 16.** “And the Lord God closed the ark from the outside.” The securing of the door as it was being shut required a more divine power.

## Genesis 19:25–26

**Verse 25.** “And he overthrew these cities, all the surrounding country, and all those dwelling in the cities.”

**Verse 26.** “And his wife looked back, and she became a pillar of salt.” The Lord destroys not only the impious people but also their Sodomite and harmful foodstuffs. Among these is the vine about which it is written, “For their vine is from the vine of Sodom, and their branch from Gomorrah.”

At the same time as Lot reached the city in safety, fire was brought upon the cities—a sign of the coming consummation. She did not simply become a pillar, but especially a pillar of salt, because of the saying, “You are the salt of the earth.” The saying “But if the salt becomes tasteless?” may be applied to the pillar of salt. Lot did not go up to the mountain at once, as he had been told; rather, he first came to Zoar, because he lacked confidence about going up to the mountain.

## Genesis 23:15

**Verse 15.** “Land worth four hundred didrachmas.” It should be noted that four hundred is the price of a tomb, because both the number four and the number one hundred carry an afflictive significance. Search out similar things from the Scriptures, and you will find them.

## Genesis 37:10

**Verse 10.** “Will we indeed come—I, your mother, and your brothers—to bow down before you upon the ground?” Clearly Joseph’s mother had died before the dreams. Can we perhaps, then, say that Mary and Joseph are being prophesied as coming to bow down before Christ?

## Genesis 43:22

**Verse 22.** “But he said to them, ‘May God be gracious to you; do not be afraid.’” This man appears to have been helped toward reverence for God through his life with Joseph.

## Genesis 46:34

**Verse 34.** “We are men who raise livestock.” Every shepherd of sheep is an abomination to Egyptians, though not to Hebrews. If, then, he has been set over us as a shepherd, he is an abomination to Egyptians; but if as a hired servant, perhaps he is even a friend to Egyptians.
