## CHAPTER XVIII

**ARGUMENT.—He shows that this God, not content with the knowledge of himself implanted in human beings, wished to teach them by word and writing when they went astray. Since this was the prerogative of the Jews, these things required the Hebrew language; but because the records had been translated into Greek at Ptolemy’s command, they could readily be consulted, flashing with such force of truth that anyone who read or heard them would be compelled even to believe.**

But so that we might approach both God himself and his ordinances and purposes more fully and more deeply, he added a written instrument, in case anyone should wish to inquire about God, to find him after inquiry, to believe after finding, and to serve after believing.
