## Fragment 1

Jephthah made a vow to the Lord. If the Spirit of the Lord was on him, how could he make such an unfitting vow? A dog, too, might have met him on his return. The Spirit did not make a mistake: it came to help, not to prompt this improper vow. Jephthah himself went wrong by promising rashly.

<!-- source-locus: 2 -->
## Fragment 2

They told him, “Say *stachys*.” The pronunciation *stachys* is said to signify *sebēla*, namely *astachys*. The others said *sebēlō* and could not clearly pronounce *sēdēla*. Though they were Hebrews, their dialect’s short pronunciation distinguished them. Thus the test-word was *sebēla*: those questioned exposed themselves as not belonging to Jephthae’s people, but to the rebels, by saying *sedēlō*.

<!-- source-locus: 3 -->
## Fragment 3

His wife was barren and bore no child. The term “barren” here refers not to her nature, but to her present role or ministry.

<!-- source-locus: 4 -->
## Fragment 4

“No iron has touched my head, because I am God’s Nazirite.” Throughout Samson’s story, his hair seems to preserve his full strength.

<!-- source-locus: 5 -->
## Fragment 5

The same author says that in Syriac a Nazirite is called “the preserved one.” God commands Samson not to conform to the Israelite custom of that period: they shaved their heads so as to be readily recognizable by everyone.

<!-- source-locus: 6 -->
## Fragment 6

He made an ephod and teraphim. The ephod was a priestly garment: when the priest put it on, the grace of the Holy Spirit came upon him. Teraphim was something of this sort, but not divine; it was idolatrous, as the books of Kingdoms show.

<!-- source-locus: 7 -->
## Fragment 7

The Benjaminites came out. Why were the Israelites defeated when their cause for attacking Benjamin was just? Clearly, earlier offenses still counted against them. As the Psalm says, “When I receive the appointed time, I will judge uprightly.” After Israel had been defeated once and then a second time, wrath turned against the wrongdoers, and the Israelites won a righteous victory.
