When the little work is nearing completion, the points that recur must now be reconsidered briefly; but some things that have occurred more often must be passed over. [Col. 0503B] I am weary of still contending over the Law, since I have so often [Note: [Col. 0503D] *Contending*, *as often as*, Semler.] proved that its cessation [Note: [Col. 0503D] *Withdrawal*, Franeker edition.] furnishes no argument for a different God, seeing that it was proclaimed in Christ and promised anew in relation to Christ by the Creator, even though the Apostle himself [Note: [Col. 0503D] *Even though the Epistle itself*, Latin edition.] appears in many places to exclude the Law (Rom. I, II, III, V, VI, VII). But we have also already shown repeatedly that God is presented by the Apostle as judge, as avenger in his capacity as judge, and as Creator in his capacity as avenger.

Accordingly, when he says here, “For I am not ashamed of the Gospel; for it is the power of God unto salvation to everyone who believes, to the Jew and to the Greek, because in it the righteousness of God is revealed, from faith to faith,” he undoubtedly assigns both the Gospel and salvation to the just God, not to the good God—if I may put it that way, according to the heretic’s distinction—who transfers people from faith in the Law to faith in the Gospel: from faith, of course, in his own [Col. 0503C] Law to faith in his own Gospel. For he also says that wrath [Note: [Col. 0503D] Franeker adds *of God*.] is revealed from heaven against the impiety and injustice of men who hold the truth in injustice. [Note: [Col. 0503D] *Who may hold*, Oberthür.] Whose God’s wrath is it? The Creator’s, of course. Therefore the truth also belongs to him whose wrath it is, since that wrath is to be revealed in vindication of the truth.

Moreover, by adding, “But we know that the judgment of God is according to truth,” he both established the wrath from which judgment comes on behalf of the truth, and once again confirmed the truth as belonging to the same God whose wrath he established by establishing his judgment. It would be otherwise if the wrathful Creator were avenging the truth of another God that had been suppressed in injustice.

[Note: [Col. 0503D] What enormous gaps Marcion made, especially in this Epistle! Tertullian indicates that he is treating only those passages that Marcion did not see fit to erase, although he removed whatever he wished. Consequently, he adduces very few passages from this exceedingly long Epistle, as does also blessed Epiphanius. To the same point belong the words he has in the following chapter: “Here too I leap over a very extensive section,” etc. PAM.]

How enormous the gaps are that Marcion made, especially in this Epistle, by removing whatever he wished, will become evident from the completeness of our own Scriptural corpus. For my purposes it is enough to take up the passages he did not see fit to erase, regarding them as instances of his negligence and blindness.

[Col. 0503D] For if God will judge the secrets of men—both of those who sinned under the Law and of those who sinned without the Law, because even these, though ignorant of the Law, naturally do the things that belong to the Law—[Col. 0504A] then surely the God who judges will be the one to whom both the Law and nature itself belong, since nature serves in place of the Law for those ignorant of the Law. [Note: [Col. 0503D] *Being ignorant, naturally*, Ciacconius.]

But how will he judge? “According to the Gospel,” he says, “through Christ.” Therefore both the Gospel and Christ belong to him to whom the Law and nature belong, since the Law and nature will be vindicated through the Gospel and through Christ, by that judgment of God which, as stated above, is “according to truth.” Therefore, just as wrath is revealed from heaven in defense of that truth only by the God of wrath, so too the sense here, cohering with the preceding one in which the Creator’s judgment is proclaimed, cannot be referred to another God who neither judges nor grows angry. It must instead be referred to him to whom these things—by which I mean judgment and wrath—belong; [Note: [Col. 0504D] *While* absent in the Paris edition.] [Note: [Col. 0504D] *Wrath*, Semler.] and therefore those things through which these actions are to be carried out—the Gospel and Christ—must likewise belong to him.

For this reason the Apostle inveighs against transgressors of the Law who teach that one must not steal and yet steal themselves. He does so as a man of [Col. 0504B] the God of the Law, not as one assailing the Creator himself in such terms because, while forbidding theft, the Creator allegedly ordered the Egyptians to be defrauded of their gold and silver, just as the other objections are turned back against him. Evidently the Apostle was afraid to revile openly the God from whom he was not afraid to defect! Indeed, he had attacked the Jews so thoroughly that he pressed upon them the prophetic rebuke: “Because of you the name of God is blasphemed.” How perverse it would therefore be for him himself to blaspheme the God whose being blasphemed is the reason for which he reproaches the wicked!

He also prefers circumcision of the heart to uncircumcision. In the presence of the God [Note: [Col. 0504D] Franeker adds *for*.] of the Law, circumcision is made of the heart, not the flesh; in spirit, not in letter. But if this is Jeremiah’s circumcision (Jer. IV, 4), “And you shall circumcise the foreskin of your heart,” as it is also that of Moses (Deut. X, 16), [Note: [Col. 0504D] *Moses*, Pamelius.] “You shall circumcise your hardness of heart,” then the spirit [Col. 0504C] that circumcises the heart will belong to the same God whose letter cuts the flesh; and the Jews who are Jews inwardly will belong to the same God whose Jew is a Jew outwardly. For the Apostle would not even have wished to use the name “Jew” if he had not been a servant of the Jews’ God.

Then there was the Law; now there is the righteousness of God through faith in Christ. What is the nature of this distinction? Did your god serve the Creator’s dispensation, granting time to him and to his Law? Or did the former dispensation belong to him to whom the present one also belongs? The Law belongs to him to whom faith belongs. Christ marks a distinction between dispensations, not between gods.

[Note: [Col. 0504D] He admonishes those justified by faith, etc., to have peace with God. In these words at the beginning of chapter V, after omitting the whole of chapter IV of the Epistle to the Romans, the Author reads ἔχομεν, “we have,” in place of what others read as ἔχωμεν, “let us have.” PAM.]

He admonishes those justified by faith in Christ, not by the Law, to have peace with God. With which God? With the one whose enemies we never were, or with the one whose Law and nature we rebelled against? For if peace is properly made with him against whom there was war, then we shall also be justified before him, and Christ will belong to him by faith in whom we shall be justified, since it is proper that those who were once his enemies should be restored to peace with him.

“But the Law,” [Col. 0504D] he says, “entered in alongside, so that the offense might abound.” Why? “So that grace,” he says, “might superabound.” Whose God’s grace is this, if not that of the God whose Law it is? Unless, perhaps, the Creator inserted [Note: [Col. 0504D] *Inserted the Law*. That is, says Rhenanus, he interjected, interposed, introduced, or put it into the middle; for here this is the meaning of *intercalare*. Macrobius mentions this word and also discusses at length the principle of intercalation. Julius Caesar established that one day should be intercalated, that is, inserted, every fourth year; hence Pliny calls the bissextile year “intercalary,” and Livy called a month “intercalary.” The Greeks call it ἐμβόλιμον and ἐμβολιμαῖον, “intercalated.” PAM.] the Law [Note: [Col. 0504D] *The Law* is absent in Rhenanus.] [Col. 0505A] precisely in order to transact the business of another god’s grace—and indeed that of a rival god, not to say an unknown one—so that, just as sin had reigned under the Creator unto death, grace too might reign through righteousness unto life through Jesus Christ, the Creator’s adversary!

For this purpose, then, the Creator’s Law had shut up all things under offense, reduced the whole world to guilt, and stopped every mouth, so that no one might boast through the Law, in order that grace might be reserved for the glory of Christ—not the Creator’s Christ, but Marcion’s. Here too I can lay a foundation concerning the substance of Christ, in anticipation of the question that follows. For he says that we have died to the Law. [Note: [Col. 0505D] *For he says that we have died to the Law.* This too is spoken from Marcion’s point of view and derives from his corrupted text, since Marcion had rendered it “we have died to the Law,” where the Apostle says “we have died to sin,” at the beginning of Romans VI. But Tertullian rightly refutes him from the words that follow, where he adds that Christ rose from the dead. PAM.] Therefore, it is through the body of Christ. [Note: [Col. 0505D] *To the Law through the body of Christ. Therefore*, Ciacconius.] It may, however, be contended that a “body” is not necessarily flesh. But whatever the substance may be, when the Apostle names the body of the one whom he goes on to say rose from the dead, [Col. 0505B] no other body can be understood than the body of flesh, against which the law of death was pronounced.

See, moreover, how he bears testimony to the Law and excuses it from being the cause of the offense. “What, then, shall we say? Is the Law sin? Far from it!” Blush, Marcion! “Far from it!” The Apostle abominates the accusation against the Law. “But I did not know the offense except through the Law.” O supreme commendation of the Law, through which it became clear that the offense had been hidden! [Note: [Col. 0505D] *It was permitted*, Semler and Oberthür.] [Note: [Col. 0505D] *Hidden*, Ciacconius.] Therefore it was not the Law that deceived, but sin, through the opportunity afforded by the commandment. Why do you impute to the God of the Law what the Apostle does not dare impute to his Law?

Indeed, he heaps praise upon it: “The Law is holy, and its commandment is just and good.” If he venerates the Creator’s Law in this manner, I do not know how he can destroy the Creator himself. Who can distinguish two gods, one just and the other good, when the God whose commandment [Col. 0505C] is both good and just must be believed to be both? And if he also confirms that the Law is spiritual, then surely it is also prophetic, and surely also figurative. From this too, therefore, I must establish that Christ was proclaimed figuratively in the Law, which is why he could not be recognized by all the Jews.

## CHAPTER XIV

*(Rom. VIII, X, XI, XII, XIII, XIV)*

If the Father sent him “in the likeness of sinful flesh,” the flesh that appeared in him is not for that reason to be called a phantom. For above, the Apostle ascribed sin to the flesh, made it the law of sin dwelling in his members and opposing the law of his understanding. The Son was therefore sent in the likeness of sinful flesh so that he might redeem sinful flesh by means of a like substance—that is, a fleshly one—which would be similar to sinful flesh [Col. 0506A] although it was not itself sinful. For this too will be the power of God: to accomplish salvation in an equal substance. It would be no great thing if the Spirit of God healed flesh; the great thing is if flesh like sinful flesh heals it, being flesh but not sinful.

Thus the likeness will pertain to the designation “sin,” not to any falsity of substance. For he would not have added “sinful” if he had wished a likeness of substance to be understood in such a way as to deny its reality. He would merely have written “flesh,” without also adding “sinful.” But since he constructed the phrase in this way, “sinful flesh,” [Note: [Col. 0505D] *He constructed it in this way*, Semler; *he set both beneath*, Latin edition.] he both confirmed the substance—that is, flesh—and referred the likeness to the defect of the substance—that is, to sin.

Suppose now that the likeness is said to be a likeness of substance: the reality of the substance will not for that reason be denied. Why, then, is something real also like? Because it is indeed real, but not from seed; like in condition, but [Note: [Col. 0505D] *And*, Latin edition.] [Col. 0506B] real in origin, and not unlike in reality. [Note: [Col. 0505D] *Real from likeness*, Latin edition.] Besides, there is no likeness between contrary things. Spirit would not be called a likeness of flesh, because neither could flesh receive the likeness of spirit. It would instead be called a phantom if it appeared to be what it was not. A likeness, however, is spoken of when a thing is what it appears to be; for it exists, since it is the equal of another.

[Note: [Col. 0506D] *But a phantom.* Tertullian discussed this at greater length above against Marcion, who attributed to Christ phantasmal flesh and a phantom, not a real body. LE PR.]

But a phantom, [Note: [Col. 0506D] *But* neglected by others.] because [Note: [Col. 0506D] *Because*, other editions.] it is only an apparition of this kind, is not a likeness. [Note: [Col. 0506D] For *only this*, the Latin edition proposes *an apparition*.]

Here too, moreover, the Apostle himself explains how he does not wish us to be “in the flesh” although we are in the flesh: namely, so that we may not be engaged in [Note: [Col. 0506D] *From*, Semler.] the works of the flesh. He thereby shows that it was in this sense that he wrote (1 Cor. XV, 50), “Flesh and blood cannot attain the kingdom of God.” He was not condemning the substance but its works; and since we can refrain from these while still situated in the flesh, they will pertain not to the guilt of the substance but to our manner of life. [Col. 0506C]

Likewise, if “the body is dead because of the offense”—so fully is death the death not of the soul but of the body—“but the spirit is life because of righteousness,” then life because of righteousness will surely come to that to which death came because of the offense, namely, the body. For nothing is restored to someone unless he has lost something else; and so the resurrection of the dead will be a resurrection of bodies.

For he also [Note: [Col. 0506D] Pamelius omits *also*.] adds: “He who raised Christ from the dead will also give life to your mortal bodies.” Thus he has both confirmed the resurrection of the flesh—apart from which no other “body” can be spoken of, nor any other thing understood as “mortal”—and proved Christ’s bodily substance. For our mortal bodies will be given life in the same way as he too was raised, and not otherwise in the same way [Col. 0507A] except because [Note: [Col. 0507D] *Which*, Junius.] he was raised in a body.

Here too I leap over a very extensive section cut away from Scripture; but I seize upon the Apostle’s testimony concerning Israel, that “they have zeal for God”—for their own God, surely—“but not according to knowledge.” “For being ignorant of God,” he says, “and seeking to establish their own righteousness, they did not subject themselves to the righteousness of God. For Christ is the end of the Law unto righteousness [Note: [Col. 0507D] *Righteousness*, Semler and Oberthür.] for everyone who believes.”

Here will be the heretic’s argument: the Jews were allegedly ignorant of a higher God, against whom they established their own righteousness—that is, the righteousness of their own Law—by not receiving Christ as the end of the Law. Why, then, does the Apostle also bear witness to their zeal for their own God, if he does not reproach them for ignorance concerning that same God? They were indeed moved by zeal for God, but not according to knowledge: they were ignorant of him, of course, insofar as they were ignorant of the consummation of his dispensations in [Col. 0507B] Christ, who was to establish an end for the Law, [Note: [Col. 0507D] *Who was to establish*, Rigault; *I shall establish*, Venetian edition.] and in this way they defended their own righteousness against him.

Indeed, the Creator himself testifies to their ignorance concerning himself (Isa. I, 3): “Israel did not recognize me, and my people did not understand me.” He also testifies that they preferred to establish their own righteousness, “teaching as doctrines the commandments of men” (Isa. XXIX, 13), and likewise that they “assembled together against the Lord and against his Christ” (Ps. II, 2), evidently out of ignorance.

Nothing, therefore, can be interpreted as referring to another God when it properly applies to the Creator. Otherwise, the Apostle would also have undeservedly censured the Jews for ignorance concerning an unknown god. For how had they sinned if they established the righteousness of their own God against one whom they did not know?

Indeed, he exclaims: [Note: [Col. 0507D] “O depth of riches.” Ὢ βάθος, “O depth,” for which the Vulgate has “O height.” LE PR.] “O depth of the riches and wisdom of God, and how unsearchable [Note: [Col. 0507D] *Unsearchable.* Since the Greek has ἀνεξιχνίαστοι, that is, “not traceable,” many rightly judge that one should read *unsearchable*, that is, “which cannot be searched out.” LE PR.] [Col. 0507C] are his ways!” From where comes this outburst? Evidently from his recollection of the Scriptures which he had reviewed above, and from his contemplation of the mysteries he had discussed above concerning faith in Christ as coming from the Law.

If Marcion deliberately did not [Note: [Col. 0507D] Pamelius omits *not*, but it is present in the Codex Wouwerianus.] erase these words, why does his apostle exclaim while contemplating no riches belonging to a god as poor and destitute as one who created nothing, foretold nothing, and in short possessed nothing, since he descended into the property of another? Yet the Creator had long ago promised resources and riches that were then hidden but have now been disclosed (Isa. XLV, 3): “And I will give them hidden treasures; I will open invisible things to them.” From this, therefore, came the exclamation: “O depth of the riches and wisdom of God!”—the God whose treasures were now being laid open. That comes from Isaiah, [Note: [Col. 0507D] *Were laid open from Isaiah, so that*, Latin edition.] and what follows comes from the written corpus of the same prophet (Isa. XL, 13): “For who has known the mind of the Lord, or who has been [Col. 0507D] his counselor? Who has given to him, and it shall be repaid to him?” You who have removed so much from the Scriptures, why did you preserve these words, as though they too did not belong to the Creator?

Let us, to be sure, examine the precepts of the new god. “Hating evil,” he says, “and cleaving to good.” It is apparently otherwise with the Creator: “Remove evil from among you,” and (Ps. XXXIII, 14), “Turn away from evil and do good.” “Affectionate toward one another in brotherly love.” This is surely not identical with, “You shall love your neighbor as yourself”! “Rejoicing in hope”—in God, of course: “For it is good to hope in the Lord [Note: [Col. 0507D] *In the Lord*, Oberthür.] rather than to hope in rulers” (Ps. CXVII). “Enduring tribulation”: “For the Lord will hear you in the day of tribulation” (Ps. XIX, 1). There you have the Psalm.

“Bless, and do not curse.” Who will teach this except the one who created all things by blessings? “Not thinking lofty thoughts, but associating with the humble, [Note: [Col. 0508D] *Pursuing*, Venetian edition.] lest you be wise in your own estimation”; for through Isaiah they hear, “Woe to them” (Isa. V, 21). “Repay no one evil for evil”; and (Lev. XIX, 18), “You shall not remember your brother’s malice.” [Col. 0508B] [Note: [Col. 0508D] *You shall not remember*, Junius.] “Neither avenge yourselves: for vengeance is mine, [Deut. XXXII, 35] and I will avenge, says the Lord.” “Have peace with all [Note: [Col. 0508D] Semler omits *all*.] people.” Thus even the legal principle of retaliation did not permit repayment of injury; rather, by fear of repayment, it restrained the initiation of injury.

Rightly, therefore, the Apostle summed up the Creator’s whole discipline in the Creator’s chief commandment: “You shall love your neighbor as yourself.” If this fulfillment of the Law comes from the Law itself, I no longer know who the God of the Law may be—I fear it may be Marcion’s god! But if Christ’s Gospel is fulfilled by this commandment, while Christ is not [Note: [Col. 0508D] *And*, Latin edition.] the Creator’s, what are we now arguing about? Did Christ say, or did he not say, “I did not come to dissolve the Law, but to fulfill it” (Matt. V, 17)? The man from Pontus labored in vain to deny this saying. [Note: [Col. 0508D] *The man from Pontus labored.* Rhenanus very well conjectured that *Ponticus*, “the man from Pontus,” should be read in place of *Pontus*; that is, Marcion, who originated in Pontus. LE PR.] If the Gospel did not fulfill the Law, behold, the Law has fulfilled the Gospel.

It is well, moreover, that in the conclusion he also threatens the tribunal of Christ—of Christ as judge and avenger, and therefore as the Creator’s—at any rate establishing that the one whom he declares must be feared is to be won over, even if he were preaching another.

## CHAPTER XV

Nor will it be irksome to attend to the shorter Epistles: there is flavor even in a few words.

The Jews had killed their own prophets. I can say: What concern is that of the apostle of another God, and indeed of the supremely good God, who is said not even to condemn the sins of his own people and who himself, by destroying those same prophets, in a certain sense puts them to death? For what evil [Note: [Col. 0508D] Semler omits *for*.] did Israel commit against him if it killed those whom he too rejected, if he had previously pronounced a hostile sentence against them? Israel did, however, sin against their own God. It was he [Note: [Col. 0508D] Semler adds *and*.] who reproached them for the iniquity, [Col. 0508D] to whom the injured party belonged—certainly anyone other than the injured party’s adversary.

Nor would the Apostle have burdened them by also charging them with the murder of the Lord, saying that they “killed both the Lord [Col. 0509A] and their own prophets”—although “their own” is an addition made by the heretic. For what would be so bitter in the fact that they killed Christ, the preacher of another God, when they had butchered the prophets of their own God? The force of the aggravation, however, lies in their having killed both the Lord and his servants. Finally, if they killed the Christ of one God and the prophets of another, the Apostle equated the acts of impiety; he did not aggravate them. But they ought not to have been equated. Therefore the charge could not have been aggravated unless both offenses, under their respective descriptions, had been committed against the same Lord. Christ and the prophets therefore belong to the same God.

You would recognize what holiness he calls the will of God from the contrary things he prohibits. He says, “To abstain [Note: [Col. 0509D] Oberthür, following Semler, adds *for*.] from fornication,” not from marriage. “That each person should know how to treat his own vessel with honor.” How? By not treating it “in lust, as the Gentiles do”; but even among the Gentiles lust [Col. 0509B] is attributed not to marriage but to extraordinary, unnatural, and monstrous practices.

Holiness is also contrary to the shame and uncleanness [Note: [Col. 0509D] “Holiness is contrary to the shame of lust,” etc. Those who read *luxury* do not adequately agree with the text, and wrongly fasten upon Tertullian the charge of having held a poor view of marriage; for the particle “not,” which follows, removes every doubt. LE PR.] [Note: [Col. 0509D] Rhenanus reads: *Monstrous. Luxury is also contrary to shame and uncleanness; it does not …*.] of lust. Such holiness does not exclude marriage but lust; it treats our vessel with the honor of marriage.

I would discuss this passage while preserving the preeminence of the other—that is, fuller—holiness, preferring continence and virginity to marriage, [Note: [Col. 0509D] *Preferring continence and virginity to marriage.* Let the heretics who assail vows of virginity and continence attend to this passage and to many others in this writer, in *On the Apparel of Women*, *On the Veiling of Virgins*, and Book I *To His Wife*. LE PR.] but not to marriage as though it were prohibited. For I am refuting destroyers of the God of marriage, not followers of chastity.

He says that those who remain until the coming [Note: [Col. 0509D] *At the coming*, Semler.] of Christ, together with those dead in Christ who will rise first, will be caught up in the clouds into the air [Note: [Col. 0509D] *Air*, Oberthür.] to meet the Lord. I recognize that, since these things had already been foreseen at that time, the heavenly substances marvel through Isaiah at Jerusalem herself, which is above (Gal. IV, 26), and declare (Isa. [Col. 0509C] LX, 8): “Who are these who fly here like clouds, like doves with their young, toward me?”

If Christ prepared this ascent for us, he will be the Christ of whom Hosea [Note: [Col. 0509D] Junius proposes *Amos*, and rightly.] says (Amos IX, 6): “He who builds his ascent into the heavens”—surely for himself and his own people. From whom, then, shall I now expect it, except from the one from whom I heard these things?

Whose spirit does he prohibit from being quenched? And whose prophecies does he forbid to be treated as nothing? According to Marcion, surely not the Creator’s Spirit or the Creator’s prophecies. For by destroying them he has already quenched them and reduced them to nothing; and he cannot prohibit people from doing what he himself has done, treating them as nothing.

Marcion is therefore under obligation to exhibit today in his own church the spirit of his god, which must not be quenched, and prophecies which must not be regarded as nothing. If he has exhibited what he thinks answers to this description, let him know [Note: [Col. 0510D] *Let you know*, Venetian edition.] that we shall challenge whatever it is to conform to the pattern of spiritual [Col. 0509D] and prophetic grace and power: [Col. 0510A] it must foretell the future, reveal the secrets of the heart, and explain mysteries. Since he has produced and proved nothing of this kind, we shall bring forward both the Creator’s Spirit and the Creator’s prophecies, while preaching in accordance with the Creator himself.

It will thus be established which things the Apostle [Note: [Col. 0510D] *The Apostle*, Pamelius.] was speaking about: namely, those things that were to be present in the church of his God—the God who, because he exists, also has his Spirit [Note: [Col. 0510D] *To them*, Semler, incorrectly.] at work and his promise being fulfilled.

Come now, you who deny the salvation of the flesh and, whenever “body” is mentioned in a passage of this sort, interpret it as some other thing unknown to me rather than as the substance of the flesh: how did the Apostle distinguish all three by definite names as distinct substances, yet include them all in a single prayer for salvation, wishing that “our spirit and body and soul may be preserved blameless at the coming of our Lord and Savior Christ”? [Col. 0510B]

For he named both soul and body as two things and as different things. Although the soul too has a body of some sort, of a quality proper to itself, as does the spirit, nevertheless, when body and soul are named distinctly, the soul possesses its own proper designation and does not require the shared designation “body.” That designation is left for the flesh, which, because it is not named by its proper term, must necessarily make use of the common term. Indeed, after spirit and soul, I see no other substance in a human being to which the designation “body” may be applied except the flesh. He understands flesh under the name of the body as often as flesh is not explicitly named—and much more so here, where that which is called “body” is designated by its own name.

## CHAPTER XVI

*(On the Second Epistle to the Thessalonians)*

We are compelled to repeat certain things over and over [Col. 0510C] so that we may confirm the points connected with them. Here too we say that the Apostle presents the Lord as the requiter of both kinds of merit: either the Creator or—what Marcion would reject—one equal to the Creator. With him [Note: [Col. 0510D] “It is just that affliction be repaid to those who afflict us.” This is how he renders 2 Thessalonians I. In Greek: ἂν ἀποδοῦναι τοῖς θλίβουσιν ἡμᾶς, “to repay those who afflict us.” PAM.] it is just that affliction be repaid to those who afflict us, and rest to us who are afflicted, “at the revelation of the Lord Jesus, coming from heaven with the angels of his power and in a flame of fire.”

But by deleting “flame” and “fire,” [Note: [Col. 0510D] *But by deleting “flame” and “fire.”* From this alone it is evident that what blessed Epiphanius writes against Marcion is true: that he himself treats nothing from this Epistle, because Marcion had already perverted it excessively. PAM.] the heretic extinguished them, lest he should, of course, make Christ belong to our God. The futility of the erasure nevertheless shines through.

For when the Apostle writes that the Lord will come to exact vengeance upon those who do not know God and those who do not obey the Gospel—people whom he says will pay a penalty, [Note: [Col. 0510D] *A penalty, whom he says*, Latin edition.] namely, eternal destruction from the face of the Lord and from the glory of his might—it follows that he should introduce the flame of fire, since the Lord is coming to punish. Thus, even in this passage and against Marcion’s will, Christ belongs to the God who burns, [Col. 0510D] [Col. 0511A] [Note: [Col. 0511C] Pamelius omits *is*.] and in the other respect he belongs to the Creator, because he takes vengeance even upon those who do not know the Lord—that is, the Gentiles.

For the Apostle separately listed those who do not obey the Gospel, whether sinful Christians or Jews. Now to exact penalties from Gentiles who perhaps do not know the Gospel is not characteristic of Marcion’s god, who is naturally unknown, revealed nowhere except in the Gospel, and incapable of being known by everyone. Knowledge of the Creator, however, is owed even by nature: he is to be understood from his works, and from that point a fuller knowledge of him is to be sought. It therefore belongs to the Creator to punish even those ignorant of God, since it is not permissible for him to be unknown. [Note: [Col. 0511C] *To be ignorant*, Pamelius.]

The very words he uses—“From the face of the Lord and from the glory of his might”—are Isaiah’s words (Isa. II, 19), and for this very reason they savor of the same Lord “rising up to crush the earth.”

Who, moreover, is [Note: [Col. 0511D] *The man of offense.* He immediately explains sufficiently who the man of offense is: namely, Antichrist. By that name he calls the Marcionites, who appear to have perpetrated the same things that were foretold concerning Antichrist. LE PR.] “the man of offense, the son of perdition,” [Col. 0511B] who must be revealed before the Lord’s coming, “exalting himself above everything called God and every object of religion,” taking his seat [Note: [Col. 0511C] *Obtaining*, other editions.] in the temple of God and boasting that he himself is God?

According to us, he is Antichrist, as the old and new prophecies teach; so too the apostle John, who says that antichrists have already gone out into the world (1 John IV, 1–2), spirits who are forerunners of Antichrist, denying that Christ came in the flesh and dissolving Jesus—that is, dissolving him in relation to God the Creator. According to Marcion, however, I do not know whether he may be [Note: [Col. 0511C] *Unless he is the Christ*, Codex Wouwerianus; *whether he may be the Christ*, Semler.] the Creator’s Christ, since according to Marcion he has not yet [Note: [Col. 0511C] Pamelius omits *for*.] come.

Whichever of the two he is, [Note: [Col. 0511C] Franeker omits *is*.] I ask why he comes “with all power and signs and lying wonders.” “For this reason,” he says: “because they did not receive love of the truth so that they might be saved; and for this reason there will be [Note: [Col. 0511C] *He will give*, Latin edition.] for them an impulse of deception, so that [Col. 0511C] all who did not believe the truth but consented to injustice may be judged.”

Therefore, if he is [Note: [Col. 0511D] *And*, Pamelius.] Antichrist according to the Creator [Note: [Col. 0512C] *According to us, and God*, Codex Wouwerianus.], then God will be the Creator who sends him to drive into error those who did not believe the truth so that they might be saved. Both truth and salvation will belong to the same God who takes vengeance by sending error—that is, to the Creator, to whom this very jealousy appropriately belongs: to deceive by error those whom he did not win through truth.

But if he is not Antichrist according to us, then according to Marcion he is the Creator’s Christ. And how can it be fitting that, to avenge his own truth, Marcion’s god should subject Christ to the Creator? [Note: [Col. 0512C] *The Creator’s*, Codex Wouwerianus.]

But even if [Note: [Col. 0512C] Pamelius omits *if*.] Marcion agrees that the passage concerns Antichrist, I would similarly ask: [Col. 0512A] how is it that Antichrist needs Satan, an angel of the Creator, and is killed by him, while having the function of carrying out the operation of deception on the Creator’s behalf?

In sum, if it is beyond doubt that the angel, the truth, and salvation belong to the same God to whom wrath, jealousy, and the sending of deception against despisers and deserters also belong—even against those who are ignorant, so that Marcion must now retreat from his position and concede that God is also jealous—which God will more deservedly be angry?

I think it is the one who, from the beginning of things, prepared nature [Note: [Col. 0512C] *Of nature*, Codex Wouwerianus.] through works, benefits, plagues, proclamations, [Note: [Col. 0512C] *Of proclamations*, Pamelius.] and witnesses so that he might be recognized, and yet was not recognized. Or is it the one who was brought forward a single time through one solitary [Note: [Col. 0512C] *United*, Codex Wouwerianus.] instrument of the Gospel—an instrument itself uncertain and not openly proclaiming another God?

Thus, to the one to whom vengeance appropriately belongs, the grounds of vengeance will also belong: by which I mean the Gospel, truth, and salvation. [Col. 0512B] Moreover, the command that anyone who wishes to eat must work belongs to the discipline of the God who commanded that the ox treading out grain be left unmuzzled.
