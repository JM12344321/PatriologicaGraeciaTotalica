## CHAPTER XXI

> **Note [Col. 0576B]:** Namely, because by his origin among psychic beings he was too weak to apprehend spiritual things. Now, ridiculing the foolish consequences of their doctrine, he says that from that psychic substance, by the circumstance of weakness, the Demiurge derived something weak; and that his weakness consisted in approaching spiritual things—that is, that he was too weak to approach them, unable and incapable of doing so. ## CHAPTER XXII

## CHAPTER XXIII

They place the citadels of the individual powers within the following boundaries. At the very summit of the heights presides the thirtyfold Pleroma, with Horos marking its outermost line. ## CHAPTER XXVII

[Col. 0581A]

I now return to Christ. Into him some insert Jesus with the same license with which they stuff the spiritual seed into the psychic element along with the breath—contriving some sort of stuffed beings, both their men and their gods.

> **Note [Col. 0581D]:** “Into whom they insert Jesus with such license.” Receiving no assistance from the manuscripts, we have written “into whom,” etc. Furthermore, the Valentinians insert as Savior into the Demiurge’s psychic Christ that Jesus who consists of the finest contributions from all the Aeons, thus making a stuffed Christ; and in the same way they cram the spiritual seed into the psychic element. **RHEN.**

For the Demiurge, they say, has his own Christ, his natural son.

> **Note [Col. 0581B]:** Rhenanus, Semler, and Oberthür read “also.”

He was, in short, emitted by the Demiurge as a psychic being and proclaimed through the prophets. He was placed within the questions concerning prepositions: that is, he was brought forth *through* a virgin, not *from* a virgin, because, having been conveyed into the virgin, he emerged by a mode of transit rather than generation—through her, not from her; she served not as his mother but as his route.

Upon this Christ, therefore, at the sacrament of baptism, the Savior descended in the likeness of a dove.

> **Note [Col. 0581D]:** “The Savior then descended at the sacrament of baptism.” The old manuscript does not have *Soterem* (“Savior”) but *Jem*, corrupted from an older exemplar in which was written *IHM* or *Ihm*, which regularly signifies Jesus in manuscripts. Nor should anything else be read here, although Irenaeus wrote “Savior.” **RHEN.**
>
> **Note [Col. 0581B]:** The manuscripts read *Jen* or *lem*; Paris reads “Jesus.”

For, according to the figure of the principal Tetrad, they cram him with four substances: the spiritual substance from Achamoth, the psychic from the Demiurge, the bodily substance of ineffable construction,

and the soteric—that is, the dove-like—substance.

The Savior remained in Christ, impassible, inviolable,

and ungraspable. Consequently, when they came to seize him,

> **Note [Col. 0582B]:** Junius reads “when they were coming.”

he departed from him at the judicial examination before Pilate. Likewise, the Mother’s seed did not admit injuries, being equally unsusceptible of subjection and unknown even to the Demiurge himself. But the psychic and fleshly Christ suffered, as an outline

> **Note [Col. 0582B]:** Junius reads “delineation.”

Thus they force everything into images—being, plainly, themselves imaginary Christians.

>
> **Note [Col. 0582C]:** “Christians.” Irenaeus, *Against Heresies* 1.7.2:

Nor, they say, did the seed from the Mother suffer, for it too was impassible, being spiritual and invisible even to the Demiurge himself. According to them, therefore, it was the psychic Christ who suffered … so that through him the Mother might display the type of the Christ above, of him who was stretched upon the Cross and formed Achamoth with the formation according to substance. According to these people, however, the psychic Christ suffered … so that through him the Mother might display the type of the higher Christ, of him who was stretched upon the Cross and formed Achamoth with the formation according to substance. with all his powers, as the centurion of the Gospel. Enlightened by him concerning everything [Col. 0583A], he also learns from him his own hope: that he will succeed to the Mother’s place. Thus, from that time onward secure, he continues the administration

## CHAPTER XXIX

I shall now gather together, from its scattered presentation, what they teach concerning the ordering of the whole human race, so as to bring the matter to a conclusion. Having initially professed a threefold nature, yet one united

> **Note [Col. 0583B]:** Others read “united.”

in Adam, they then divide it according to the particular properties of the races, having found an occasion for such a distinction in Adam’s posterity, which is likewise divided into three by differences of character. Cain, Abel, and Seth, as it were the fountains of the human race, they turn into a corresponding number of proofs of nature and essence.

The *choic* element, degenerate with respect to salvation,

> **Note [Col. 0584B]:** “The *choic* element, degenerate with respect to salvation.” Just as we call someone “degenerate” who is not nobly born, so Tertullian calls that “degenerate with respect to salvation” which has not been generated for salvation. **RHEN.**
>
> **Note [Col. 0584C]:** “Degenerate with respect to salvation.” Irenaeus: “It passes into corruption.” **RIG.**

they trace to Cain. The psychic, released to a hope lying midway,

> **Note [Col. 0584C]:** “Released to a hope lying midway.” That is, entrusted to its own liberty and choice. By “intermediate hope” he means what he called above an “uncertain outcome.” Meanwhile, however, he also alludes to the middle region, where the Valentinians said that the souls of the righteous find refreshment. “Released”: liberated. Thus Tertullian himself writes in *Against Marcion* IV, “released from the torment of ten months,” that is, liberated. **RIG.**
>
> **Note [Col. 0583B]:** Junius reads *delibratum*.

they connect with Abel. The spiritual, prejudged for certain salvation, they deposit in Seth.

Thus they also distinguish the souls themselves by a twofold property—as good and evil: according to the *choic* condition from Cain, the psychic from Abel,

> **Note [Col. 0583B]:** Junius adds “and.”

and the spiritual from Seth. By way of what supervenes they now add not a nature but a favor: what Achamoth pours from above

> **Note [Col. 0583B]:** Junius reads “by which Achamoth concerning …”

into good souls—that is, those enrolled in the psychic class.

> **Note [Col. 0584C]:** “Pours down upon good souls.” Irenaeus: “Achamoth sows the spiritual elements.” **RIG.**

For the *choic* race—that is, evil souls—can never receive saving things. They have declared its nature immutable and incapable of reformation.

That grain of spiritual seed, therefore, though small and slight when sown, grows and advances through its education—which is faith—

> **Note [Col. 0584C]:** “But through its education—which is faith,” etc. Thus below Tertullian uses the thing emitted for the act of emission, a usage also common in Tacitus and Livy. **RHEN.**
