## CHAPTER IX

How many supports of nature, how many privileges of grace, how many solemn rites of discipline, figures, preliminary constructions, and prayers have established the religious significance of water!

First, when the people, freely [Note: [Col. 1209C] “Freely” is absent from Fran.] delivered from Egypt (Exod. XIV), escaped the power [Note: [Col. 1209C] “Free from Egypt, the power,” etc. See the manuscript of Wouw.] of the Egyptian king by passing through the water, the water extinguished the king himself together with his entire army. What figure is more evident in the sacrament of Baptism? The nations are delivered from the world through water, and they leave behind in the water the devil, their former ruler, overwhelmed.

Again, water is healed from the defect of bitterness and restored to its proper [Note: [Col. 1209C] “Use.” Jun., Lat.] sweetness [Col. 1210A] by Moses’ wood. That wood was Christ, healing from himself the most unwholesome channels of the formerly poisoned and bitter nature—namely, the waters of Baptism (Exod. VII; Num. XX; 1 Cor. X). [Note: [Col. 1210C] Wouwer punctuates: “the channels; the most unwholesome waters of Baptism.”]

This is the water that flowed for the people from the rock that accompanied them. For if the rock is Christ, we undoubtedly see that water [Note: [Col. 1210C] “Waters,” manuscript of Wouw.; “water,” Jun.] is blessed for Baptism in Christ. How great is the grace of water in the presence of God and his Christ, for the confirmation of Baptism!

Christ is never without water. Indeed, he himself is baptized in water (Matt. III, X, XIV, XXVII; John III, IV, VII, XIII, XIX). When summoned to a wedding, he inaugurates the first rudiments of his power with water. When he speaks, he invites the thirsty to his everlasting water. When he teaches concerning love, he approves a cup of water offered to a brother [Note: [Col. 1210C] “Father,” Paris; others, “a poor person.” See the manuscript of Wouw., Rig.] among the works of affection. [Col. 1210B] At a well he recovers his strength; he gladly walks upon water and crosses it by boat; he ministers water to his disciples. The testimony of Baptism persists as far as the Passion: when he is handed over to the cross, water intervenes—the hands of Pilate know it; when he is wounded, water bursts from his side—the soldier’s lance knows it.

## CHAPTER X

As for us, in proportion to the small measure of our faith, we are able to judge with a correspondingly small measure of understanding that this Baptism was indeed divine by command, though not also by power. What the Lord was not yet conferring, the servant certainly could not furnish.

## CHAPTER XI

“But look,” he says, [Note: [Col. 1211C] “They say.” Paris.] “the Lord came and did not baptize.” For we read: “And yet he himself did not baptize, but his disciples did” (John IV), as though [Note: [Col. 1212C] “As though.” Paris, Jun., Corbinian.] John had proclaimed that he would himself baptize with his own hands (Luke III).

This is certainly not to be understood in that way; rather, the statement was made simply, according to ordinary usage. For example: “The emperor issued an edict,” or “the prefect beat someone with rods.” Does the emperor himself post it? Does the prefect himself administer the beating? The person for whom the service is performed is always said to perform the act. Thus “He will baptize you” must be understood to mean “You will be baptized through him” or “into him.”

But let no one be disturbed by the fact that he did not himself baptize. For into what would he have baptized? Into repentance? Why, then, would he have needed a forerunner? Into the remission of sins, which he granted by his word? Into himself, whom he concealed through humility? Into the Holy Spirit, who had not yet descended from the Father? Into the Church, which the Apostles had not yet established?

Therefore, his disciples baptized as ministers, just as John had previously baptized as a forerunner, with the same Baptism as John’s—lest anyone suppose it was with another, since no other existed except, later, Christ’s Baptism. At that time this could certainly not be given by the disciples, [Col. 1212C] since the Lord’s glory had not yet been fulfilled, nor had the efficacy of the bath been established through his Passion and Resurrection. For neither could our death be dissolved except through the Lord’s Passion (Rom. VI), nor could life be restored without his Resurrection. [Note: [Col. 1212C] “His rising.” Paris.]
