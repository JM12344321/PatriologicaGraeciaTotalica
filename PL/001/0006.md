[Col. 0064A] **1769–1776. FIRST HALLE EDITION.** Halle-on-Saale, Johann Christian Hendel, six volumes: Tertullian’s works edited by Johann Salomo Semler. Five books *Against Marcion* first appeared separately; the remaining works followed in four volumes, and volume VI supplies indexes and glossary. Semler began *Against Marcion* to make this outstanding work affordable for academic study; this became a complete edition. He restored Rhenanus’s first-edition text, corrected by his judgment and other editions, adding variants in doubtful places and rarely conjectures [Col. 0064B]. He used Rhenanus; Gagneius’s Paris 1566 octavo; Pamelius 1597; Junius’s Franeker edition; first Rigault; latest Venice; Latini; and Wouwer’s 1603 notes.

Volume II contains most polemical writings: *Prescriptions, Against Hermogenes, Valentinians, Praxeas, Jews; Scorpiace; Against the Psychics*. III: *Veiling of Virgins, Female Dress, Adornment of Women, To His Wife* I–II, *Exhortation to Chastity, Monogamy, Flight, To Scapula, Resurrection of the Flesh, Flesh of Christ*. IV: complete *Prayer* from Muratori’s Ambrosian supplements, *Testimony of the Soul, Repentance, Martyrs, Patience, Spectacles, Idolatry, Baptism, Soul, Soldier’s Crown, Modesty* [Col. 0064C]. V: *Apology, To the Nations* I–II, *Pallium*, and Semler’s dissertation on the variable, uncertain character of Tertullian’s books. VI: Semler’s preface and indexes of Scripture, memorable matters and opinions, and Tertullianic Latin, serving as a continuous commentary.

From volume IV Christian Gottfried Schütz managed the edition and prefaced it. Having gathered variants for earlier volumes, he continued but chose only the better ones [Col. 0064D], omitting useless readings, including many of Junius’s guesses. Where Rhenanus failed he followed Pamelius’s old reading unless intolerable. In V the *Apology* follows Havercamp, *Pallium* Salmasius, and *To the Nations* Rigault. Would that Schütz’s promised history of Tertullian’s works and editions had accompanied Semler’s illuminating dissertations. The Göttingen reviewer inaccurately calls chapter division peculiar to this edition; it had prevailed since Pamelius.

## ARTICLE IV. The new Paris edition

It has three components: prolegomena, appendices, and the complete text between them with variants and commentary [Col. 0069A]. First we preserve Pamelius’s *Life of Tertullian through Twenty-Four Years*, adding brief notes and correcting the badly disordered consular list, though its persistent one-year anticipation could not be removed without upheaval. Pamelius’s annals remain imperfect and erroneous; Nösselt’s learned dissertation on the true dates and doctrine of Tertullian’s surviving writings, excerpted from Lumper [Col. 0069B], supplies needed aid, though we reserve judgment on some doubtful opinions of both.

We retain Pamelius’s *Paradoxes with Antidote* and proverbial formulas explained by Rhenanus and Hoye, more from precedent than evident usefulness. After the *Apology* we reproduce Mosheim’s inquiry from Venice II on its date and Severus’s persecution.

Nothing fuller exists than Le Nourry’s Maurist apparatus [Col. 0069C]. Though examining only three books, he enters all Tertullian’s doctrine, confidently illuminates difficulties, restores the author’s sense, vindicates him from false charges, marks real errors and spurious works, and rejects bold fictions even when supported by editors. We reprint this rare treasury.

Five appendices conclude the work. I contains genuine or spurious poems with brief notes [Col. 0069D]. II gives the Vatican fragment published about 1630 by Suares and forgotten for two centuries. III gathers what Septimius spoke, translated, or wrote in Greek. IV lists lost works, fragments, and spuria. V supplies Corbinian Thomas’s rich notes on *Baptism* and *Repentance*, received too late for their place but in time to conclude the work. We thank J. B. Malou of Louvain [Col. 0070A].

The body has text, variants, and commentary. Following Lumper, Schoenemann, and Walch, we chiefly use Le Prieur’s 1664 Pamelius–Rigault edition, collating Rhenanus, Pamelius, Rigault, Paris, Venice, Halle, Würzburg, and Leopold’s recent Leipzig recension [Col. 0070B]. Continuous variant lists represent the Agobard, Leiden, Fulda, Vatican, Ambrosian, Wouwer, Latini, and Gelenius manuscripts and principal editions. Some will disdain these trifles; fairer readers will judge no smallest aid useless in recovering Tertullian’s voice from scribal license [Col. 0070C], since one rejected word may yield more light than a long note.

We also add interpreters. We omit some Paris notes that merely give variants or oppose Catholic faith, drawing especially on Pamelius. We use fuller special commentaries [Col. 0070D]: Thomas on *Baptism* and *Repentance*; much of Havercamp on the *Apology*, removing undigested professorial and heretical matter; Salmasius on *Pallium*; Muratori and Panciroli on the enlarged *Prayer*. Against Semler’s impious dispute over Irenaeus, parallel passages show clearly that Irenaeus’s Greek works and old Latin translation preceded Tertullian [Col. 0071A].

Editions have imposed many orders: La Cerda and Oberthür chronology; Rhenanus and Rigault manuscript lists; Pamelius theological synthesis; Semler a mixture suggesting all works Catholic and Montanist alike are one Montanist mass [Col. 0071B]. We preserve familiar order where possible but adopt a partly chronological, partly systematic sequence: volume I Catholic, volume II Montanist; each divided into apologetic/polemical and moral/dogmatic works. Catholic apologetics are *Apology, To the Nations, Testimony, Martyrs, Spectacles, Idolatry*, with the Montanist *To Scapula* appended for affinity [Col. 0071C]. Catholic dogmatics are *Prayer, Baptism, Repentance, To His Wife* II, and *Adornment of Women* II; comparison with Lumper shows the chronology is preserved [Col. 0072A].

Montanist polemics are *Soldier’s Crown, Flight, Against the Psychics, Scorpiace, Praxeas, Hermogenes, Marcion, Valentinians, Jews, Soul against Philosophers, Flesh of Christ,* and *Resurrection*. Moral works are *Veiling, Exhortation, Monogamy, Fasts* [Col. 0072B], *Modesty,* and *Pallium*. The disputed *Prescriptions* stands between Catholic and Montanist works at the front of volume II, leading the twelve polemical books.

We conclude by imploring God, the Virgin Mother of God, mother and delight of angels and men, and the triumphant Church’s crown—festively celebrated on the day we finished—to make this long work acceptable to God, pleasing to the Church, useful to many, and eternally profitable to us [Col. 0072C].

Written at Paris, 1 November 1843.

## THE LIFE OF Q. SEPTIMIUS FLORENS TERTULLIAN, PRESBYTER OF CARTHAGE, BY JAMES PAMELIUS, CONTAINING THE HISTORY OF TWENTY-FOUR YEARS OF HIS AGE

[Col. 0041A] **§ I. A preliminary twofold controversy is set forth.—1.** As, God favoring, we write Tertullian’s life—he who, remarkably, flourished as an African under the African pope St Victor and the African emperor bearing his name, Septimius Severus—two very difficult controversies arise. The first concerns the consul Tertullus, our Tertullian, Tertylian the jurist, and St Tertullinus the martyr, four men whom some through error tried to make one. The second concerns the author’s period and age when, as a new recruit of Christ, he was initiated into the mysteries of faith.
