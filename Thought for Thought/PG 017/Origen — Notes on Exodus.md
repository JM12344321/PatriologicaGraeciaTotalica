## Exodus 15:25

**Verse 25.** “There God gave him statutes and judgments, and there he put him to the test.” God tests people for their benefit; evil itself cannot tempt him. His purpose in testing them is that, if they obey him, they may receive what is good and be delivered from evil.

## Exodus 20:4

**Verse 4.** “You shall not make an idol for yourself.” An idol is not the same thing as a likeness. A likeness is an artistic or painted representation of something that exists—a fish, a four-footed animal, or a wild beast. An idol, by contrast, is something the imagination fashions without any real original, such as a creature made by combining a human being and a horse.

**Verse 4.** “You shall not bow down to or serve anything in heaven above, on the earth below, or in the waters beneath the earth.” Bowing down and serving are different. Someone who devotes his whole soul to these things both bows down to them and worships them. Someone who merely keeps up appearances and conforms to custom does not give them such service, although he still bows down. In Numbers you will find people of this sort: they accommodate women and bow down to idols.

## Exodus 28:30

**Verse 30.** The high priest must carry protective vigilance before and behind him like a shield, lest a hostile design of the spirits of evil wound his rational faculty, throw the proven soundness of his judgments into confusion, and, by mingling evil with them, cause Manifestation and Truth to depart.

[Translation note: “Manifestation and Truth” renders the Greek names used in this cultic context for the objects placed in the breastpiece of judgment.]

## Exodus 31:1

**Verse 1.** “The Lord spoke to Moses.” The all-wise God kept Moses for the full forty days while giving him the law, even though he could have given it in three. He allowed the delay so that Moses’ prolonged absence would expose the impiety hidden deep in the Jews’ hearts.
