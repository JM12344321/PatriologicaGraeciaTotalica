## Genesis 7:4

**Verse 4.** “I will bring rain upon the earth for forty days and forty nights. I will wipe from the face of the earth every living thing I have made, from human beings to livestock.” Why did God destroy so many people in the flood? He intended to wipe out Cain’s race, but the descendants of the godly had intermarried with them and so came to share their punishment.

God wished to give human life a new beginning, so he preserved Noah, his sons, and their wives. Noah was pious and righteous. He had grown from the godliness of his family and rejected any alliance with lawlessness. God did not act, as some claim, because he flew into a rage or changed his mind. Anger and regret of that sort are human passions, and the divine nature is free from passion.

Regret properly belongs to people who discover the real nature of something only after experiencing it. Once experience teaches them that their decision was wrong, they change their minds. God, however, sees what will happen many generations from now as though it had already happened. He governs everything with complete foresight and foreknowledge. How, then, could he regret a decision when he orders everything in keeping with his own foreknowledge?

When Scripture speaks of God’s regret, it therefore means that he changes the way he administers events. He says, “I regret that I made Saul king,” meaning, “I have decided to remove this man and appoint another.” Likewise, “I considered that I had made humanity” means, “I decided to destroy the human race.” Yet in his love for humanity God preserved Noah as the seed from which humanity could grow again.

God had created the various kinds of animals to serve human needs, so they too were destroyed with humanity. The exceptions were the animals kept safely with Noah in the ark. The Lord God commanded Noah to preserve two of every kind regarded as unclean and seven of every clean kind. God was going to allow people to eat meat, and those reared in godliness would also offer him sacrifices. He therefore ordered more clean animals to be kept: three pairs to propagate their kind, and one additional animal for sacrifice.

As soon as the punishment had ended, Noah offered God a sacrifice of thanksgiving “from every kind of clean livestock and every kind of clean bird.” This makes the reason for the seven animals clear. Noah could offer one clean animal from each kind without destroying any breeding pair.

[Translation note: The source has an anomalous word before “of the punishment.” The translation follows the contextually probable sense “cessation” or “end.”]

## Genesis 19:25–26

**Verse 25.** “He overthrew those cities, the whole surrounding region, and everyone who lived in the cities.”

**Verse 26.** “His wife looked back and became a pillar of salt.” The Lord destroys not only ungodly people but also their Sodomite and harmful foodstuffs. This includes the vine described in Scripture: “Their vine comes from the vine of Sodom, and their branch from Gomorrah.”

The moment Lot reached safety in the town, fire swept over the other cities, foreshadowing the consummation to come. His wife did not become just any pillar, but a pillar of salt, in connection with the saying, “You are the salt of the earth.” The question “What if the salt loses its taste?” may also be applied to this pillar. Lot did not immediately climb the mountain as he had been told. He stopped first at Zoar because he lacked confidence that he could make the ascent.

## Genesis 23:15

**Verse 15.** “Land worth four hundred didrachmas.” Notice that four hundred is the price of a tomb: both four and one hundred have an afflictive significance. Search the Scriptures and you will find similar examples.

## Genesis 37:10

**Verse 10.** “Will your mother and I and your brothers really come and bow before you to the ground?” Joseph’s mother had clearly died before he had these dreams. Perhaps, then, this is a prophecy that Mary and Joseph would come to bow before Christ.

## Genesis 43:22

**Verse 22.** “He told them, ‘May God be gracious to you. Do not be afraid.’” It appears that this man’s association with Joseph had led him toward reverence for God.

## Genesis 46:34

**Verse 34.** “We are livestock breeders.” Every shepherd is abhorrent to the Egyptians, though not to the Hebrews. If he has been appointed as our shepherd, Egypt finds him abhorrent; but if he is merely a hired worker, perhaps Egypt will even regard him as a friend.
